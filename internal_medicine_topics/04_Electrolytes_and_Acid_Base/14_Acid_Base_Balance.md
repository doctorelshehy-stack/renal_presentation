## Overview of Acid-Base Disturbances

| | metabolic acidosis | metabolic alkalosis | respiratory acidosis | respiratory alkalosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary event** | Primary reduction of plasma HCO3-, leading to decrease in pH stimulating ventilation to decrease CO2 (low pH, CO2 and HCO3-) | Primary increase of plasma HCO3-, leading to increase in pH inhibiting ventilation to increase CO2 (high pH, CO2 and HCO3-) | Primary retention of CO2, leading to decrease in pH enhancing renal retention of HCO3 (low pH, high CO2 and HCO3). | Primary loss of CO2, leading to increase in pH enhancing renal execretion of HCO3 (high pH, low CO2 and HCO3-). |

**Table: Body compensation to Acid-Base imbalance**

| Acid-Base Disturbances | Metabolic | | Respiratory | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Acidosis | Alkalosis | Acidosis | Alkalosis |
| **PH** | ↓ | ↑ | ↓ | ↑ |
| **CO2** | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| **HCO3-** | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |

## Metabolic Acidosis

#### Definition: Decrease in blood pH due to a decrease in serum HCO3- concentrations.

#### The Anion Gap:
Plasma, is electro-neutral i.e. total anions match total cations.
- major measured plasma cation is Na+ & major measured anions are HCO3- and Cl-
- There are some unmeasured plasma cations (e.g. K+, Mg+2 and Ca+2) as well as unmeasured anions (e.g. Sulfate, Phosphate and some organic anions). The unmeasured anions are slightly more than the unmeasured cations; this difference is termed "the Anion Gap (AG)".
- Based on the fact that the total cations equal the total anions; AG can be calculated as the difference between the major cation (Na+) and the sum of the major anions (HCO3- and Cl-). i.e. AG = Na+ - (HCO3- + Cl-)
- An increase of AG can result from either;
  - A decrease in the unmeasured cations (e.g. hypokalemia) or
  - An increase in the unmeasured anions (e.g. hyperphosphatemia).
- In certain forms of metabolic acidosis, acidic substances (anions) accumulate and increase the AG. By recognizing the increase in AG, this can help to differentiate the cause of acidosis. Thus, metabolic acidosis can be either:

1. **High AG**, in which the reduction in plasma HCO3- equals the increase in AG.
2. **Normal (Non) AG**, in which the reduction in plasma HCO3- is compensated by an increase in plasma Cl- to maintain electro-neutrality (Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis).

---

#### Causes and Classifications:

**Causes of normal AG (hyperchloremic) metabolic acidosis:**
1- GI loss of HCO3- (e.g. diarrhea) & Enteric fistula).
2- Renal loss of HCO3- (Proximal Type II- RTA).
3- Failure of renal H+ secretion (Distal Type I – RTA).
4- Acid infusion (e.g. Ammonium chloride, hyperalimentation).

**Causes of high AG metabolic acidosis:**
1- Ketoacidosis (Beta-hydroxyburate and acetoacetate).
2- Renal failure (phosphate, sulfate, urate).
3- Lactic acidosis (L-lactate, D-lactate).
4- Massive rhabdomyolysis (H+ and organic anions from the damaged muscles).
5- Exogenous (Salicylate, Methanol, ethylene glycol, metformin).

---

#### Clinical Features:

| Symptoms | Signs |
|---|---|
| - Variable degrees of dyspnea (due to hyperventilation). | - The most prominent sign is Kussmaul Respiration (rapid deep breathing). |
| - Other symptoms are non-specific and symptoms of the cause if present e.g.; | - Other signs are non-specific if present e.g.; |
| 1- Symptoms of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or of CKD. | 1- Hypotension, cardiac arrhythmia. |
| 2- History of alcoholism, drug intake (e.g. salicylates, acetazolamide, metformin), or of renal stone (e.g. in RTA). | 2- Disturbed consciousness and coma. |
| | 3- Signs of the cause. e.g. DKA, CKD, or alcoholism. |

---

#### Investigations:

**General investigations:** → to diagnose metabolic acidosis and calculate AG e.g.;
- ABG (arterial blood gases): pH, HCO3- , CO2
- Serum electrolytes: Na+, K+ and Cl-

**Specific investigations:** → to diagnose the etiology e.g.;
- Kidney functions, blood glucose level, urinary ketones, serum lactate and toxicological screening.

---

#### Treatment:

- Treatment is generally directed to the underlying condition or disease.
- Treatment of acidosis using alkali therapy (HCO3-) is usually indicated to raise plasma pH above 7.20 in the following circumstances:
  1- Low serum HCO3- and PCO2 close to the lower limit of compensation (approximately 15 mmHg). In this situation, any further drop in HCO3- will not matched by a corresponding fall in PCO2 leading to marked drop in pH.
  2- Well-compensated metabolic acidosis with impending respiratory failure due to respiratory muscle fatigue. In this situation, PCO2 starts to rise leading to a dramatic drop in pH.
- Route of administration of bicarbonate:
  - Intravenous sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3): correct acute metabolic acidosis.
  - HCO3- deficit can be calculated as follow: (Desired HCO3- - Measured HCO3-) × 0.5 × body weight.
- Monitoring:
  - Serum HCO3- and pH should be assessed frequently.
  - Monitoring of serum Na+, volume status and PCO2 during treatment.
  - Oral sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3): used to correct chronic metabolic acidosis (e.g. RTA) and uncommonly used in acute situations when the I.V. route is not suitable.

## Metabolic Alkalosis

#### Definition: An increase in blood pH due to increase in serum HCO3- concentrations.

#### Causes and classification:
Causes of metabolic alkalosis can be classified into;
1. Chloride-responsive alkalosis (urine chloride < 20 mEq/L),
2. Chloride-resistant alkalosis (urine chloride >20 mEq/L), and
3. Other causes, including alkali-loading alkalosis.

**A- Causes of chloride-responsive alkalosis (urine chloride < 20 mEq/L):**
1. Loss of gastric secretions; Vomiting, nasogastric suction.
2. Loss of colonic secretions; Congenital chloridorrhea, villous adenoma.
3. Volume depletion.
4. Thiazides and loop diuretics (after discontinuation).
5. Cystic fibrosis.

**B- Causes of chloride-resistant alkalosis (urine chloride >20 mEq/L):**
- **Chloride-resistant alkalosis with hypertension:**
  1. Hyperaldosteronism (primary, adrenal adenoma, hyperplasia, carcinoma, glucocorticoid-remediable hyperaldosteronism).
  2. Cushing syndrome.
  3. Exogenous steroids.
  4. Liddle's syndrome.
  5. Reno-vascular hypertension.

- **Chloride-resistant alkalosis without hypertension:**
  1. Bartter's syndrome.
  2. Gitelman syndrome.
  3. Severe potassium depletion.
  4. Current use of thiazides and loop diuretics.
  5. Hypomagnesaemia: The mechanism probably involves hypokalemia, which is usually caused by or associated with magnesium depletion.

**C- Other causes of metabolic alkalosis;**
1. Exogenous alkali administration: sodium bicarbonate or massive blood transfusion (high citrate content) in the presence of renal insufficiency
2. Recovery of metabolic acidosis (due to the metabolism of lactic acid or ketoacids).
3. Milk-alkali syndrome (comprises hypercalcemia, renal insufficiency, and metabolic alkalosis).
4. Hypercalcemia causes volume depletion and enhanced bicarbonate reabsorption.
5. Hypoalbuminemia: may be related to loss of negative charges of albumin.

---

#### Clinical Features:

**History;**
- Helpful in establishing the etiology. e.g. GI fluid loss, surgery, drug history, or renal insufficiency.
- Symptoms: are not specific
- Hypoventilation because of inhibition of the respiratory centers.
- Symptoms of hypokalemia e.g. weakness, myalgia, polyuria, and cardiac arrhythmias.
- Symptoms of hypocalcemia (e.g. perioral tingling, muscle cramps and carbopedal spasms) due to decreased ionized calcium.

**Signs:** are also not specific but may help to establish the cause
- Volume depletion usually accompanies chloride-responsive alkalosis, while volume expansion accompanies chloride-resistant alkalosis.
- There may be signs of hypocalcaemia (e.g. tetany, Chvostek sign, and Trousseau sign), change in mental status, or seizures.

---

#### Investigations:

**General investigations:** → for the diagnosis of metabolic alkalosis.
- ABG (arterial blood gases): pH, HCO3- , CO2
- Serum electrolytes: Na+, K+, Cl-, Ca+2 and Mg+2
- Urinary Cl-

**Specific investigations:** → for determination of the etiology e.g.
- Plasma aldosterone and cortisol levels, plasma renin activity and reno-vascular imaging.

---

#### Treatment; depends on the underlying etiology and on the patient's volume status.

**Treatment of chloride-responsive metabolic alkalosis:**
- **In patients with volume depletion:**
  - Infusion of isotonic (0.9% NaCl) sodium chloride to correct alkalosis and restore volume.
  - Potassium chloride (I.V. or oral) to correct the hypokalemia, if present.
- **In patients with volume overload (edematous states):**
  - Potassium chloride (I.V. or oral) to correct the alkalosis and avoid volume overload.
  - Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors or potassium-sparing diuretics can be used to decrease volume and correct the alkalosis.

**Treatment of chloride-resistant metabolic alkalosis:** Management is based on the specific cause.

# Hyponatremia

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definition: A decrease in serum sodium concentration to a level below 135 mmol/L.

- **Dilutional hyponatremia:** It is the most common form of the disorder and is caused by water retention rather than sodium depletion.
- **Pseudohyponatremia:** a spuriously low plasma sodium concentration; the measured sodium concentration is low, but the true physiological plasma sodium concentration is normal. pseudohyponatremia can occur in any clinical situation in which the serum lipids or protein concentration is markedly increased to maintain plasma osmolality.

---

#### Pathological effects:

- Hyponatremia leads shift of water from the ECF to the ICF compartments.
- Increased ICF volume leads to cell swelling.
- In the brain this leads to brain oedema and neurologic manifestations.

---

#### Clinical Manifestations:

1- Manifestations of the cause
2- Manifestations of hyponatremia (neurologic symptoms): changes in mental status including altered personality, lethargy, and confusion. As the serum sodium falls, stupor, neuromuscular hyperexcitability, hyperreflexia, seizures, coma, and death can result.
The severity of neurologic symptoms correlates well with the severity and the rate of development of hyponatremia.

---

#### Classification and Clinical types:

- Hyponatremia states can be classified into three types according to plasma osmolality (tonicity); Hypertonic, Isotonic and Hypotonic.
- The first two types, Hypertonic and Isotonic, can be considered as pseudohyponatremia while the last type, Hypotonic, is considered true hyponatremia.
- Hypotonic Hyponatremia is classified into three subtypes according to the volume status of the patient.

```
                    Hyponatremia
                         |
            +------------+------------+
            |            |            |
        Hypotonic    Isotonic    Hypertonic
            |
    +-------+-------+
    |       |       |
Hypervolemic Euvolemic Hypovolemic
```

---

#### Diagnostic Algorithm:

**Types and causes of hyponatremia - Flowchart:**

Hyponatremia → Plasma osmolality
- **Low** → EVS
  - **Increased** → Consider heart failure, cirrhosis or nephrosis
  - **Normal** → Urine osmolality
    - **<100 mOsm/kg H2O** → Consider polydipsia
    - **>100 mOsm/kg H2O** → Consider SIADH, hypothyroidism, or hypocortisolism
  - **Decreased** → Consider gastrointestinal fluid loss, Addison disease, salt-losing nephritis or cerebral salt wasting
- **Normal or high** → Consider hyperglycemia or pseudohyponatremia

---

#### Treatment:

- Primary goal is to control symptoms, then to correct Na+ level.
- Determine whether hyponatremia is acute or chronic.
- Be careful of the rate of correction according to the rate of development (acute or chronic) to avoid osmotic demyenation $ i.e. central pontine myelinoysis.
- The fluid of choice for correction depends on the volume status of the patient.

**Medications:**
- Isotonic 0.9% NaCl (hypo and euvolemic states).
- Hypertonic 3% NaCl + Loop diuretics (hyper and euvolemic states).
- Vasopressin receptor antagonists: Conivaptan, Tolvaptan.

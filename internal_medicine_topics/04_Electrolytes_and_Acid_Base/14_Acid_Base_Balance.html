Overview — Primary events

Primary Events of the Four Disturbances

DisturbancePrimary event
Metabolic acidosisPrimary reduction of plasma HCO3- → decrease in pH → stimulates ventilation to decrease CO2 (low pH, CO2 and HCO3-)
Metabolic alkalosisPrimary increase of plasma HCO3- → increase in pH → inhibits ventilation to increase CO2 (high pH, CO2 and HCO3-)
Respiratory acidosisPrimary retention of CO2 → decrease in pH → enhances renal retention of HCO3 (low pH, high CO2 and HCO3)
Respiratory alkalosisPrimary loss of CO2 → increase in pH → enhances renal excretion of HCO3 (high pH, low CO2 and HCO3-)

Metabolic Acidosis

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Acidosis

Definition

Definition

Decrease in blood pH due to a decrease in serum HCO3- concentrations.

ℹ
Classification

Metabolic acidosis can be either high anion gap (AG) or normal (non-) AG — recognizing the AG helps differentiate the cause.

High AG acidosis Normal (non-) AG — hyperchloremic acidosis
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Acidosis — The Anion Gap

The Anion Gap (AG)

Formula

AG = Na+ - (HCO3- + Cl-)

Electro-neutrality of plasma

  • Major measured cation: Na+; major measured anions: HCO3- and Cl-.
  • Unmeasured plasma cations: e.g. K+, Mg+2 and Ca+2.
  • Unmeasured anions: e.g. sulfate, phosphate and some organic anions.

Origin and meaning of the AG

  • Total cations = total anions; the unmeasured anions are slightly more than the unmeasured cations — this difference is the anion gap.
  • Increased AG results from a decrease in unmeasured cations (e.g. hypokalemia) or an increase in unmeasured anions (e.g. hyperphosphatemia).
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Acidosis — Types

High AG vs Normal AG Acidosis

High AG metabolic acidosis

  • Acidic substances (anions) accumulate and increase the AG.
  • The reduction in plasma HCO3- equals the increase in AG.
  • Recognizing the increase in AG helps to differentiate the cause of acidosis.

Normal (non-) AG metabolic acidosis

  • The reduction in plasma HCO3- is compensated by an increase in plasma Cl- to maintain electro-neutrality.
  • Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis.

Metabolic Acidosis — Clinical Features

Clinical Features

Symptoms

  • Variable degrees of dyspnea (due to hyperventilation).
  • Other symptoms are non-specific, plus symptoms of the cause if present, e.g.:
    • Symptoms of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or of CKD.
    • History of alcoholism, drug intake (e.g. salicylates, acetazolamide, metformin), or of renal stone (e.g. in RTA).

Signs

  • The most prominent sign is Kussmaul respiration (rapid deep breathing).
  • Other signs are non-specific if present, e.g.:
    • Hypotension, cardiac arrhythmia.
    • Disturbed consciousness and coma.
    • Signs of the cause: DKA, CKD, or alcoholism.

Metabolic Acidosis — Investigations

Investigations

General — to diagnose metabolic acidosis and calculate AG

  • ABG (arterial blood gases): pH, HCO3-, CO2.
  • Serum electrolytes: Na+, K+ and Cl-.

Specific — to diagnose the etiology

  • Kidney functions.
  • Blood glucose level.
  • Urinary ketones.
  • Serum lactate.
  • Toxicological screening.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Acidosis — Treatment

Treatment Principles

ℹ
General rule

Treatment is generally directed to the underlying condition or disease.

Alkali therapy (HCO3-) is indicated to raise plasma pH above 7.20 in:

  1. Low serum HCO3- and PCO2 close to the lower limit of compensation (approximately 15 mmHg): any further drop in HCO3- will not be matched by a corresponding fall in PCO2 → marked drop in pH.
  2. Well-compensated metabolic acidosis with impending respiratory failure (respiratory muscle fatigue): PCO2 starts to rise → dramatic drop in pH.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Acidosis — Treatment

Bicarbonate Therapy: Routes and Deficit

HCO3- deficit calculation

(Desired HCO3- - Measured HCO3-) × 0.5 × body weight

Intravenous sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3)

  • Corrects acute metabolic acidosis.

Oral sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3)

  • Corrects chronic metabolic acidosis (e.g. RTA).
  • Uncommonly used in acute situations when the I.V. route is not suitable.
⚠
Monitoring

Assess serum HCO3- and pH frequently; monitor serum Na+, volume status and PCO2 during treatment.

Metabolic Alkalosis

Metabolic Alkalosis

Definition and Classification

Definition

An increase in blood pH due to increase in serum HCO3- concentrations.

Chloride-responsive — urine Cl- < 20 mEq/L Chloride-resistant — urine Cl- > 20 mEq/L Other causes — alkali-loading alkalosis
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Alkalosis — Causes

Chloride-Responsive Causes (urine Cl- < 20 mEq/L)

  • Loss of gastric secretions: vomiting, nasogastric suction.
  • Loss of colonic secretions: congenital chloridorrhea, villous adenoma.
  • Volume depletion.
  • Thiazides and loop diuretics (after discontinuation).
  • Cystic fibrosis.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Alkalosis — Causes

Chloride-Resistant Causes (urine Cl- > 20 mEq/L)

With hypertension

  • Hyperaldosteronism (primary, adrenal adenoma, hyperplasia, carcinoma, glucocorticoid-remediable hyperaldosteronism).
  • Cushing syndrome.
  • Exogenous steroids.
  • Liddle's syndrome.
  • Renovascular hypertension.

Without hypertension

  • Bartter's syndrome.
  • Gitelman syndrome.
  • Severe potassium depletion.
  • Current use of thiazides and loop diuretics.
  • Hypomagnesemia — mechanism probably involves hypokalemia, usually caused by or associated with magnesium depletion.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Alkalosis — Causes

Other Causes of Metabolic Alkalosis

  • Exogenous alkali administration: sodium bicarbonate or massive blood transfusion (high citrate content) in the presence of renal insufficiency.
  • Recovery of metabolic acidosis (due to the metabolism of lactic acid or ketoacids).
  • Milk-alkali syndrome — comprises hypercalcemia, renal insufficiency, and metabolic alkalosis.
  • Hypercalcemia — causes volume depletion and enhanced bicarbonate reabsorption.
  • Hypoalbuminemia — may be related to loss of negative charges of albumin.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Alkalosis — Clinical Features

Clinical Features

History

  • Helpful in establishing the etiology: GI fluid loss, surgery, drug history, or renal insufficiency.
  • Symptoms are not specific.
  • Hypoventilation because of inhibition of the respiratory centers.
  • Symptoms of hypokalemia: weakness, myalgia, polyuria, cardiac arrhythmias.
  • Symptoms of hypocalcemia (e.g. perioral tingling, muscle cramps, carpopedal spasms) due to decreased ionized calcium.

Signs

  • Also not specific, but may help to establish the cause.
  • Volume depletion usually accompanies chloride-responsive alkalosis; volume expansion accompanies chloride-resistant alkalosis.
  • Signs of hypocalcemia: tetany, Chvostek sign, and Trousseau sign.
  • Change in mental status, or seizures.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Alkalosis — Investigations

Investigations

General — for the diagnosis of metabolic alkalosis

  • ABG (arterial blood gases): pH, HCO3-, CO2.
  • Serum electrolytes: Na+, K+, Cl-, Ca+2 and Mg+2.
  • Urinary Cl-.

Specific — for determination of the etiology

  • Plasma aldosterone and cortisol levels.
  • Plasma renin activity.
  • Renovascular imaging.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Alkalosis — Treatment

Treatment of Chloride-Responsive Alkalosis

Patients with volume depletion

  • Infusion of isotonic (0.9% NaCl) sodium chloride to correct alkalosis and restore volume.
  • Potassium chloride (I.V. or oral) to correct the hypokalemia, if present.

Patients with volume overload (edematous states)

  • Potassium chloride (I.V. or oral) to correct the alkalosis and avoid volume overload.
  • Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors or potassium-sparing diuretics to decrease volume and correct the alkalosis.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Metabolic Alkalosis — Treatment

Treatment of Chloride-Resistant Alkalosis

ℹ
Principle

Treatment depends on the underlying etiology and on the patient's volume status.

✓
Chloride-resistant

Management is based on the specific cause (e.g. hyperaldosteronism, Cushing syndrome, Bartter's or Gitelman syndrome, potassium or magnesium depletion).

Closing

Key Takeaways

✓
Identify the disturbance

From the pattern of pH, CO2 and HCO3- (metabolic vs respiratory, acidosis vs alkalosis) and the compensation table.

✓
Use the anion gap

AG = Na+ - (HCO3- + Cl-); classify metabolic acidosis into high AG vs normal (hyperchloremic) AG to narrow the cause.

✓
Alkali therapy is selective

Give HCO3- to raise pH above 7.20 only when compensation limits are reached; deficit = (Desired - Measured HCO3-) × 0.5 × body weight.

✓
Classify metabolic alkalosis by urine chloride

Chloride-responsive (< 20 mEq/L) → saline and KCl; chloride-resistant (> 20 mEq/L) → treat the specific cause.

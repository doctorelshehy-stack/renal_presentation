# Hyperkalemia

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definition: An increase in S. potassium concentration to a level above 5.5 mmol/L.

---

#### Causes:

**I) Increased potassium release from the cells:**
1- Pseudo-hyperkalemia; increase blood cell destruction in vitro (e.g. hemolysis) leading to K+ release from the damaged cells. when blood samples are left for a long time at low temperature, especially in patients with polycythemia or leukocytosis.
2- Metabolic acidosis
3- Insulin deficiency, hyperglycemia, and hyperosmolality
4- Increased tissue catabolism: e.g. trauma, cytotoxic and radiation therapy for malignancy (tumor lysis syndrome).
5- β adrenergic blockade
6- Exercise
7- Digitalis overdose.

**II) Reduced urinary potassium excretion:**
1- Renal failure; due to decreased K+ excretion
2- Hypoaldosteronism (primary and secondary).
3- Circulatory volume depletion
4- Type (IV) RTA
5- Drugs:
   - ACEIs, ARBs, NSAIDs, Cyclosporin; cause impaired angiotensin II induced aldosterone secretion → hypoaldosteronism → K+ retention.
   - Potassium sparing diuretics (spironolactone), trimethoprim antibiotic, and heparin → aldosterone resistance → K+ retention.

---

#### Manifestations:

Elevation of the extracellular potassium concentrations results in a decrease in cell membrane excitability (especially nerve, muscle and cardiac cells) which is manifested by;

- Muscle weakness or paralysis, irritability, anxiety, paraesthesia, and/or impaired cardiac conduction, irregular pulse and cardiac standstill (if hyperkalemia is sudden or severe).

---

#### Treatment:

Urgent therapy is indicated when serious manifestations of hyperkalemia are present such as; muscle weakness or paralysis, cardiac conduction abnormalities, cardiac arrhythmias, and/or serum K+ concentration ≥ 7 mmol/L.

**Treatment strategies:**

**1- Stabilize the myocardial cell membrane** by IV calcium gluconate, 10 mg in 10 ml over 10 minutes, as calcium antagonizes the cellular effects of hyperkalemia (a physiologic antidote).

**2- Drive extracellular potassium into the cells (by increasing Na+/K+ -ATPase pump activity).**

| Agent | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| β2 agonists (albuterol, salbutamol) | 10 to 20 mg inhaled over 10 minutes | Peak effect occurs in 90 minutes. Contraindicated in patients with ischemic heart diseases |
| Insulin and glucose | One ampule dextrose 50% concentration with 5-10 units of regular insulin intravenous | Peak effect is seen within 60 min and last for several hours. Side effect is hypoglycemia. |
| Sodium bicarbonate (Na HCO3) | | The effect is rapid within few minutes but not long lasting. |

**3- Removal of potassium from the body:**

| Method | Mechanism/Notes |
|---|---|
| 1- Loop diuretics | Leads to K+ loss in the urine by inhibiting Na/Kcl transporter in loop of Henle. |
| 2- Potassium exchanges resins (sodium polystyrene sulfate) | Given orally or per rectum. Exchanges Na+ for K+ and bind it in the gut (primary in large intestine) → decreasing total body K+. Potassium is removed from the body in the stool, 8-12 hours after administration. Side effects; intestinal necrosis/ gangrene. |
| 3- Hemodialysis | In refractory and emergency cases (i.e. high K+ level >7 mmol/L or ECG changes). |

**4- Monitoring:**
- Continuous cardiac monitoring and serial ECGs
- Measurement of serum potassium at 1-2 hours after the initiation of therapy.

---

#### ECG changes:

tall peaked T waves, prolonged PR intervals, ST segments depression, loss of P waves, widen QRS, VT and cardiac standstill

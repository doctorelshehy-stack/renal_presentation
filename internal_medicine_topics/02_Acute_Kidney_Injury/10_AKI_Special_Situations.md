## 1. Contrast-Induced Nephropathy (CIN)

**Topics:**
- Definition
- Risk factors
- Pathogenesis
- Diagnosis
- Prevention and treatment
- Prognosis

#### Definition:

CIN is acute kidney injury characterized by rise of serum creatinine of ≥ 0.5 mg/dl or 25% above the base line within 48 hours after contrast administration.

#### Risk factors:

- **Patient-related factors:**
  - Pre-existing CKD; it is the most potent risk factor. Nearly 60% of patients developing CIN have CKD, and the incidence of CIN parallels the severity of preexisting renal impairment.
  - Diabetes mellitus
  - Congestive heart failure
  - Hypotension
  - Volume depletion
  - Old age
- **Contrast-related factors:**
  - Large volume (dose) of parental contrast material.
  - The type of contrast material especially its osmolarity. The incidence of CIN is high with the high osmolar contrast materials.

#### Pathogenesis: Several mechanisms have been proposed.

- **Renal ischemia;** Contrast media induce renal ischemia by two mechanisms;
  a. Production of VC compounds as endothelin and adenosine.
  b. Increased oxygen utilization in the renal tubules.
- **Hyperosmolarity;** Intra-tubular hyperosmolarity cause;
  a. Increase intra-tubular hydrostatic pressure which decreases glomerular filtration,
  b. Increase tubular cell apoptosis.
- **Generation of oxygen free radicals.**
- **Direct cellular toxicity.**

#### Diagnosis:

- **Symptoms and signs:**
  - In the vast majority of patients, there are no symptoms or signs.
  - In a smaller subset of patients, there is oliguria with or without volume overload.
  - Rarely a patient with CIN may present with symptoms and signs of uremia.
- **Laboratory finding Assessment of serum creatinine level:**
  - In the majority of patients, the serum creatinine level begins to rise 24- 48 hours after the contrast media exposure, peaks within 3- 5 days and returns to the base line level within 7- 10 days.
  - In more severe cases, the serum creatinine does not peak until 5- 10 days and may be accompanied by oliguria and requirement for dialysis.
- **Imaging:** To exclude other causes of AKI.

#### Management:

- **Prevention:**
  1. Use non-iodinated contrast media.
  2. Use low osmolar contrast media.
  3. Minimize contrast volume (dose). A formula based on body weight and baseline S. creatinine is used to limit the volume of contrast media.
     Volume of contrast = 5 ml of contrast/kg of BW (maximum 300 ml) / S. creatinine mg/dl.
  4. Space between contrast administrations.
  5. Avoid nephrotoxic drugs before and after the procedure.
  6. Adequate hydration before, during, and after the procedure.
  7. Use of N-acetyl cysteine; it is unclear whether N-acetyl cysteine prevents CIN as studies are conflicting.
- **Treatment:** no specific therapy for CIN once it occurs. The best strategy is one of prevention. However, once a patient develops CIN the following should be taken:
  1. Manage fluid and electrolytes.
  2. Adjust medications that are eliminated by the kidney.
  3. Regular monitoring of; electrolytes, S. creatinine and BUN.
  4. Dialysis as needed.

#### Prognosis:

- In most patients, complete recovery of renal function is usually occur.
- A small minority of patients may go on CKD.

## 2. Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS)

**Topics:**
- Definition
- Patients at risk
- Clinical features
- Diagnosis and DD
- Management
- Prognosis

#### Definition:

It is an acute critical illness characterized by severe hyperuricemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia, and AKI seen in patients with rapidly growing cancers (especially Burkett's lymphomas) after administration of chemotherapeutic agents.

The acute electrolyte disorders are due to massive sudden death of tumor cells → release of intra-cellular electrolytes (uric acid, phosphorus, and potassium). Hypocalcemia is secondary to hyperphosphatemia to maintain calcium-phosphorus balance.

#### Patients at risk:

1. Hematological and lymphoproliferative malignancy with marked elevation of serum LDH.
2. Volume depletion.
3. Acidic urinary pH
4. Patients with CKD; This because renal clearance is the primary mechanism of excretion of UA and phosphates.

#### Clinical features:

Varying degrees of AKI due to intra-tubular precipitation of UA (acute UA nephropathy) and/or acute nephrocalcinosis secondary to marked hyper-phosphatemia. AKI is most marked during the induction of chemotherapy.

#### Diagnosis:

- Any patient with a known or suspected malignancy, especially Burkett's lymphoma, who present with; AKI, hyperuricemia, and elevated serum LDH should be examined for TLS.
- The presence of concomitant volume depletion, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia and hypocalcemia strongly support the clinical diagnosis.

#### Differential diagnosis: from other causes of AKI in cancer patients including:

1. UT obstruction.
2. Severe volume depletion.
3. Parenchymal renal diseases; e.g.
   - GN secondary to cryoglobimemia or tumor-related Ag-Ab complexes.
   - Vasculitis.
   - Hypercalcemic nephropathy.
   - Tumor infiltrating the kidney parenchyma.
   - Myeloma kidney (cast nephropathy).
   - Nephrotoxic Drug: Methotrexate, cisplatinum, mitomycin C, INF-Alfa, antibiotics.

#### Prevention (prophylactic):

- Early identification of patients at risk.
- Institution of the following measures prior to anti-neoplastic drug therapy;
  1. Correct initial electrolyte and fluid disorders.
  2. Maintain adequate hydration and urine output.
  3. Alkaline urine to pH > 7.0 to enhance UA solubility and prevent precipitation.
  4. Give oral phosphate binding antacids.
  5. Allopurinol (300 mg/ m2) 1-2 days before chemotherapy.
  6. Correct any renal or pre-renal dysfunction, if present.

#### Treatment of established AKI:

1. Administer allopurinol, 600 mg/day.
2. Intravenous isotonic NaHco3 at rate of 200-300 ml/ h to expand volume, wash out the renal medulla, and alkalinize the urine.
3. Dialysis: HD is life saving in the management of TLS and should be considered for every patient. It is generally required during induction.
   The main goal of HD is to;
   - Decrease plasma levels of; UA, phosphorus, and potassium.
   - Restore volume overload, and
   - Control of uremia.
4. Post-chemotherapy measures:
   - Discontinue urine alkalization when UA homeostasis is achieved to avoid Ca3Po4 precipitation.
   - Treat symptomatic hypocalcaemia after correction of hyperphosphatemia.

## 3. Rhabdomyolysis

#### Definition:

Rhabdomyolysis is a disorder resulting from an injury or a metabolic defect in the skeletal muscle cell resulting in lysis of the cell membrane and leakage of its contents (myoglobin, enzymes, phosphorus, potassium) into the blood.

#### Causes:

1. **Direct trauma**
   - Crush syndrome.
   - Electrical shock.
   - Prolonged pressure with coma.
   - Thermal burns & Freezing.
2. **Excessive exercise;** e.g.; athletic injury and convulsive seizures.
3. **Hereditary myopathies;** e.g. myophosphorylase deficiency (McArdles diseases).
4. **Acquired metabolic disorders:**
   - Hyperthyroidism.
   - Hypokalemia (acute).
   - Diabetic ketoacidosis.
   - Hypophosphatemia (acute).
   - Alcoholism.
   - hyponatremia (acute).
5. **Hypoxia and ischemia:**
   - Carbon monoxide poisoning.
   - Vascular occlusion.
   - Atheromatous embolism.
   - Compartment syndrome.
6. **Drugs:**
   - Statins and lipid-lowering drugs.
   - Cocaine.
   - Amphetamine derivatives.
7. **Infectious diseases;**
   - Viral: Coxsacki, HIV, Influenza
   - Bacterial: Clostridia infection, Legionella, Streptococcal infection, Staphylococcal infection, Pneumococcal pneumonia
8. **Toxins;** Snake venom, poisonous mushrooms, fish poisoning (Haff disease).
9. **Miscellaneous;** malignant hyperthermia, neuroleptic malignant syndrome.

#### Pathogenesis:

- Mild and moderate rhabdomyolysis (e.g. after intense exertion, violent repetitive activities or a grand mal seizure) → direct injury of muscle cells and depletion of energy stores → muscle pain and weakness.
- Severe acute rhabdomyolysis (crush injury/pigmented nephropathy), AKI is due to;
  1. **Renal ischemia:** Myoglobin have intense VC effect → renal ischemia.
  2. **Tubular obstruction and injury:** Once the myoglobin molecule is filtered by the glomerulus and enters the PCT fluid;
     a. A portion enters into the PC cells. Inside the cells the molecule release elemental iron and iron compounds forming toxic products that injure the cells.
     b. The remaining unabsorbed pigment passes to distal nephron and interact with Tamm-Hors Fall protein (in acidic urine) forming a gel and obstruct urine flow. Then, the concentration of pigment in the tubule rises, augmenting proximal tubular absorption and toxicity.

#### Diagnosis:

- **Clinical features;** depend on the severity of muscle injury.
  - mild and moderate cases; muscular pain, tenderness, edema, stiffness, weakness and impaired mobility.
  - In severe acute cases (crush injury); AKI.
- **Laboratory;** Estimation of serum Creatine Kinase "CK" enzyme level;
  - Elevated total serum CK levels > 500 IU/L → high suspicion of acute rhabdomyolysis.
  - The CK-MM isoform → is the most sensitive test to confirm the diagnosis.

#### Treatment:

Severe cases should be managed in ICU.

1. Aggressive and urgent volume replacement to maintain organ perfusion, but caution must be taken to avoid volume overload and pulmonary edema.
2. Alkalinization of urine with bicarb infusion to prevent obstructive cast formation
3. Special care to respiratory failure due to diaphragmatic weakness if severe.
4. Monitor for hyperkalemia by ECG and serum potassium level.
5. Dialysis for severe AKI.

#### Prognosis:

who survive acute rhabdomyolysis are at risk of permanent disability (muscle fibrosis)

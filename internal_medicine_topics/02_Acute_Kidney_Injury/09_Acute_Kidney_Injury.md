# Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definition:

- AKI is an abrupt (within 48 hours) and sustained decrease in kidney function, accompanied by changes in blood biochemistry (e.g. a rise in serum creatinine), urine output, or both.
- The term AKI has been proposed to encompass the entire spectrum of the syndrome from a mild transient rise in s. creatinine to overt renal failure requiring RRT, hence the term AKI is more precise than the term acute RF.

---

#### Table: Categorization of AKI according to RIFLE and AKIN stage using serum creatinine and urine output criteria.

| RIFLE | AKIN | S. Cr. changes | Urine output criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Risk | 1 | ≥1.5 - 2 folds ↑ above baseline; or S. Cr. ↑ by ≥0.3 | < 0.5 ml/kg/h for 6 hours |
| Injury | 2 | ≥ 2 folds ↑ | < 0.5 ml/kg/h for 12 hours |
| Failure | 3 | ≥ 3 folds ↑; or S. Cr. ≥ 4.0 with rise of ≥ 0.5 | < 0.3 ml/kg/h for 24 hours or anuria for 12 hs |
| Loss | | Persistent loss of renal function for > 4 weeks | |
| ESRD | | Permanent loss of renal function for > 3 months | |

#### Causes:

| Pre-renal | Renal | Post-renal |
|---|---|---|

**1. Pre-renal causes:**

| Category | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Decreased effective extracellular volume** | Fluid loss; hemorrhage, vomiting, diarrhea, burn & diuretics. Redistribution; liver cell failure, NS, intestinal obstruction, pancreatitis, and malnutrition. |
| **Decreased cardiac output** | Cardiogenic shock, valvulopathy, myocarditis, MI, arrhythmias, CHF, p. emboli, and cardiac tamponade. |
| **Peripheral VD** | hypotension, sepsis, hypoxemia, anaphylactic shock. |
| **Renal vasoconstriction** | PG inhibition, adrenergics, sepsis, hepato-renal syndrome and hypercalcemia. |
| **Efferent arteriole vasodilatation** | ACEIs and ARBs → decreased intraglomerular pressure and renal blood flow → renal ischemia. |

**2. Intra-renal (intrinsic) causes:**

1. **ATN**
   a. Hemodynamic; cardiovascular surgery, sepsis, prolonged pre-renal cause.
   b. Toxic; antimicrobials, iodide contrast agents, immunosuppression or antineoplastic agents, organic solvents, heavy metals, and radiation.
   c. Intratubular deposits; Acute UA nephropathy, myeloma, severe hypercalemia, primary oxalosis, sulfadiazine.
   d. Organic pigments (endogenous nephrotoxins); myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis), hemoglobinuria (intravascular hemolysis).
2. **Acute GN** e.g. RPGN.
3. **Acute interstitial nephritis.**
4. **Renal vascular occlusion.**

**3. Post-renal causes:**
1. Congenital anomalies; ureterocele, bladder diverticulosis, post-urethral valves, neurogenic bladder.
2. Acquired uropathies; BPH, uretrolithiasis, papillary necrosis, ureteral ligation.
3. Malignant disease; prostate, bladder, cervix, colon.
4. Gynecologic non-neoplastic; uterine prolapse, endometriosis.
5. Drugs; sulfonamides, aminocoproic acid.
6. Infections; schistosomiasis, TB, candidiasis, asprillosis, actinomycosis.
7. Others; accidental uretheral catheter occlusion.

**NB:** Nearly all cases of ICU-associated AKI results from more than a single insult.

---

#### Clinical manifestations:

**1. Oliguria/anuria:** Oliguria (<300 ml/day, < 5 ml/kg/day or 0.5 ml/kg/ h) or anuria. Oliguria is significantly associated with the occurrence of AKI. Oliguria alone is the best predictor of AKI. However, about 50% or more of all cases of AKI are non-oliguric. Thus, the maintenance of normal urine output does not provide assurance to the clinician that GFR is normal.

**Causes of anuric AKI:**
1. Complete UT obstruction (90% of cases).
2. Renal vascular occlusion;
   - Renal artery thrombosis.
   - Renal vein thrombosis.
   - Cortical necrosis; sepsis, obstetric accidents, DIC.
   - Renal vasculitis and RPGN.
3. AKI complicating; sepsis, heat stroke and rhabdomyolysis.

**2. Manifestations of loss of kidney function:**
On rare occasions, when frequent monitoring of urine output or serum creatinine concentration is not being done as in ambulatory or hospital ward populations, AKI will initially come by one or more of the clinical manifestations of loss of kidney function; e.g.
- Volume overload e.g. peripheral edema, weight gain, shortness of breath.
- Rarely;
  - GIT symptom e.g. anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
  - Flank/back pain due to edematous kidneys or UT obstruction
  - Altered mental status or seizures
  - Anemia and bleeding
  - Other symptoms

#### Investigations:

**1. Urine examination:**
- Urine volume/ 24 h.
- Urine color; Reddish brown or cola-colored urine → myoglobin or Hb, especially in the setting of a positive dipstick and no red blood cells on microscopic examination.
- Urinary red blood cells;
  - Eumorphic (normal shape) → bleeding along the collecting system.
  - Dysmorphic or red cell casts → GN.
- Urinary casts:
  - Muddy brown casts → ATN, the presence of tubular cell casts and oxalate crystals supports the diagnosis of ATN.
  - While blood cells casts or white blood cells → pyelonephritis or acute interstitial nephritis (AIN), The presence of urine eosinophils is helpful in establishing the diagnosis of AIN.
- Proteinuria.
- **Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa).**
  FENa = (UNa/ PNa)/ (Ucr/ Pcr) X 100.
  [U= urine, P= plasma, Na= sodium, cr= creatinine]
  Values;
  - < 1%. → pre-renal AKI
  - > 1%. → ATN, Exceptions are ATN caused by; radio-contrast, severe burns, acute GN and rhabdomyolysis.

  **N.B**
  - FENa is a valuable test for helping to detect extreme renal avidity for Na in condition such as hepato-renal syndrome (HRS).
  - It is useful in AKI only in the presence of oliguria. It cannot be used as an indication of AKI in patients with; non-oliguric states, GN, those receiving diuretics, and liver cirrhosis.

- **Fractional excretion of urea (FE urea);**
  FEur= (Uur/ Pur)/ (Ucr/ Pcr) X 100
  [U= urine, P= plasma, ur= urea, cr= creatinine]
  FEurea is used in patients who are receiving diuretics, since urea transport is not affected by diuretics. Levels < 35% is suggestive of a pre-renal state.

**2. Blood examination and serology:**
- S. creatinine and blood urea.
- Increases serum creatinine concentration is a better surrogate marker for GFR than BUN concentration.
- BUN/creatinine ratio;
  - 20/1 → pre-renal AKI.
  - < 20/1 → intrinsic or post-renal AKI.
- Increased serum uric acid → tumor lysis syndrome (TLS).
- Increased serum LDH → renal infarction
- Schitocytes in blood film → HUS or TTP.
- Increased rouleaux formation → suggests multiple myeloma.
- Myoglobuling or free Hb → pigment nephropathy.
- Serology; complement levels, ANA, ANCA, Ani-GBM Ab., HBV& HCV, ASOT.

**3. Bladder pressure:** a measure of intra-abdominal pressure;
- < 10 mmHg → normal.
- > 10 mmHg → abnormal
- 15- 25 mmHg → risk of abdominal compartment syndrome and AKI.

**4. Ultrasound and Doppler study:**
- Ultrasound is useful for evaluating existing renal disease and obstruction of the urinary collecting system. Small kidney → suggests CKD.
- Doppler scans: for detecting the presence and nature of renal blood flow.
  - It is reduced in pre-renal and renal AKI. Thus, it is of little diagnostic value in these conditions.
  - It is useful in thrombo-embolic or renal vascular disease.

**5. Renal biopsy:**
Indicated if the cause of intrinsic renal AKI is unclear after exclusion of pre- and post-renal causes.

**6. Biomarkers for AKI:**
1. Serum creatinine and eGFR

#### Principles of treatment of AKI:

Currently, there is no definitive therapy for AKI and supportive care is the mainstay of management regardless of the etiology. However, the following issues require attention.

**A. First;**
Recognition of the etiology of AKI and underlying risk factors that predispose patients to AKI. e.g. DM, CKD, hypertension, cardiac or liver dysfunction, should be evaluated and managed.

**B. Second:**
Monitoring the serum creatinine and urine output to perform staging.

**C. Third:**
Identification and careful management of comorbid conditions, as they can influence the outcome of AKI. e.g.;
- Tight glucose control by intensive insulin therapy.
- Control of hyperkalemia.
- Use of nephrotoxic drugs and planned procedures e.g. imaging study and surgery, have to be balanced against their potential benefits.
- Maintain renal perfusion by use of; volume resuscitations, inotropic or vasopressor support to maintain hemodynamic stability, enhancing kidney recovery and preventing further kidney damage.
- Small dose of dopamine (= 1-3 mcg/kg/ min) causes; selective dilatation of the renal vasculatures, enhancing renal perfusion, and reduce sodium absorption which enhances urine flow that helps to prevent tubular casts obstruction. However, there is controversial decision in use of small dose dopamine in treating AKI.

**D. Fourth:**
- Consensus of renal replacement therapy for AKI:
- There is no current consensus on the indications of RRT for AKI. Moreover, RRT by itself may introduce multiple complications in the patient's management.
- Absolute indications for dialysis in AKI include;
  - Clinically apparent signs and symptoms of uremia.
  - Management of; refractory hyperkalemia, acidosis, and hypervolemia.
  - Many nephrologists initiate dialysis empirically for BUN >100 mg/dl.

Part 1 — Definition

Definition of AKI

Definition

AKI is an abrupt (within 48 hours) and sustained decrease in kidney function, accompanied by changes in blood biochemistry (e.g. a rise in serum creatinine), urine output, or both.

✎
AKI vs. Acute Renal Failure

The term AKI has been proposed to encompass the entire spectrum of the syndrome — from a mild transient rise in s. creatinine to overt renal failure requiring RRT — hence the term AKI is more precise than the term acute RF.

Part 3 — Causes

Intra-renal (Intrinsic) Causes — ATN

Acute tubular necrosis (ATN) is the most common intrinsic cause — subtypes:

Hemodynamic

Cardiovascular surgery, sepsis, prolonged pre-renal cause.

Toxic

Antimicrobials, iodide contrast agents, immunosuppression or antineoplastic agents, organic solvents, heavy metals, and radiation.

Intratubular deposits

Acute UA nephropathy, myeloma, severe hypercalemia, primary oxalosis, sulfadiazine.

Organic pigments (endogenous nephrotoxins)

Myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis), hemoglobinuria (intravascular hemolysis).

Intra-renal (Intrinsic) Causes — Others

Acute GN

e.g. RPGN.

Acute interstitial nephritis

Inflammatory injury of the renal interstitium.

Renal vascular occlusion

Occlusion of renal vessels causing renal ischemia.

N.B.

Nearly all cases of ICU-associated AKI results from more than a single insult.

Part 3 — Causes

Post-renal Causes

  1. Congenital anomalies — ureterocele, bladder diverticulosis, post-urethral valves, neurogenic bladder.
  2. Acquired uropathies — BPH, uretrolithiasis, papillary necrosis, ureteral ligation.
  3. Malignant disease — prostate, bladder, cervix, colon.
  4. Gynecologic non-neoplastic — uterine prolapse, endometriosis.
  5. Drugs — sulfonamides, aminocoproic acid.
  6. Infections — schistosomiasis, TB, candidiasis, asprillosis, actinomycosis.
  7. Others — accidental uretheral catheter occlusion.

Part 4 — Clinical Manifestations

How Does AKI Present?

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part 4 — Clinical Manifestations

Oliguria / Anuria

Oliguria

Urine output < 300 ml/day, < 5 ml/kg/day or 0.5 ml/kg/h. Anuria is complete cessation of urine output.

  • Oliguria is significantly associated with the occurrence of AKI — oliguria alone is the best predictor of AKI.
  • However, about 50% or more of all cases of AKI are non-oliguric.
  • Thus, the maintenance of normal urine output does not provide assurance to the clinician that GFR is normal.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part 4 — Clinical Manifestations

Causes of Anuric AKI

  1. Complete UT obstruction — 90% of cases.
  2. Renal vascular occlusion
    • Renal artery thrombosis.
    • Renal vein thrombosis.
    • Cortical necrosis — sepsis, obstetric accidents, DIC.
    • Renal vasculitis and RPGN.
  3. AKI complicating sepsis, heat stroke and rhabdomyolysis.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part 4 — Clinical Manifestations

Manifestations of Loss of Kidney Function

On rare occasions, when frequent monitoring of urine output or serum creatinine concentration is not being done (ambulatory or hospital ward populations), AKI presents with one or more of the following:

Volume overload

  • Peripheral edema
  • Weight gain
  • Shortness of breath

Rarely

  • GIT symptoms — anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
  • Flank/back pain due to edematous kidneys or UT obstruction
  • Altered mental status or seizures
  • Anemia and bleeding
  • Other symptoms

Part 5 — Investigations

Urine Examination (1/2)

ParameterFinding & interpretation
Urine volume Measure urine volume / 24 h.
Urine color Reddish brown or cola-colored urine → myoglobin or Hb, especially in the setting of a positive dipstick and no red blood cells on microscopic examination.
Urinary red blood cells Eumorphic (normal shape) → bleeding along the collecting system. Dysmorphic or red cell casts → GN.
Urinary casts Muddy brown casts → ATN; presence of tubular cell casts and oxalate crystals supports ATN. WBC casts or WBCs → pyelonephritis or acute interstitial nephritis (AIN); urine eosinophils helpful in establishing AIN.
Proteinuria Assess degree of protein loss.

Part 5 — Investigations

Urine Examination (2/2) — FENa

Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa)

FENa = (UNa / PNa) / (Ucr / Pcr) × 100   [U = urine, P = plasma, Na = sodium, cr = creatinine]

< 1%

Pre-renal AKI.

> 1%

ATN. Exceptions — ATN caused by: radio-contrast, severe burns, acute GN and rhabdomyolysis.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part 5 — Investigations

FENa — N.B. & FEurea

FENa — N.B.

  • FENa is a valuable test for helping to detect extreme renal avidity for Na in conditions such as hepato-renal syndrome (HRS).
  • It is useful in AKI only in the presence of oliguria. It cannot be used as an indication of AKI in patients with: non-oliguric states, GN, those receiving diuretics, and liver cirrhosis.

Fractional excretion of urea (FEurea)

FEur = (Uur / Pur) / (Ucr / Pcr) × 100   [U = urine, P = plasma, ur = urea, cr = creatinine]

  • Used in patients who are receiving diuretics, since urea transport is not affected by diuretics.
  • Levels < 35% are suggestive of a pre-renal state.

Part 5 — Investigations

Bladder Pressure & Ultrasound / Doppler

Bladder pressure

A measure of intra-abdominal pressure:

  • < 10 mmHg → normal
  • > 10 mmHg → abnormal
  • 15 – 25 mmHg → risk of abdominal compartment syndrome and AKI

Ultrasound & Doppler study

  • Ultrasound: useful for evaluating existing renal disease and obstruction of the urinary collecting system. Small kidney → suggests CKD.
  • Doppler: reduced renal blood flow in pre-renal and renal AKI — of little diagnostic value in these conditions.
  • Doppler is useful in thrombo-embolic or renal vascular disease.

Part 6 — Principles of Treatment

Managing AKI

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part 6 — Principles of Treatment

Supportive Care — First, Second, Third

Currently, there is no definitive therapy for AKI and supportive care is the mainstay of management regardless of the etiology. The following issues require attention:

  1. First — Recognition of the etiology of AKI and underlying risk factors that predispose patients to AKI (e.g. DM, CKD, hypertension, cardiac or liver dysfunction) should be evaluated and managed.
  2. Second — Monitoring the serum creatinine and urine output to perform staging.
  3. Third — Identification and careful management of comorbid conditions, as they can influence the outcome of AKI.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part 6 — Principles of Treatment

Managing Comorbid Conditions (Third)

  • Tight glucose control by intensive insulin therapy.
  • Control of hyperkalemia.
  • Use of nephrotoxic drugs and planned procedures (e.g. imaging study and surgery) have to be balanced against their potential benefits.
  • Maintain renal perfusion by use of volume resuscitation, inotropic or vasopressor support to maintain hemodynamic stability, enhancing kidney recovery and preventing further kidney damage.
  • Small dose of dopamine (= 1–3 mcg/kg/min) causes selective dilatation of the renal vasculature, enhancing renal perfusion, and reduces sodium absorption, which enhances urine flow that helps to prevent tubular casts obstruction. However, there is controversial decision in use of small dose dopamine in treating AKI.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part 6 — Principles of Treatment

Renal Replacement Therapy (Fourth)

There is no current consensus on the indications of RRT for AKI. Moreover, RRT by itself may introduce multiple complications in the patient's management.

✓
Absolute indications for dialysis in AKI
  • Clinically apparent signs and symptoms of uremia.
  • Management of refractory hyperkalemia, acidosis, and hypervolemia.
✎
In practice

Many nephrologists initiate dialysis empirically for BUN > 100 mg/dl.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
Takeaway 1

AKI is an abrupt (within 48 hours) decrease in kidney function; the term spans the whole spectrum from a mild creatinine rise to overt renal failure requiring RRT.

✓
Takeaway 2

Stage AKI by RIFLE/AKIN using serum creatinine changes and urine output; think of causes as pre-renal, intra-renal (ATN), or post-renal.

✓
Takeaway 3

FENa < 1% → pre-renal; > 1% → ATN (exceptions: radio-contrast, severe burns, acute GN, rhabdomyolysis). FEurea < 35% suggests pre-renal in patients on diuretics.

✓
Takeaway 4

There is no definitive therapy — supportive care is the mainstay; dialysis is indicated for uremia and refractory hyperkalemia, acidosis, or hypervolemia (many start empirically at BUN > 100 mg/dl).

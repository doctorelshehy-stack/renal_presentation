Simple (Solitary) Renal Cysts

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part I — Simple (Solitary) Renal Cysts

Incidence & Characteristics

Common, benign and clinically silent lesions — found in 50% of persons aged >50 years.

Key facts

  • Common — present in 50% of persons aged >50 years.
  • Not inherited and do not affect the renal function.
  • Commonly < 2 cm & typically solitary, but occasionally multiple & bilateral.
  • Most of them are cortical in site and often bulge through the renal capsule.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part I — Simple (Solitary) Renal Cysts

Clinical Presentations

Commonly asymptomatic

  • Discovered incidentally in clinical examination or imaging.

Rarely symptomatic — there may be

  • Abdominal mass.
  • Hematuria (rare — should raise suspicion of renal cell carcinoma).
  • Occasionally, hypertension caused by compression of intra-renal vessels.
  • Normal renal functions, and prognosis is excellent without treatment.
  • Complications; e.g. hemorrhage, infection (fever, pain, pyuria), or carcinoma.

Part I — Simple (Solitary) Renal Cysts

Diagnosis — Renal Imaging (US / CT)

ModalityCriteria
US
3 sonographic criteria
  1. Echoes-free (i.e. homogeneous)
  2. Sharply demarcated with smooth thin wall
  3. An enhanced back wall
CT Sharply demarcated, smooth thin wall cyst — does not enhance with contrast media (renal cell carcinoma does)
⚠
Caution

Contrast enhancement on CT points to renal cell carcinoma rather than a simple cyst.

Part I — Simple (Solitary) Renal Cysts

Differential Diagnosis & Treatment

Differential diagnosis

Renal abscess PCKD

Treatment

  • Asymptomatic cysts → periodic reevaluation.
  • Large cyst → may require aspiration and alcohol instillation; if it contains > 500 ml of fluid it should be drained surgically.
  • Infected cyst → should be differentiated from abscess & treated with antibiotics.
  • Hypertension may improve after decompression of the cyst.

Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD)

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part II — ADPKD

Incidence & Inheritance

ADPKD

A relatively common familial condition, inherited as a simple autosomal dominant (AD) trait.

Genetics

  • ADPKD-1: genetic defect on chromosome 16.
  • ADPKD-2: genetic defect on chromosome 4.

Age of presentation

  • Common age: middle age (40 years), but may present at any age.

Part II — ADPKD

Associated Abnormalities

ADPKD is a systemic disease that involves other organs.

  • Hepatic cysts — in 50% of patients (usually cause no functional impairment).
  • Pancreatic and splenic cysts.
  • Colonic diverticulosis.
  • Aneurysms of the cerebral arteries → subarachnoid hemorrhage.
  • Mitral valve prolapse, aortic valve abnormalities and aortic aneurysms.
⚠
High risk

Cerebral artery aneurysms predispose to subarachnoid hemorrhage.

Part II — ADPKD

Complications

Renal complications

  • Cyst infection
  • Hematuria
  • Renal stones
  • Renal tumor
  • Renal failure

Other complications

  • Cyst rupture
  • Malignant hypertension
  • Subarachnoid hemorrhage
  • Intractable pain
  • Liver failure (rare)
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part II — ADPKD

Diagnosis

Clinical clues

  • Middle age.
  • Positive family history of cystic kidney disease (in 75% of cases).
  • Urine analysis may show hematuria and mild proteinuria.
  • Slow and steady decline in renal function.

Imaging

  • US: 5 cysts scattered in the cortex and medulla of each kidney.
  • CT: if US results are unclear.
  • IVP: stretching and distortion of the renal calyces.

Treatment (1/2)

ProblemManagement
Control of hypertensionTreated aggressively. ACEIs and ARBs should be used with caution in the presence of renal impairment especially in patients with large cysts. Diuretics should be used cautiously to avoid hypokalemia which is a factor in development of renal cyst?
Renal infection (difficult to treat)Parenteral antibiotics with cyst penetration (e.g. ciprofloxacin and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) for 2 weeks, followed by long-term oral therapy (months).
PainBed rest and analgesics. Cyst decompression may help.

Part III — Autosomal Recessive PKD

Autosomal Recessive Polycystic Kidney Disease (ARPKD)

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part III — ARPKD

Incidence & Clinical Presentation

Incidence

  • A rare autosomal recessive (AR) disorder with a defect on chromosome 6.
  • Parents are not affected and occurrence in siblings is variable.

Presentation by age

  • Perinatal period: high mortality due to pulmonary hypoplasia.
  • Early life: dominated by features of liver disease — hepatic fibrosis and portal hypertension (GI bleeding, hepato-splenomegaly and hypersplenism); hepatic cysts are uncommon.
  • Increasing age: renal involvement becomes prominent — abdominal mass, hypertension, recurrent UTIs, and renal failure in adolescence.
✎
NB

ARPKD may be associated with berry aneurysms, aortic aneurysms and Ehlers-Dalnos syndrome.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Part III — ARPKD

Diagnosis & Treatment

Diagnosis

  • US (test of choice): enlarged kidneys of smooth appearance with increased echogenicity. However, normal US may not exclude the diagnosis.
  • Diagnosis is confirmed by absence of: family history (i.e. normal US in parents), liver cysts, and of other manifestations of ARPKD.

Treatment

  • Neonatal presentation: no good treatment.
  • Childhood: CKD and liver disease are treated as usual. Hypertension and UTIs are treated aggressively.

Closing

Key Takeaways

✓
Simple renal cysts

Common (50% of persons aged >50 years), benign and non-hereditary — diagnosed by US (echo-free, smooth thin wall, enhanced back wall) or CT (no contrast enhancement); asymptomatic cysts need only periodic reevaluation.

✓
ADPKD

Autosomal dominant — chromosome 16 (ADPKD-1) or 4 (ADPKD-2); presents in middle age with hematuria, renal infection, nephrolithiasis, pain, hypertension (50%) and kidney enlargement.

✓
ADPKD is a systemic disease

Hepatic cysts (50%), cerebral artery aneurysms (subarachnoid hemorrhage), colonic diverticulosis and cardiac valve/aortic abnormalities; treat hypertension aggressively and avoid peritoneal dialysis in renal failure.

✓
ARPKD

Rare, autosomal recessive (chromosome 6); perinatal mortality from pulmonary hypoplasia, early-life disease dominated by hepatic fibrosis and portal hypertension; US is the test of choice.

# Cystic Kidney Diseases

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

## I- Simple (Solitary) Renal Cysts

#### Incidence

- Are common (50% of persons aged >50 years).
- They are not inherited and do not affect the renal function.
- Commonly < 2 cm & typically solitary, but occasionally may be multiple & bilateral.
- Most of them are cortical in site and often bulge through the renal capsule.

#### Presentations:
- Commonly asymptomatic (discovered incidentally in clinical exam or imaging).
- Rarely symptomatic: There may be;
  - Abdominal mass
  - Hematuria (rare and should rise a suspicious of renal cell carcinoma)
  - Occasionally, hypertension caused by compression of intra-renal vessels.
  - Normal renal functions, and prognosis is excellent without treatment.
  - Complications; e.g. hemorrhage, infection (fever, pain, pyuria), or carcinoma.

#### Diagnosis: by renal imaging (US/ CT).

- **US; 3 sonographic criteria;**
  1. Echoes-free (i.e. homogeneous)
  2. Sharply demarcated with smooth thin wall
  3. an enhanced back wall.
- **CT;**
  - Sharply demarcated, smooth thin wall cyst.
  - Not enhance with contrast media (renal cell carcinoma does)

#### Differential diagnosis:
- Renal abscess
- PCKD

#### Treatment:
- Asymptomatic cysts → Periodic reevaluation.
- Large cyst → May require aspiration and alcohol instillation. If contains > 500 ml of fluid should be drained surgically.
- Infected cyst should be differentiated from abscess & treated with antibiotics.
- Hypertension may improve after decompression of the cyst.

## II- Hereditary Polycystic Kidney Diseases

### Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD)

#### Incidence

- It is a relatively common familial condition.
- It is inherited as a simple autosomal dominant (AD) with genetic defect on chromosome 16 (ADPKD-1) and on chromosome 4 (ADPKD-2).

#### Clinical features:
- Common age (middle age, 40 years), but may present at any age.
- Presentations:

| Symptom | Cause |
|---|---|
| Hematuria | due to; rupture of a cyst into the renal pelvis, renal stone or UTI. |
| Renal infection | Flank pain, fever, and leukocytosis. Blood cultures may be positive and urine analysis may be normal because the cyst does not communicate directly with urinary tract, |
| Nephrolithiasis | mainly calcium oxalate stone. |
| Abdominal Pain or flank pain | due to infection, bleeding into cyst, rupture of cysts, or nephrolithiasis. |
| Hypertension | in 50% of patients that increase with renal failure. It is due to cyst induced ischemia with activation of RAAS (cyst decompression can lower blood pressure temporarily). |
| Mild anemia | relative to the degree of renal failure |
| Kidney Enlargement | The kidneys may be palpable with irregular surface. |

#### Associated abnormalities:
ADPKD is a systemic disease that involves other organs.
- Hepatic cysts (in 50% of patients) [usually cause no functional impairment]
- Pancreatic and splenic cysts.
- Colonic diverticulosis.
- Aneurysms of the cerebral arteries → subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- Mitral valve prolapse, aortic valve abnormalities and aortic aneurysms.

#### Complications:
- Cyst infection
- Renal tumor
- Cyst rupture
- Malignant hypertension.
- Hematuria
- Subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- Intractable pain
- Liver failure (rare).
- Renal failure.
- Renal stones

#### Diagnosis:
- Middle age.
- Positive family history of cystic kidney disease (in 75% of case).
- Urine analysis may show hematuria and mild proteinuria.
- Slow and steady decline in renal function.
- Imaging:
  - US: 5 cysts scattered in the cortex and medulla of each kidney.
  - CT: if US results are unclear.
  - IVP: stretching and distortion of the renal calyces.

#### Treatment:

| Problem | Management |
|---|---|
| **Control of hypertension** | treated aggressively; ACEIs and ARBs should be used with caution in the presence of renal impairment especially in patients with large cysts. Diuretics should be used cautiously to avoid hypokalemia which is a factor in development of renal cyst? |
| **Renal infection: difficult to treat.** | Parental antibiotics with cyst penetration (e.g. ciprofloxacin and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole), for 2 weeks followed by long term oral therapy (months). |
| **Pain** | Bed rest and analgesics, Cyst decompression may help. |
| **Hematuria** | Usually resolve within 7 days with bed rest and hydration. Recurrent bleeding suggest underlying renal cell carcinoma. |
| **Renal stone** | good hydration (2-3 L/day). |
| **Renal failure** | as usual but avoid peritoneal dialysis. |

### 2- Autosomal Recessive Polycystic Kidney Disease (ARPKD)

#### Incidence

- A rare autosomal recessive (AR) disorder with a defect on chromosome 6.
- Parents are not affected and occurrence in sibling is variable.

#### Symptoms:
Presentation may be seen in; perinatal period, early childhood or early adulthood.
- **Perinatal presentation:** high mortality due to pulmonary hypoplasia.
- **In early life;** The presentation is dominated by features of liver disease. Hepatic cysts are uncommon, but symptoms result from hepatic fibrosis and portal hypertension (i.e. GI bleeding, hepato-splenomegaly and hypersplenism).
- **With increasing age,** symptoms of renal involvement become prominent e.g. abdominal mass, hypertension, recurrent UTIs, and renal failure in adolescence.

**NB:** ARPKD may be associated with; berry aneurysms, aortic aneurysms and Ehlers-Dalnos syndrome.

#### Diagnosis:
- **US:** (test of choice); Enlarged kidneys of smooth appearance with increased echogenicity. However, normal US may not exclude the diagnosis.
- The diagnosis is confirmed by absence of; family history (i.e. normal US in parents). liver cysts, and of other manifestations of ARPKD.

#### Treatment:
- Neonatal presentation, no good treatment.
- Childhood; CKD and liver disease are treated as usual. Hypertension and UTIs are treated aggressively.

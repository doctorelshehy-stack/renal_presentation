Risk factors

Risk Factors

Demographic & lifestyle

  • Age (> 50 years)
  • Male gender
  • Cigarette smoking
  • Low socioeconomic status
  • Family history of hypertensive nephrosclerosis

Clinical & laboratory

  • Systolic hypertension (persistently high)
  • Dyslipidemia
  • Proteinuria
  • Decreased GFR

Diagnosis

Clinical Criteria of Diagnosis

The diagnosis of hypertensive nephrosclerosis is generally based on clinical criteria, not renal biopsy criteria.

  1. History of prolonged hypertension (> 10 years).
  2. Low grade proteinuria (< 2 gm/day).
  3. Grade I or II hypertensive retinopathy.
  4. Small scarred kidneys.
  5. No evidence of primary renal disease.

Management

BP Goal, Salt Restriction & Diuretics

✓
BP goal in CKD

An intensive BP goal <130/80 mmHg has been recommended in patients with CKD.

Salt restriction

  • A low salt diet is critical to achieve BP control while maintaining a simple BP medication regimen.
  • A modest dietary sodium restriction can enhance the effects of antihypertensive medications.

Diuretics

  • Commonly used — represent the cornerstone in the management of CKD patients.
  • In general, as GFR falls, higher doses of diuretics are needed to achieve a natriuretic response.

Management

Antihypertensive Drugs (1/2)

  1. Angiotensin Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACEIs)
  2. Angiotensin Receptors Blockers (ARBs)
  3. Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)
    • Non-dihydropyridine CCBs consistently reduce albuminuria and slow the decline in kidney function.
    • Dihydropyridine CCBs should not be used as monotherapy in proteinuric CKD patients but always in combination with a RAAS blocker.

Management

Antihypertensive Drugs (2/2)

4. Mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) antagonists

  • Constitute an important fourth-line BP agent in the treatment of resistant HTN in later stages of CKD.
  • Risks of hyperkalemia and AKI have limited mineralocorticoid antagonist use in advanced CKD.

5. Other agents

  • Used when treatment with the other agents have failed.

Key Takeaways

✓
Definition & diagnosis

Hypertensive nephrosclerosis is chronic kidney disease complicating arterial hypertension — diagnosed clinically: prolonged HTN (> 10 years), low grade proteinuria (< 2 gm/day), grade I–II hypertensive retinopathy, small scarred kidneys, no primary renal disease.

✓
BP goal & general measures

Intensive BP goal <130/80 mmHg in CKD; a low salt diet is critical, and diuretics are the cornerstone — higher doses are needed as GFR falls.

✓
Drug classes

ACEIs and ARBs are first-line; non-dihydropyridine CCBs reduce albuminuria and slow the decline in kidney function; dihydropyridine CCBs should not be used as monotherapy in proteinuric CKD patients.

✓
Fourth-line agent

Mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) antagonists are important in resistant HTN in later CKD — limited by risks of hyperkalemia and AKI.

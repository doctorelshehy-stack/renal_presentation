# Hypertensive Nephrosclerosis

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definition:

Generally, the term hypertensive nephrosclerosis is used for chronic kidney disease which complicates arterial hypertension (AHT).

---

#### Risk factors:

- Age (> 50 years)
- Male gender.
- Cigarette smoking.
- Low socioeconomic status.
- Family history of hypertensive nephrosclerosis.
- Systolic hypertension (persistently high).
- Dyslipidemia.
- Proteinuria.
- Decreased GFR.

---

#### Diagnosis:

The diagnosis of hypertensive nephrosclerosis is generally based on clinical criteria not renal biopsy criteria.

**The clinical criteria of diagnosis:**

1- History of prolonged hypertension (> 10 years).
2- Low grade proteinuria (<2 gm/day).
3- Grade I or II hypertensive retinopathy.
4- Small scarred kidneys.
5- No evidence of primary renal disease.

---

#### Management:

In patients with CKD, an intensive BP goal <130/80 mmHg has been recommended.

**Salt Restriction:**
- A low salt diet is critical to achieve BP control while maintaining a simple BP medication regimen.
- A modest dietary sodium restriction can enhance the effects of antihypertensive medications.

**Diuretics:**
- Diuretics are commonly used and represent the cornerstone in the management of CKD patients.
- In general, as GFR falls, higher doses of diuretics are needed to achieve a natriuretic response.

**Antihypertensive drugs:**

1- **Angiotensin Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACEIs)**
2- **Angiotensin Receptors Blockers (ARBs)**
3- **Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)**
   - Non-dihydropyridine CCBs consistently reduce albuminuria and slow the decline in kidney function.
   - Dihydropyridine CCBs should not be used as monotherapy in proteinuric CKD patients but always in combination with a RAAS blocker.
4- **Mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) Antagonists**
   - Mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) Antagonists constitute an important fourth-line BP agent in the treatment of resistant HTN in later stages of CKD. However, risks of hyperkalemia and AKI have limited mineralocorticoid antagonist use in advanced CKD.
5- Other agents are used when treatment with the other agents have failed.

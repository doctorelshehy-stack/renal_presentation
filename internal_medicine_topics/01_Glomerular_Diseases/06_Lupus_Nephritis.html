Definition & Prevalence

Definition

What is Lupus Nephritis?

Definition

L.N. is the renal involvement in patients with SLE. It exists with or without clinical manifestations of SLE, and is extremely diverse in its presentation and pathology.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Prevalence

How Common is it?

The kidney

The kidney is the most common organ involvement in SLE.

At diagnosis

Clinically, about 50% of lupus patients have L.N. at diagnosis.

Part 2 — Pathology

Pathology

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Pathology

Glomerular Lesions

Haematoxylin bodies

Rounded bluish inclusions in Hx & E stained sections. They represent the naked nuclei that have been altered by binding to ANA, resulting in clumping of nuclear chromatin. Seen in only 2% of cases — pathognomonic of active L.N.

Wire loop deposits

Subendothelial immune deposits which encircle the entire gl. tuft circumference. Best seen in trichrome & silver stained sections.

Hyaline thrombi

Massive subendothelial immune deposits that protrude into or occlude the glomerular tuft lumina (i.e. intraluminal immune deposits).

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Pathology

Tubulo-Interstitial Disease

The glomeruli are essentially involved. Tubulo-interstitial disease is seen in 50% of cases, especially proliferative L.N.

Pathological classes

ISN/RPS Classification — 6 Major Classes

Based upon LM, IF and EM studies of renal biopsy, according to the International Society of Nephrology / Renal Pathology Society (ISN/RPS) classification.

ClassNameDescription
IMinimal mesangial LNNormal glomeruli by LM and mesangial immune deposits by IF or EM.
IIMesangial proliferative LNMesangial hypercellularity and/or matrix expansion of any degree, in addition to mesangial immune deposits.
IIIFocal proliferative LN<50% of glomeruli are involved.
IVDiffuse proliferative LN>50% of glomeruli are involved.
VMembranous LNDiffuse (global) glomerular capillary wall thickness plus continuous subepithelial immune deposits.
VIAdvanced-stage LNGlobal glomerulosclerosis of ≥90% of the total glomeruli, without evidence of active lesion.

Part 5 — Diagnosis

Diagnosis

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Diagnosis

Clinical & Laboratory

Clinical

Hypertension and/or renal edema.

Laboratory findings

  • Urine exam: red cells, casts, proteinuria.
  • Elevated serum creatinine level.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Diagnosis

Renal Biopsy

Useful in determining initial management. Every patient suspected of microangiopathy with L.N. requires a biopsy to:

  • A. Determine the nature of the renal pathology and the pathological class.
  • B. Exclude other lesions that may complicate the course of SLE, such as: hypersensitivity interstitial nephritis when the patient develops ATN, and pure antiphospholipid $.
  • C. Assessment of the activity index "0–24 score" (acute and potentially treatable lesions) and the chronicity index "0–12 score" (irreversible lesions) — these determine therapy & prognosis.

Part 6 — Management

Management

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Management

I. General Treatment

To treat extra-renal manifestations that may affect the kidney.

MeasureRecommendation
HydroxychloroquineOr an equivalent antimalarial, to all patients with L.N. unless contraindicated.
Control of hypertensionTarget blood pressure is ≤120/80 mmHg.
Control of proteinuriaLow doses of ACEIs or ARBs & protein restriction.
Control of hyperlipidemiaUse statins and low-fat diet.
Avoid nephrotoxic drugse.g. NSAIDs; non-acetylated salicylates are safe.
Avoid pregnancy in active L.N.It may worsen their renal disease; moreover, certain medications may be teratogenic.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Management

II. Specific Treatment

Principal goal in L.N.: normalize renal function & prevent loss of renal function.

  • Corticosteroids and immunosuppressive treatment of L.N. is divided into induction and maintenance phases.
  • Corticosteroids should be instituted if the patient has clinically significant renal disease.
  • Immunosuppressive agents: cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine, or cyclosporine A.

Prognosis

Prognosis by Class

Classes I, II & V

Generally good prognosis.

Classes III & IV

Poor prognosis — tend to progress to ESRD, particularly class IV.

Class VI

HD or transplantation.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
Definition

L.N. is renal involvement in SLE — the most common organ involvement; about 50% of lupus patients have L.N. at diagnosis.

✓
Pathology

Haematoxylin bodies (2% of cases), wire loop deposits and hyaline thrombi are glomerular lesions of active L.N.; tubulo-interstitial disease in 50% (especially proliferative).

✓
ISN/RPS classes

Six classes (I–VI); classes III (<50%) and IV (>50%) are proliferative with poor prognosis; activity index (0–24) and chronicity index (0–12) guide therapy & prognosis.

✓
Management

Hydroxychloroquine for all (unless contraindicated), BP ≤120/80 mmHg, ACEIs/ARBs, statins, avoid NSAIDs and pregnancy in active disease; corticosteroids + immunosuppressives (induction and maintenance).

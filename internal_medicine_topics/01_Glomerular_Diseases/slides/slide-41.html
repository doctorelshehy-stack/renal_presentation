Ch 06 · Lupus Nephritis

Clinical Features & Diagnosis

Clinical Features

• Asymptomatic in many patients.
• Symptomatic — manifested as:
– Hypertension: a clue of renal disease
– Symptoms and signs of acute nephritis, nephrotic syndrome, or rapidly progressive renal failure, and ESRD

Diagnosis — Clinical & Laboratory

• Clinical: hypertension and/or renal edema.

• Laboratory findings:
– Urine examination (red cells, casts, proteinuria)
– Elevated serum creatinine level

Role of Renal Biopsy

Renal biopsy is useful in determining initial management — every patient suspected of having microangiopathy with LN requires a biopsy to:
Determine the nature of the renal pathology and the pathological class.
Exclude other lesions that may complicate the course of SLE, such as hypersensitivity interstitial nephritis when the patient develops ATN, and pure antiphospholipid syndrome.
Assessment of the activity index (0–24 score) — acute and potentially treatable lesions — and the chronicity index (0–12 score) — irreversible lesions — which determine therapy & prognosis.

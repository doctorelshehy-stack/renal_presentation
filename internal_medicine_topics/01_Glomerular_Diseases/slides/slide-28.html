Ch 03 · Nephrotic Syndrome

Treatment — 1. General Treatment

Protein intake

Daily physiological need (1 gm/kg) + the daily protein losses in urine.

N.B: Protein restriction (0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day) in the presence of renal impairment.

High protein intake (1.5–2 gm/kg/day) is NOT advised because:
– Ineffective, due to increased protein loss
– Such a diet is difficult to manage with concomitant salt restriction
– Such a diet could aggravate glomerular damage

Drug control of proteinuria

• ACE & ARBs: cause ↓ intraglomerular hydrostatic pressure → ↓ filtered urinary protein losses.
• Medical nephrectomy (mercurial drug or renal infarction) to abolish severe persistent proteinuria in ESRD.

Control of Edema

  • Bed rest → induces diuresis
  • Na+ restriction
  • Diuretics in unresponsive patients
  • Human albumin infusion:
  • – Indications: severe hypoalbuminemia; diuretic-resistant patient undergoing surgery or an invasive procedure (biopsy)
  • – Disadvantages: expensive & transient effect (24–48 hours)

Rationale

Contrast note: unlike the general principles chapter, here the emphasis is on replacing losses (albumin) while restricting sodium — never use high-protein diets in nephrotic patients.

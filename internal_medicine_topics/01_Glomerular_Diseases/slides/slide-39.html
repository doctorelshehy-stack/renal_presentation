Ch 06 · Lupus Nephritis

Definition, Prevalence & Pathology

Definition

LN is the renal involvement in patients with SLE. It exists with or without clinical manifestations of SLE. It is extremely diverse in its presentation and pathology.

Prevalence

The kidney is the most common organ involvement in SLE.

Clinically, about 50% of lupus patients have LN at diagnosis.

Pathology

The glomeruli are essentially involved, which may show:

Haematoxylin bodies

Rounded bluish inclusions in H&E-stained sections. They represent naked nuclei altered by binding to ANA, resulting in clumping of nuclear chromatin. Seen in only 2% of cases — pathognomonic of active LN.

Wire loop deposits

Subendothelial immune deposits that encircle the entire glomerular tuft circumference. Best seen in trichrome & silver stained sections.

Hyaline thrombi

Massive subendothelial immune deposits that protrude into or occlude the glomerular tuft lumina (i.e. intraluminal immune deposits).

N.B.: Tubulo-interstitial disease is seen in 50% of cases, especially in proliferative LN.

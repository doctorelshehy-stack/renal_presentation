Ch 01 · Principles of Glomerulopathies

Principles of Treatment — I: General Measures

Aims of general treatment

  • Control of symptoms
  • Prevent complications
  • Slow progression of the disease

Diet

Salt & water restriction for edematous and/or hypertensive patients, especially with oliguria.

Protein intake — remember:
– High protein intake → increases proteinuria in renal failure patients and causes nephron damage → progression of renal disease.
– Low protein intake → negative nitrogen balance and malnutrition.

Therefore:
• Normal GFR → physiological needs (1 mg/kg/day) + daily urinary protein loss
• ↓ GFR → moderate protein restriction (0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day) to control anorexia, nausea, vomiting

Control of Edema

  • Bed rest
  • Salt restriction (< 2 gm/day) & fluid restriction
  • Diuretics — preferably loop diuretics; thiazides can be combined
  • Salt-poor albumin (in resistant cases with hypoalbuminemia)
  • Ultrafiltration
  • Hemodialysis

Control of Proteinuria

1. Avoid high-protein diet (0.8–1 gm/day + estimated daily loss)
2. Use of ACEIs & ARBs:
Both compete with the vasoconstrictor (VC) effect of angiotensin II on the efferent arterioles → efferent arteriole vasodilation → decrease intraglomerular pressure → decrease GFR → decrease glomerular filtered protein → decrease proteinuria.

NB: ACEIs are used in small doses in normotensive patients, and used cautiously in renal failure and in hyperkalemic patients.

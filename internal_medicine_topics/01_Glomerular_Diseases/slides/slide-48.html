Ch 07 · Diabetic Nephropathy

Treatment

Aim

Aim: slow the progression of the disease by tight control of hyperglycemia, hypertension, UTI, and protein restriction — and RRT for ESRD diabetic patients.

Hyperglycemia, UTI, Protein, RRT

• Control of hyperglycemia: insulin + proper diet.
• Control of UTI: proper antibiotic according to urine culture and sensitivity.
• Dietary protein restriction: 0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day in patients with decreased GFR.
• RRT for ESRD patients: considered early at GFR < 20 ml/min.

Hypertension

Control of hypertension — one of the most important factors.

Goals:
• < 130/80 mmHg in patients without proteinuria
• < 125/75 mmHg in patients with proteinuria

Drugs:
– ACEI / ARBs
– Diuretics in addition to ACEIs
– CCB (non-dihydropyridine: verapamil & diltiazem)
– β-blockers (BBs)
– Alpha-receptor antagonist, e.g. prazosin

N.B.: Because the kidney metabolizes and excretes insulin, the half-life of insulin (endogenous and exogenous) is prolonged in the setting of decreased GFR — therefore decrease the dose of insulin in DN patients to avoid hypoglycemia.

Ch 04 · Anti-GBM / Goodpasture

Investigations & Treatment

Investigations

  • Evidence of GN: hematuria with dysmorphic red cells, red cell casts and variable degrees of proteinuria
  • Blood: positive circulating anti-GBM antibodies; other serological tests (complements, ASOT, ANA) are normal
  • Renal biopsy: diffuse proliferative GN
CorticosteroidsCyclophosphamide
IV methylprednisolone 7–15 mg/kg/day (maximum 1 gm/day) for 3 days, then oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day; reduce to 20 mg/day by 6 weeks and continue for 6 months.Dose depends upon the patient’s age:
• < 55 years: 3 mg/kg (down to 50 mg) for 3 months
• > 55 years: 2 mg/kg (down to 50 mg) for 3 months

Plasma exchange

Plasma exchange (plasmapheresis): replacement with daily 40–50 ml/kg plasma with albumin for 2 weeks or until anti-GBM antibody disappears.

Caution

N.B.: Plasmapheresis should not be given in patients with anuria and/or crescents > 85% of glomeruli, unless there is pulmonary hemorrhage. Albumin is the recommended replacement fluid with plasmapheresis because of the lower incidence of complications.

Protocol: Summary: 3 days pulse methylprednisolone → 2 weeks course of daily plasmapheresis → 6 months oral corticosteroids and 3 months cyclophosphamide.

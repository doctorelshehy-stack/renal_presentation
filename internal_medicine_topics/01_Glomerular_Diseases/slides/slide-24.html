Ch 03 · Nephrotic Syndrome

Clinical Features

Proteinuria

1. Proteinuria: frothy urine — protein decreases the surface tension of urine, similar to bile salts and detergents.

Edema

2. Edema: usually generalized — involves limbs, face, genitalia & serous cavities (ascites, pleural & pericardial effusion).

Severity of edema correlates with serum albumin & extent of urinary protein losses, and is conditioned by other factors such as heart failure.

Hypertension

3. Hypertension: variable, e.g.:
• In MCD → blood pressure is always normal
• In MCGN → hypertension is always present

High-yield: Frothy urine + generalized edema + hyperlipidemia — think nephrotic syndrome. Remember the MCD vs MCGN blood-pressure contrast.

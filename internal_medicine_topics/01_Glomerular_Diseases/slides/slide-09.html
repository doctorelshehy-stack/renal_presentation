Ch 01 · Principles of Glomerulopathies

Clinical Presentations — The 5 Major Syndromes

Acute GN (acute nephritic syndrome)

Onset: Relatively abrupt
Features: Hematuria, Proteinuria, Oliguria, Edema, Hypertension
Pathology: DPGN
Course: Spontaneous resolution

Nephrotic syndrome

Onset: Variable
Features: Albumin < 2.5 g/dl, Proteinuria ≥ 3+, Hyperlipidemia, Edema, Hypertension (MCGN)
Pathology: Variable lesions
Course: Variable
Notes: Variable onset, pathology & course

Chronic GN

Onset: Insidious / progressive
Features: Proteinuria 0/1+, Hypertension, Urinary sediments +, Slow progressive decline in GFR
Pathology: Glomerulosclerosis, chronic interstitial fibrosis
Course: Progress to renal failure
Notes: The common pathway to ESRD

Rapidly Progressive GN (RPGN)

Onset: Rapid decline in GFR
Features: Hematuria, Proteinuria, Oliguria, Absent or mild hypertension
Pathology: DPGN with crescents
Course: Rapidly progresses to AKI
Notes: Renal emergency — intensive therapy

Asymptomatic proteinuria ± hematuria

Onset: Insidious
Features: Hematuria / proteinuria, Hypertension +/–, Normal GFR
Pathology: FSGN
Course: Persists or recurs
Notes: Accidentally discovered, relatively benign

Key: Every glomerulopathy presents as one (or more) of these 5 clinical syndromes: acute GN, nephrotic syndrome, chronic GN, RPGN, or asymptomatic proteinuria ± hematuria.

# Nephrotic Syndrome (N.S)

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definition: N.S. is a clinical syndrome characterized by:

1. Heavy proteinuria ≥ 3.5 gm/d/1.73 m2 s.a or urinary protein/creatinine ratio > 3.5 in adults.
2. Hypoalbuminemia ≤ 2.5 gm/dl
3. Edema.
4. Hyperlipidemia (hypercholesterlemia).

---

#### Causes: All causes of acute G.N.

- Primary (MCD, MGN, MPGN, MCGN, or FSGN)
- Secondary to (infection, multisystem, malignancy, drugs, metabolic or familial).

---

#### Clinical Features:

**1. Proteinuria:** frothy urine (protein decrease surface tension of urine similar to bile salt and detergents).

**2. Edema:** usually generalized involve limbs, face, genetalia & serous cavities (ascities, pleural & pericardial effusion). Severity of edema correlates with serum albumin & extent of urinary protein losses. & conditioned by other factors as H.F.

**3. Hypertension:** variable, e.g.
- In MCD → blood pressure is always normal.
- In MCGN → hypertension is always present.

| Investigation | Finding |
|---|---|
| **24 h urine ptn** | 24 h urinary proteins (> 3.5 gm/D in adults) |
| **Plasma proteins** | Decrease serum albumin (≤ 2.5 gm/dl) |
| | Serum protein electrophoresis; Decrease albumin. Increase alpha2 & beta globulin fraction. Normal or slightly increase in gamma globulin. Increase fibrinogen. |
| **Plasma lipids** | Increase cholesterol and LDL. Increase TG in 50% of patients. Normal or decrease HDL (due to increased urine loss) |
| **Hypocalcaemia** | due to urinary loss of cholecalciferol binding protein. |
| **RFT** | urea, creatinine, & eGFR are usually normal. Impaired in: Severe hypovolemia → pre-renal AKI. MGN and MCGN. NB: patient with impaired renal function biochemical features of NS are uncommon because of concomitant decease of GF |
| **Urine exam** | red cells and red cell casts → GN & exclude MCD |
| **PLA2R** | Phospholipase A2 Receptor (PLA2R) Ab → Primary MN |
| **↓ serum C3** | Decreased serum C3 → immune complex mediated GN. |
| **ASOT & swab** | ASOT & throat swab → Streptococcal infection. |
| **ANA / ANCA** | ANA → SLE & ANCA → Systemic vasculitis. |
| **Hyperglycemia** | Hyperglycemia → D.M. |

**Selective proteinuria:** tested in children. Blood + Urine samples are examined simultaneously to determine clearance of large MW protein (e.g. IgG) in comparison with small MW protein (e.g. albumin or transferrin).
- Low ratio → selective in MCD, DM & renal amyloidosis
- High ratio → unselective proteinuria in crescentic G.N

**Renal biopsy:**
- Indications: histological diagnosis and to plan therapy.
  - In children: With steroid resistant, dependent and frequent relapse & children with renal impairment
  - In adult: Most adults.
- Not indicated in:
  - Young children with selective proteinuria, normotensive & benign urinary sediment → MCD.
  - Long standing DM (>10 years in type1 and > 5 years in type 2 with retinopathy or neuropathy).
  - Patient under drug therapy such as penicillamine.

---

#### Treatment: (General + specific)

**1. General treatment:**

**Protein intake:** Daily physiological need (1 gm/ kg) + the daily protein losses in urine.
N.B: Protein restriction (0.6-0.8 gm/ kg/day) in the presence of renal impairment. High protein intake (1.5-2 gm/kg/ d) is not advised because:
- Ineffective due to increase protein loss.
- Such diet is difficult to manage with concomitant salt restriction.
- Such diet could aggravate glomerular damage.

**Drug control of proteinuria:**
- ACE & ARBS; cause ↓ intraglomerular hydrostatic pressure → ↓ filtered urinary protein losses.
- Medical nephrectomy by using mercurial drug or renal infarction to abolish severe persistent proteinuria in ESRD.

**Control of Edema:**
1. Bed rest → induce diuresis.
2. Na+ restriction.
3. Diuretics in unresponsive patients.
4. Human albumin infusion:
   - Indications:
     - Severe hypoalbuminemia
     - Diuretic resistant patient undergoing to surgery or invasive procedure (biopsy)
   - Disadvantages:
     - Expensive & Transient effect (24- 48 hours).

**Control of complications:**
1. Subnutrition: proper diet, mineral, and vitamins.
   N.B; Severe proteinuria may justify ablation of kidneys by medical or surgical means.
2. Sepsis: early detection and aggressive treatment
3. Hyperlipidemia: by statins.
4. Hypertension: salt restriction + anti hypertensives.
5. Thrombotic complications: long term oral anticoagulant.
   - Heparin is ineffective due to concomitant urinary loss of antithrombin III.
   - Prophylactic anticoagulant is used in NS due to membranous GN as thromboembolic complications are common.

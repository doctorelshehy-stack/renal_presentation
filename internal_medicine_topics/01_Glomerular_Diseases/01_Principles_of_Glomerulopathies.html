Definition

What is a Glomerulopathy?

Definition

Term used to describe a group of glomerular disorders characterized by the presence of the following, either singly or in combination:

The 5 cardinal features

  1. Proteinuria
  2. Hematuria
  3. Oliguria
  4. Hypertension
  5. Edema

Key features

  • Involvement of both kidneys, either symmetrically.
  • Unknown cause → Primary GN.
  • Secondary to various causes → Secondary GN.

Part 2 — Causes

Causes of Glomerulopathies

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Causes

Primary & Secondary — Infections

I. Primary = Unknown causes = Idiopathic

No identifiable underlying cause.

II. Secondary — Bacterial

  • Streptococcal B-hemolytic (types 1, 4, 12, 49) → post-streptococcal GN.
  • Non-streptococcal: Staphylococci, Gonococci, Salmonella, Meningococcal, Secondary syphilis, Leprosy.

Viral & parasitic

Virus: HBV, HCV, HIV, EBV (infectious mononucleosis), Mumps, Measles, Coxacki, Varicella.

Parasite: Malaria, Schistosomaisis, Filariasis, Toxoplasmosis.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Causes

Multisystem & Malignant Diseases

Multisystem diseases

  • SLE, Rh. Arthritis, Dermatomyositis, Sjogren's, Good-Pasture's syndrome.
  • Vasculitis: PAN, Wagner's granulomatosis, Henochschonlein purpura.

Malignant diseases & paraproteinemias

  • Carcinoma (lung, colon, melanoma).
  • Hematological malignancy: lymphomas (HL, non-HL) and leukemias.
  • Paraproteinaemias, e.g. MM, Amyloidosis, WM, Cryoglobulinaemia.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Causes

Drugs, Metabolic, Heredofamilial & Others

Drugs & toxins

Penicillin, heavy metals (Mercury, Gold), Heroin, Captopril, Antivenom, Antitoxins, Contrast media.

Metabolic

Diabetes mellitus and Gout.

Heredofamilial

Thin basement membrane disease, Alport's syndrome, Fabry's disease, Nail-Patella syndrome, Sickle cell disease.

Others

Sarcoidosis, Pre-eclampsia, Thyrotoxicosis & Myxedema, Serum sickness, Chronic graft rejection, Allergic (bee stings, pollens, cow milk).

Pathology

The Histopathological Types (1/2)

Minimal lesion GN (MCD)

  • LM: normal or mild increase in mesangial cells.
  • EM: fusion of foot processes of epithelial cells (podocytes).
  • IF: no deposits.

Membranous GN (MGN)

  • LM: diffuse thickening of glomerular B.M.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Pathology

The Histopathological Types (2/2)

Proliferative GN

  • Diffuse proliferative: proliferation of all glomerular cells (endothelial, epithelial & mesangial cells):
    1. Acute diffuse proliferative GN.
    2. Diffuse proliferation with crescents (Crescentic GN, RPGN).
    3. Mesangio-proliferative (MPGN): increased mesangial cells & matrix.
    4. Mesangio-capillary GN (MCGN): proliferation of mesangial cells & matrix + thickening of glomerular B.M.
  • Focal proliferative: Focal segmental GN (FSGN): cellular proliferation of some segments in some glomeruli.

Focal segmental glomerulo-sclerosis (FSGS)

Sclerosis of some segments in some glomeruli.

Part 5 — Investigations

Investigations

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Urine & Blood Examination

1. Urine examination

  • Volume in 24 hours: may be decreased or normal.
  • 24-hour urinary protein or urinary protein/creatinine ratio.
  • Microscopic examination of urinary sediment:
    • Red cells → may or may not be present.
    • Dysmorphic red cells → strongly suggestive of GN.
    • Casts → granular & red cell casts (diagnostic of acute GN).

2. Blood examination

  • Blood urea.
  • Serum creatinine.
  • Serum electrolytes (Ca, PO4, K+, Na+).
  • Assessment of GFR (N = 90–120 ml/min).
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Renal Imaging & Renal Biopsy

3. Renal imaging (US)

To exclude other renal pathology:

  • Smooth renal outline.
  • Normal pelvicalyceal system and lower urinary tract.

4. Renal biopsy — needed for

  • Accurate histopathological diagnosis.
  • Assess severity & response to treatment.
  • Prognosis.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Specific Investigations for the Cause

  • Blood glucose → D.M.
  • ASOT and throat or skin swab → recent streptococcal infection.
  • ANA & Anti-Ds DNA → systemic disease, e.g. SLE.
  • Anti-GBM antibody → Goodpasture's disease.
  • Serum IgA antibody → IgA nephropathy (Berger's disease).
✓
Serum complement — decreased in

a) Post-infection, e.g. acute post-streptococcal GN (transient 8 ws), infective endocarditis, and shunt nephropathy. b) SLE. c) Cryoglobulinaemia. d) Serum sickness.

Part 6 — Treatment

Treatment

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Aims & Diet

I. General treatment — aims

  1. Control of symptoms.
  2. Prevent complications.
  3. Slow progression of the disease.

Diet

  • Salt and water restriction for edematous and/or hypertensive patients, especially with oliguria.
  • Protein intake: high protein intake → increases proteinuria in renal failure patients and causes nephron damage → progression of renal disease. Low protein intake → negative nitrogen balance and malnutrition.
  • Normal GFR → physiological needs (1 mg/kg/day) + daily urinary protein loss.
  • ↓ GFR → moderate protein restriction (0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day) for control of anorexia, nausea, vomiting.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Control of Edema

  1. Bed rest.
  2. Salt restriction (<2 gm/day) & fluid restriction.
  3. Diuretics — preferably loop diuretics; thiazides can be combined.
  4. Salt-poor albumin (in resistant cases with hypoalbuminemia).
  5. Ultrafiltration.
  6. Hemodialysis.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Control of Proteinuria

  1. Avoid high protein diet (0.8–1 gm/day + estimated daily loss).
  2. Use of ACEIs & ARBs — both compete with the vasoconstrictor effect of angiotensin II on the efferent arterioles → efferent arteriole V.D. → decrease intraglomerular pressure → decrease GFR → decrease glomerular filtered protein → decrease proteinuria.
✎
N.B.

ACEIs are used in small doses in normotensive patients and used cautiously in R.F. and in hyperkalemic patients.

Treatment — General

Hyperlipidemia & Hypercoagulability

Control of hyperlipidemia

  1. Avoid high-fat diet.
  2. Physical activity.
  3. Use statins (not used in H.D. patients).

Control of hypercoagulability & thrombosis

Prophylactic anticoagulation should be employed in patients with nephrotic syndrome with high risk of thromboembolism.

  1. Increased risk of venous thrombosis (albumin <2.5) → anticoagulants.
  2. Increased risk of arterial thrombosis → aspirin.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — Specific

Specific Treatment According to Etiology

In primary G.N.

  • Immunosuppressive + cytotoxic.
  • Other measures, e.g. plasma exchange.

In secondary G.N.

Treatment of the cause:

  • Control of infection.
  • Treat malignancy.
  • Stop toxins & drugs.
  • Control disease activity, e.g. SLE, DM & Gout.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
The syndrome

Glomerulopathies present with proteinuria, hematuria, oliguria, hypertension and edema — both kidneys affected symmetrically; primary (idiopathic) vs secondary causes.

✓
Pathology triad

LM gives the type and severity, EM the site of immune deposition, and IF the type and pattern (diffuse granular in I.C. GN, smooth linear in anti-GBM disease).

✓
Five clinical syndromes

Acute GN, nephrotic syndrome, chronic GN, RPGN and asymptomatic proteinuria ± hematuria — each with a distinct course; RPGN is a renal emergency.

✓
Investigation & treatment

Renal biopsy gives diagnosis, severity, response and prognosis; general treatment (diet, edema, proteinuria, BP, lipids, anticoagulation) plus specific treatment of the cause.

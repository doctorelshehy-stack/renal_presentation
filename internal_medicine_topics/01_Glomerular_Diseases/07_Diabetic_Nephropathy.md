# Diabetic Nephropathy (DN)

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Renal complications in diabetic patients:

1. Diabetic Nephropathy (DN), (Diabetic Glomeruloscelerosis)
2. Papillary necrosis
3. UTI (asymptomatic bacteriuria, acute pyelonephritis, and perinephric abscess)
4. Ischemic Nephropathy due to renal artery atherosclerosis
5. Hydronephrosis due to atonic bladder
6. AKI secondary to:
   a- Reaction to contrast (contrast nephropathy)
   b- U.T obstruction secondary to papillary necrosis

**N.B:** As type 2 DM is more common, most of the cases of DN seen are associated with type 2 DM.

---

#### Definition of DN:

Presence of proteinuria with or without hypertension and renal impairment in patients with diabetes mellitus of several years duration.

---

#### Prevalence:

- In type 1; about 30-50% of patients develop DN since they survive longer.
- In type 2; about 20% of patients develop DN, since they usually die earlier.

---

#### Clinical Features (stages):

**In type 1 DM; 5 stages:**

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| **Stage I (Hyperfiltration /Hypertrophy stage)** | Increase in GFR by 20-40% above age matched control subjects. Hypertrophy of the kidneys by US. Clinically there may be polyuria. |
| **Stage II (Silent stage)** | Normal GFR (in most of patients). Normal urinary albumin excretion (< 30 mg/day). Early structural renal damage. N.B.: About 30-50% of patients will proceed to stage III. |
| **Stage III (Incipient Nephropathy)** | GFR start to decline. Microalbuminuria (30-300 mg/day) [5-10 ys after the onset of DM]. Early hypertension or rise of BP above the previous reading. |
| **Stage IV (Overt Nephropathy)** | GFR progressive reduction. Overt proteinuria > 0.5 gm/day. Hypertension. |
| **Stage V (ESRD)** | ESRD require RRT. Presence of other complications of diabetes such as; retinopathy, neuropathy, CHF, cerebrovascular disease & peripheral vascular disease. |

**In type 2 DM;**
- Hyperfiltration stage is rarely detected.
- Microalbuminuria is frequently present at the diagnosis of DM.
- HTN is usually present at the time of diagnosis of nephropathy.

---

#### Pathology: (3 major lesions)

1. **Glomerulo-sclerosis.** Diffuse (more common but non-specific) and/or nodular (less common but pathognomonic)
2. **Chronic tubule-interstitial nephritis.**
3. **Vasculitic lesions;** arteriolosclerosis in afferent & efferent arterioles (pathognomonic for DM).

---

#### Pathogenesis: (Multifactorial)

1. **Haemodynamic factor:** Hyperglycemia → ↑ GFR → ↑ Intraglomerular.pressure → endothelial injury → glomerulosclerosis.
2. **Metabolic factors:** Hyperglycemia → glycosylation of mesangial & GBM proteins → trapping of circulating macromolecules in Glomerular capillary wall and mesangium → mesangial hyperplasia → alteration of GBM permeability.
3. **Genetic susceptibility:** As not all diabetic patients develop DN.

---

#### Risk factors for progression of D,N.:

1. Proteinuria
2. Hypertension
3. Hyperglycemia
4. Smoking
5. High protein diet
6. Genetic factors

---

#### Diagnosis: (clinical + urine examination ± Renal biopsy)

**Clinical Diagnosis:**
- DM of ≥ 10 years duration with proteinuria ± HTN & renal insufficiency.
- The presence of diabetic retinopathy strengthens the diagnosis.

**Criteria of diagnosis of DN as a cause of CKD:**
1. Long standing DM (≥ 10 years) before the onset of CKD.
2. Normal sized kidneys (by US).
3. Presence of diabetic retinopathy.
4. Benign urinary sediment i.e. no hematuria, no cellular casts.
5. Proteinuria is still present when the patient has already started dialysis.

**Urine examination:** Presence of proteinuria (micro- OR macro-albuminuria).

**Renal Biopsy:** Biopsy is needed if;
1. Duration of DM is < 10 years in the absence of retinopathy or neuropathy.
2. Suspicion of alternative diagnosis such as;
   - Sudden onset of NS in early DM (< 7 ys in type 1 and < 5ys in type 2).
   - Renal insufficiency + active urinary sediment (i.e. red cells, cellular casts).

---

#### Treatment:

**Aim:** Slow the progression of the disease by tight control of; hyperglycemia, hypertension, UTI, and protein restriction. & RRT for ESRD diabetic patients.

**Control of hyperglycemia:** Insulin + proper diet.

**Control of hypertension:** one of the most important factors
- The goal is to lower the Blood Pressure to:
  - < 130/80 mmHg in patients without proteinuria.
  - < 125/75 mmHg in patients with proteinuria.
- Drugs:
  - ACEI/ARBs
  - Diuretics in addition to ACEIs.
  - CCB (non-dihydropyridine; verapamil & diltiazem)
  - Β-blockers (BBs).
  - Alpha-receptor antagonist e.g. prazosin.

**Control of UTI:**
Proper antibiotic according to urine culture and sensitivity.

**Dietary protein restriction:**
0.6- 0.8 gm / kg/day in patients with decreased GFR.

**RRT for ESRD patients:**
considered early at GFR < 20 ml/min.

**N.B:**
Because the kidney metabolizes and excretes insulin, the half-life of insulin (endogenous and exogenous) is prolonged in the setting of decreased GFR, therefore decrease the dose of insulin in D.N patients to avoid hypoglycemia.

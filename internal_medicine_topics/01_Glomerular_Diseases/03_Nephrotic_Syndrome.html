Definition

What is N.S.?

Definition

N.S. is a clinical syndrome characterized by:

  1. Heavy proteinuria ≥3.5 gm/d/1.73 m2 s.a, or urinary protein/creatinine ratio >3.5 in adults.
  2. Hypoalbuminemia ≤2.5 gm/dl.
  3. Edema.
  4. Hyperlipidemia (hypercholesterolemia).

Causes

Causes of Nephrotic Syndrome

All causes of acute G.N.

Primary

MCD, MGN, MPGN, MCGN, or FSGN.

Secondary

Infection, multisystem, malignancy, drugs, metabolic or familial.

Part 3 — Clinical Features

Clinical Features

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

Proteinuria & Edema

1. Proteinuria

Frothy urine — protein decreases the surface tension of urine, similar to bile salts and detergents.

2. Edema

Usually generalized: involves limbs, face, genitalia & serous cavities (ascites, pleural & pericardial effusion).

Severity correlates with serum albumin & extent of urinary protein losses, and is conditioned by other factors such as H.F.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

3. Hypertension — Variable

In MCD

Blood pressure is always normal.

In MCGN

Hypertension is always present.

Investigations

Selective Proteinuria

Tested in children

Blood + urine samples are examined simultaneously to determine the clearance of large MW protein (e.g. IgG) in comparison with small MW protein (e.g. albumin or transferrin).

  • Low ratio → selective proteinuria in MCD, DM & renal amyloidosis.
  • High ratio → unselective proteinuria in crescentic G.N.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Renal Biopsy

Indications

Histological diagnosis and to plan therapy.

  • In children: with steroid-resistant, steroid-dependent and frequent relapse, and children with renal impairment.
  • In adults: most adults.

Not indicated in

  • Young children with selective proteinuria, normotensive & benign urinary sediment → MCD.
  • Long-standing DM (>10 years in type 1 and >5 years in type 2, with retinopathy or neuropathy).
  • Patients under drug therapy such as penicillamine.

Part 5 — Treatment

Treatment (General + Specific)

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Protein Intake

Daily physiological need (1 gm/kg) + the daily protein losses in urine.

✎
N.B.

Protein restriction (0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day) in the presence of renal impairment. High protein intake (1.5–2 gm/kg/day) is not advised because:

  • Ineffective due to increased protein loss.
  • Such a diet is difficult to manage with concomitant salt restriction.
  • Such a diet could aggravate glomerular damage.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Drug Control of Proteinuria

  • ACE & ARBs: cause ↓ intraglomerular hydrostatic pressure → ↓ filtered urinary protein losses.
  • Medical nephrectomy by using mercurial drug or renal infarction, to abolish severe persistent proteinuria in ESRD.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Control of Edema

  1. Bed rest → induces diuresis.
  2. Na+ restriction.
  3. Diuretics in unresponsive patients.
  4. Human albumin infusion.
ℹ
Human albumin infusion

Indications: severe hypoalbuminemia; diuretic-resistant patient undergoing surgery or an invasive procedure (biopsy).

Disadvantages: expensive & transient effect (24–48 hours).

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Control of Complications

  1. Subnutrition: proper diet, minerals and vitamins.
  2. Sepsis: early detection and aggressive treatment.
  3. Hyperlipidemia: by statins.
  4. Hypertension: salt restriction + antihypertensives.
  5. Thrombotic complications: long-term oral anticoagulant.
✎
N.B.

Heparin is ineffective due to the concomitant urinary loss of antithrombin III. Prophylactic anticoagulant is used in N.S. due to membranous GN, as thromboembolic complications are common. Severe proteinuria may justify ablation of kidneys by medical or surgical means.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
Diagnostic tetrad

Proteinuria ≥3.5 gm/day (or UPCR >3.5), hypoalbuminemia ≤2.5 gm/dl, edema and hyperlipidemia.

✓
Histology clues

In MCD blood pressure is always normal; in MCGN hypertension is always present. PLA2R Ab → primary MN; ↓ serum C3 → immune complex mediated GN.

✓
Renal biopsy

Indicated in most adults and in steroid-resistant / dependent / frequently relapsing children; not indicated in typical MCD children, long-standing DM, or drug-induced cases.

✓
Treatment

ACE & ARBs reduce proteinuria; albumin infusion in resistant cases; statins for lipids; long-term oral anticoagulant (heparin ineffective — antithrombin III loss).

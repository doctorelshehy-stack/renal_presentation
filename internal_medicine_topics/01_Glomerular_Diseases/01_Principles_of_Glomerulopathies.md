## Glomerulopathies

### Principles of Glomerulopathies

#### Definition

Term used to describe a group of glomerular disorders characterized by:

| Clinically | Pathologically | Etiologically |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

---

Presence of following, either singly or in combination.
1. Proteinuria
2. Hematuria
3. Oliguria
4. Hypertension
5. Edema

Involvement of both kidneys Either symmetrically.

- Unknown: Primary GN
- Secondary to various causes: Secondary GN

---

#### Causes:

**I- Primary = Unknown Causes = Idiopathic.**

**II- Secondary:**

1. **Infections (post-infectious):**
   - **Bacterial:**
     - Streptococcal: B-hemolytic (1, 4. 12. 49) [post-streptococcal GN].
     - Non-streptococcal bacterial infection; Staphylococci, Gonococci, Salmonella, Meningococcal, Secondary syphilis, Leprosy.
   - **Virus:** HBV, HCV, HIV, EBV (infection mononucleosis), Mumps, Measles, Coxacki, Varicella.
   - **Parasite:** Malaria, Schistosomaisis, Filariasis, Toxoplasmosis.

2. **Multisystem diseases:**
   - SLE, Rh. Arthritis, Dermatomyosities, Sjogren's $, Good-Pasture's syndrome, Vasculitis (PAN, Wagner's granulomatosis, Henochschonlein purpura).

3. **Malignant diseases & Paraproteinemias:**
   - Carcinoma (lung, colon, Melanoma).
   - Hematological malignancy: Lymphomas (HL, non-HL) and Leukemias.
   - Paraproteinaemias e.g: MM, Amyloidosis, WM, Cryoglobinaemia.

4. **Drugs & Toxins:**
   - Penicillin, Heavy Metals (Mercury- Gold), Heroin, Captopril, Antivenom, Antitoxins, Contrast media.

5. **Metabolic:**
   - Diabetes mellitus and Gout.

6. **Heridofamilial:**
   - Thin basement membrane disease, Alport's syndrome, Fabry's disease, Nail-Patella syndrome, Sickle cell disease.

7. **Others:**
   - Sarcoidosis, Pre-eclampsia, Thyrotoxicosis & Myxedema, Serum Sickness, Chronic graft rejection, Allergic: (bee stings, pollens, cow milk).

---

#### Pathology:

**Macroscopic:** of no value (can be evaluated by Ultrasound).

| | In acute GN | In Chronic GN |
|---|---|---|
| **Size of kidney** | N. or increased | N. or decreased |
| **Surface of kidney** | punctate hge. | fine granular or cortical scars |

**Microscopic: Renal biopsy**

| Method | Value |
|---|---|
| **LM** | The histopathological type / The severity/degree of disease |
| **EM** | Define site of immune deposition / Subendothelial, Subepithelial, Mesangial |
| **IF** | The type of immune deposition e.g. IgA, C3, C4 / In I.C. GN; deposits appear diffuse granular pattern / In Anti-GBM Ab; deposits appear smooth linear pattern |

---

#### The histopathological types:

1. **Minimal lesion GN (Minimal Change Disease "MCD"):**
   - LM: Normal or mild increase in mesangial cells.
   - EM: Fusion of foot process of epithelial cells (Podocytes).
   - I.F: No deposits.

2. **Membranous GN (MGN):**
   - LM: diffuse thickening of glomerular B.M.

3. **Proliferative GN:**
   - **Diffuse proliferative:** Proliferation of all glomerular cells (endothelial, epithelial & mesangial cells).
     - a) Acute diffuse proliferative GN
     - b) Diffuse proliferation with crescents (Crescentic GN, RPGN)
     - c) Mesangio-proliferative (MPGN): Increased mesangial cells & matrix.
     - d) Mesangio-capillary GN "MCGN": Proliferation of mesangial cell & matrix + thickening of glomerular B.M.
   - **Focal proliferative**
     - a) Focal segmental GN "FSGN": cellular proliferation of some segments in some glomeruli

4. **Focal segmental glomerulo-sclerosis (FSGS):** Sclerosis of some segments in some glomeruli.

#### Investigations:

**1. Urine Examination:**
- Volume in 24 hours: may be decreased or normal.
- 24 hours urinary protein or urinary protein/creatinine ratio.
- Microscopic Examination of urinary sediment.
  - Red Cells → may or may not be present.
  - Dysmorphic red cells → strongly suggestive of GN.
  - Casts → granular & red cell cast (Diagnostic of acute GN).

**2. Blood Examination:**
- Blood urea.
- Serum creatinine.
- Serum electrolytes (Ca, PO4, K+, Na+).
- Assessment of GFR (N = 90-120 ml/min).

**3. Renal imaging:** US (i.e., to exclude other renal pathology).
- Smooth renal outline.
- Normal pelvicalyceal system and lower urinary tract.

**4. Renal Biopsy:** Needed for:
- Accurate histopathological diagnosis.
- Assess severity & response to treatment.
- Prognosis.

**5. Specific investigations for the determination of the cause:** e.g
- Blood glucose: for D.M
- ASOT and throat or skin swab for recent streptococcal infection.
- ANA & Anti-Ds DNA for systemic disease e.g., SLE.
- Anti-GBM antibody for Good pasture's disease.
- Serum IgA antibody: IgA nephropathy (Berger's disease).
- Serum complement: Decreased in:
  - a) Post-infection; e.g. acute post-streptococcal GN "transient 8 ws", infective endocarditis, and shunt nephropathy.
  - b) SLE.
  - c) Cryoglobulinaemia.
  - d) Serum Sickness.

#### Principles of treatment:

**I) General treatment: aims of general treatment;**

1. Control of symptoms
2. Prevent complications
3. Slow progression of the disease.

**• Diet:**
- Salt and water restriction for edematous and /or hypertensive patients especially with oliguria.
- Protein intake: Remember that;
  - High protein intake → Increase proteinuria in renal failure patients and cause nephron damage → Progression of renal disease.
  - Low protein intake → Negative nitrogen balance and malnutrition.
- For this;
  - Patients with normal GFR → give the physiological needs (1mg/kg/day) + daily urinary protein loss.
  - Patients with ↓ GFR → allow moderate protein restriction (0.6-0.8 gm/kg/day) for control of anorexia, nausea, vomiting.

**• Control of edema:**
1. Bed rest
2. Salt restriction (<2 gm/day) & Fluid restriction.
3. Diuretics; preferably loop diuretics. Thiazides can be combined.
4. Salt poor albumin (in resistant cases with hypoalbuminemia).
5. Ultrafiltration.
6. Hemodialysis.

**• Control of proteinuria:**
1. Avoid high protein diet (0.8-1 gm/day + estimated daily loss)
2. Use of ACEIs & ARBs.
   - Both compete with VC effect of angiotensin II on the efferent arterioles → efferent artery V.D. → decrease intraglomerular pressure → decrease GFR → decrease glomerular filtered protein → decrease proteinuria.
   - NB: ACEIs are used in small doses in normotensive and used cautiously in R.F and in hyperkalemic patients.

**• Control of hypertension:**
- Value of blood pressure control:
  - Relief symptoms.
  - Prevent hypertensive complications on other systems.
  - Decrease progression of renal disease (i.e. Reno-protective).
- Target blood pressure control: According to degree of proteinuria
  - If >1gm/day → target BP <125/75 (mean BP <92).
  - If <1gm/day → target BP< 130/80 (mean BP<98)
- Choice of antihypertensive agent:
  - ACEIs/ARBs
  - CCB
  - All have reno-protective effect.

**• Control of hyperlipidemia:**
1. Avoid high-fat diet
2. Physical activity
3. Use statins (not used in H.D. patients).

**• Control of Hypercoagulability and thrombosis:**
- Prophylactic anticoagulation should be employed in patients with nephrotic syndrome with high risk of thromboembolism.
1. Increased risk of venous thrombosis (Albumin<2.5) → Anticoagulants.
2. Increased risk of arterial thrombosis → Aspirin

**II) Specific treatment: according to etiology;**

- In primary G.N.:
  - Immunosuppressive + Cytotoxic.
  - Other measures e.g. Plasma exchange.
- In Secondary G.N.:
  - TTT of cause e.g. Control of infection, treat malignancy, stop toxins & drugs, control of disease activity e.g. SLE, DM & Gout.

**Course:**
Variable depending on renal pathology & etiology. e.g.
- Anti-GBM & ANCA-associated crescentic GN → rapid progression to ESRD, unless treated.
- IgA Nephropathy & FSGS → indolent but persistence course→ to RF.
- Post-streptococcal GN → tend to resolve completely with little risk for progression to ESRD.
- MGN → unpredictable course may remit spontaneously, persistent/ relapses, or progress over years to ESRD.

Disease vs Syndrome

Goodpasture Disease (Anti-GBM GN)Goodpasture's syndrome
A kidney-limited disease with:
a) G.N. resulted from glomerular deposition of anti-GBM antibodies.
b) Circulating anti-GBM antibodies.		 A systemic disease with a triad of:
a) G.N. resulted from glomerular deposition of anti-GBM antibodies.
b) Circulating anti-GBM antibodies.
c) Pulmonary hemorrhage.

Differential diagnosis

D.D. of Hemoptysis with Renal Disease

Pulmonary-renal syndromes:

  1. Goodpasture syndrome.
  2. Henoch-Schonlen purpura.
  3. Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE).
  4. Microscopic polyangitis.
  1. Cryoglobulinaemia.
  2. Advanced uremia with pulmonary edema and coagulopathies.
  3. Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP).
  4. Pulmonary embolism with R.V. thrombosis.

1. Urine Examination

Evidence of G.N.: hematuria with dysmorphic red cells, red cell casts and variable degrees of proteinuria.

Part 5 — Treatment

Treatment

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

Corticosteroids & Cyclophosphamide

CorticosteroidsCyclophosphamide
IV methylprednisolone 7–15 mg/kg/day (maximum 1 gm/day) for 3 days, then oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day; reduce to 20 mg/day by 6 weeks & continue for 6 months. Dose depends upon the patient's age:
  • <55 y: 3 mg/kg (down to 50 mg) for 3 months.
  • >55 y: 2 mg/kg (down to 50 mg) for 3 months.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

Plasma Exchange (Plasmapheresis)

Replacement with daily 40–50 ml/kg plasma with albumin for 2 weeks, or until anti-GBM antibody disappears.

N.B. — When to Withhold Plasmapheresis

⚠
Warning

Plasmapheresis should not be given in patients with anuria and/or crescents >85% of glomeruli, unless there is pulmonary hemorrhage.

ℹ
Note

Albumin is the recommended fluid with plasmapheresis, because of the lower incidence of complications.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
Disease vs syndrome

Goodpasture disease is kidney-limited (anti-GBM GN); Goodpasture syndrome adds pulmonary hemorrhage (triad with circulating anti-GBM antibodies).

✓
Histology

Diffuse proliferative GN by LM with linear deposits of anti-GBM IgG by IF; other serological tests (complements, ASOT, ANA) are normal.

✓
Treatment

Pulse methylprednisolone 7–15 mg/kg/day × 3 days → oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day → reduce to 20 mg/day by 6 weeks, continue 6 months; cyclophosphamide 2–3 mg/kg (down to 50 mg) for 3 months; plasma exchange 40–50 ml/kg daily for 2 weeks.

✓
Contraindication

No plasmapheresis with anuria and/or crescents >85% of glomeruli unless pulmonary hemorrhage is present; albumin is the recommended replacement fluid.

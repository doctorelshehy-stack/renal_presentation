# Post-Streptococcal G.N. (PSGN)

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Organism:

Group A β-hemolytic streptococci (nephritogenic strain, types 1,4,12,49). Type 49 is the most common isolated type.

---

#### Clinical features:

**History of streptococcal infection**
History of streptococcal infection (tonsillitis, pharyngitis, O.M., or cellulitis): 1-2 weeks before the onset of acute nephritis. In skin infection latent period may be prolonged to 4 weeks.

The latent period (time between exposure to infection and development of acute nephritis) reflects the time taken for I.C. formation & deposition & glomerular injury, shorter latent period signifies exacerbation of underlying CKD e.g. IgA nephropathy rather than de novo acute G.N.

| Presentation | Notes |
|---|---|
| 1. Acute GN; In children, picture of acute G.N. In adult, the history of streptococcal infection is less commonly obtained, and the onset is subacute or insidious with progressive and slowly developing edema of the L.L. | -About 10% of infected will develop GN. - Infection may be mild and pass unnoticed. - no relationship between severity of infection & probability of the development of acute nephritis. |
| 2. N.S.; uncommon (in < 20% of cases). | |
| 3. RPGN; in 5% of cases. | |
| 4. Asymptomatic; discovered during routine urine examination. | |

---

#### Investigations:

- **Evidence of streptococcal infection:**
  - Antibody to streptococcal exo-enzymes (SO, DNAase, BNDase and hyalurenidase).
  - Decrease CH50 & C3 during acute phase, then returns to normal within 8 weeks of nephritis (i.e. transient hypo-complementemia)
- **Evidence of acute GN through:**
  - Urine exam: dysmorphic red cells, red cell casts & proteinuria (< 3 gm/day)
  - Blood (transient increase S. cholestrol (in NS), ↓S. albumin, renal impairment).
  - Renal biopsy:
    - LM: Diffuse endocapillary proliferative GN ± few crescents.
    - EM: Subepithelial electron dense deposits (humps) and variable degrees of mesangial deposits.
    - IF: Granular deposit of C3& IgG.

---

#### Treatment:

**General/ Supportive/Symptomatic (see treatment of GN)**
1. Bed rest
2. Diet: Salt, fluid and protein restriction
3. Drugs:
   a. Diuretics
   b. Vasodilator for control of blood pressure.
   c. Steroid, cytotoxic drugs and anticoagulants are of no values and may be harmful except in RPGN.
4. Dialysis for R.F.

**Specific treatment: Antibiotic (penicillin) therapy.**

1. **For patients:**
   Aim:
   - Decrease antigen load → decrease I.C. formation → may stop progression of disease.
   - Halt spread of potentially nephritogenic streptococci from the patient to family members in close contact.
   - Such treatment, however, does not seem to be very effective in aborting or ameliorating the course of the disease.

2. **For close contact:**
   Use of short-term penicillin prophylaxis (phenoxy penicillin 500 mg/day) to all individuals at high risk in a closed community or family.

**N.B**
No effect of use of long-term penicillin in prophylaxis after the development of GN. Removal of infected tonsil or the septic foci should be delayed until convalescence is advanced as the operation may be followed by exacerbation of the disease, however, if needed give benzyl penicillin on the day of operation and three days after.

Part 1 — Renal Complications in Diabetes

Renal Complications in Diabetic Patients

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Renal complications

Renal Complications in Diabetes

  1. Diabetic Nephropathy (DN) — diabetic glomerulosclerosis.
  2. Papillary necrosis.
  3. UTI (asymptomatic bacteriuria, acute pyelonephritis, and perinephric abscess).
  4. Ischemic nephropathy due to renal artery atherosclerosis.
  5. Hydronephrosis due to atonic bladder.
  6. AKI secondary to: a) reaction to contrast (contrast nephropathy); b) U.T. obstruction secondary to papillary necrosis.
✎
N.B.

As type 2 DM is more common, most of the cases of DN seen are associated with type 2 DM.

Part 2 — Definition & Prevalence

Definition & Prevalence

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Definition

Definition of DN

Definition

Presence of proteinuria with or without hypertension and renal impairment in patients with diabetes mellitus of several years duration.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Prevalence

Prevalence

Type 1 DM

About 30–50% of patients develop DN, since they survive longer.

Type 2 DM

About 20% of patients develop DN, since they usually die earlier.

Part 3 — Clinical Features (Stages)

Clinical Features — Stages

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features — stages

Stages in Type 1 DM (1/2)

StageDescription
Stage I — Hyperfiltration / HypertrophyIncrease in GFR by 20–40% above age-matched control subjects. Hypertrophy of the kidneys by US. Clinically, there may be polyuria.
Stage II — Silent stageNormal GFR (in most patients). Normal urinary albumin excretion (<30 mg/day). Early structural renal damage.
✎
N.B.

About 30–50% of patients will proceed to stage III.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features — stages

Stages in Type 1 DM (2/2)

StageDescription
Stage III — Incipient nephropathyGFR starts to decline. Microalbuminuria (30–300 mg/day) — 5–10 years after the onset of DM. Early hypertension or rise of BP above the previous reading.
Stage IV — Overt nephropathyGFR progressive reduction. Overt proteinuria >0.5 gm/day. Hypertension.
Stage V — ESRDESRD requires RRT. Presence of other complications of diabetes: retinopathy, neuropathy, CHF, cerebrovascular disease & peripheral vascular disease.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features — stages

In Type 2 DM

  • Hyperfiltration stage is rarely detected.
  • Microalbuminuria is frequently present at the diagnosis of DM.
  • HTN is usually present at the time of diagnosis of nephropathy.

Part 4 — Pathology & Pathogenesis

Pathology & Pathogenesis

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Pathology

Pathology — 3 Major Lesions

1. Glomerulosclerosis

Diffuse (more common but non-specific) and/or nodular (less common but pathognomonic).

2. Chronic tubulo-interstitial nephritis

Chronic interstitial inflammation and fibrosis.

3. Vasculitic lesions

Arteriolosclerosis in afferent & efferent arterioles (pathognomonic for DM).

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Pathogenesis

Pathogenesis — Multifactorial

1. Haemodynamic factor

Hyperglycemia → ↑ GFR → ↑ intraglomerular pressure → endothelial injury → glomerulosclerosis.

2. Metabolic factors

Hyperglycemia → glycosylation of mesangial & GBM proteins → trapping of circulating macromolecules in the glomerular capillary wall and mesangium → mesangial hyperplasia → alteration of GBM permeability.

3. Genetic susceptibility

As not all diabetic patients develop DN.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Risk factors

Risk Factors for Progression of DN

1. Proteinuria 2. Hypertension 3. Hyperglycemia 4. Smoking 5. High protein diet 6. Genetic factors

Part 5 — Diagnosis

Diagnosis

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Diagnosis

Clinical Diagnosis

Clinical

  • DM of ≥10 years duration with proteinuria ± HTN & renal insufficiency.
  • The presence of diabetic retinopathy strengthens the diagnosis.

Criteria of DN as a cause of CKD

  1. Long-standing DM (≥10 years) before the onset of CKD.
  2. Normal sized kidneys (by US).
  3. Presence of diabetic retinopathy.
  4. Benign urinary sediment — no hematuria, no cellular casts.
  5. Proteinuria is still present when the patient has already started dialysis.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Diagnosis

Urine Examination & Renal Biopsy

Urine examination

Presence of proteinuria (micro- OR macro-albuminuria).

Renal biopsy — needed if

  1. Duration of DM is <10 years in the absence of retinopathy or neuropathy.
  2. Suspicion of alternative diagnosis, such as:
    • Sudden onset of N.S. in early DM (<7 years in type 1 and <5 years in type 2).
    • Renal insufficiency + active urinary sediment (red cells, cellular casts).

Part 6 — Treatment

Treatment

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

Aim & Control of Hyperglycemia

Aim

Slow the progression of the disease by tight control of: hyperglycemia, hypertension, UTI, and protein restriction; and RRT for ESRD diabetic patients.

Control of hyperglycemia

Insulin + proper diet.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

Control of Hypertension

One of the most important factors.

PatientsGoal blood pressure
Without proteinuria<130/80 mmHg
With proteinuria<125/75 mmHg

Drugs

  • ACEI / ARBs.
  • Diuretics, in addition to ACEIs.
  • CCB (non-dihydropyridine: verapamil & diltiazem).
  • β-blockers (BBs).
  • Alpha-receptor antagonist, e.g. prazosin.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

UTI, Protein Restriction & RRT

  • Control of UTI: proper antibiotic according to urine culture and sensitivity.
  • Dietary protein restriction: 0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day in patients with decreased GFR.
  • RRT for ESRD patients: considered early at GFR <20 ml/min.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

N.B. — Insulin Dose

⚠
N.B.

Because the kidney metabolizes and excretes insulin, the half-life of insulin (endogenous and exogenous) is prolonged in the setting of decreased GFR. Therefore decrease the dose of insulin in D.N. patients to avoid hypoglycemia.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
Definition & prevalence

Proteinuria ± hypertension ± renal impairment in DM of several years duration; 30–50% of type 1 and ~20% of type 2 patients develop DN.

✓
Five stages in type 1

Hyperfiltration (GFR +20–40%) → silent → incipient (microalbuminuria 30–300 mg/day) → overt (proteinuria >0.5 gm/day) → ESRD; in type 2, microalbuminuria is often present at diagnosis.

✓
Pathology & diagnosis

Nodular glomerulosclerosis and arteriolosclerosis of afferent & efferent arterioles are pathognomonic; DN as cause of CKD: ≥10 years DM, normal sized kidneys, retinopathy, benign sediment.

✓
Treatment

Tight control of glycemia, BP (<130/80 without, <125/75 with proteinuria), UTI and protein intake (0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day); RRT early at GFR <20 ml/min; reduce insulin dose in renal impairment.

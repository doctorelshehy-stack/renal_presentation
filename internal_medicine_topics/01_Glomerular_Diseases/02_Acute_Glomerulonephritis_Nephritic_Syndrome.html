Definition

What is Acute G.N.?

Definition

Acute G.N. is a clinical syndrome characterized by acute onset of:

  1. Hematuria (macro or microscopic).
  2. Proteinuria (<3.5 gm/d/1.73 m2 s.a).
  3. Hypertension.
  4. Edema.
  5. Oliguria (<300 ml/day) in severe cases.

Part 2 — Clinical Features

Clinical Features

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

1. Hematuria

Due to damage of glomerular capillary walls. It may be:

MacroscopicMicroscopic
Color of urine depends on pH: red urine in alkaline urine; smoky (or reddish brown) urine in acidic urine — due to denaturation of Hb with formation of acid haematine and prolonged transit time through the nephron. Formation of methemoglobin gives the smoky color of urine. Presence of dysmorphic red cells: small & distorted due to mechanical injury of RBCs passing through the damaged glomerular capillary walls; have low Hb content. Best seen by phase contrast microscopy.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

2. Proteinuria

Characteristics

  • Glomerular origin (consists mainly of albumin).
  • Non-nephrotic range: <3.5 gm/day.

It is due to

  • Increase of glomerular capillary permeability.
  • Mechanical disruption of glomerular capillary walls.
  • Altered glomerular hemodynamics.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

3. Edema & 4. Hypertension

3. Edema

  • Early → eyelids (puffiness), as this area is of low tissue pressure.
  • Then progresses to involve the legs and other parts.
  • Due to salt and water retention because of:
    • Increased reabsorption of filtered Na+ at the distal tubules.
    • Decreased GFR → decreased salt delivery to tubules.

4. Hypertension

Due to salt & water retention, i.e. volume dependent.

Clinical features

5. Oliguria & Associated Disease

5. Oliguria

Urine output <300 ml/day — due to decreased GFR.

B. Associated specific disease

Manifestations of the associated specific disease, e.g. infection (PSGN), SLE, vasculitis, malignancy.

Part 3 — Investigations

Investigations

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

1. Urine Examination

  • Color → red or smoky (dark brown).
  • Oliguria: <300 ml/day in severe cases.
  • Proteinuria <3.5 gm/day.
  • Microscopic examination:
    • Hematuria.
    • Dysmorphic red cells & red cell casts (both are pathognomonic for acute GN).
    • Granular casts.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Blood, Imaging & Renal Biopsy

2. Blood

Urea, creatinine & GFR: variable impairment of renal function.

3. Renal imaging

Usually unnecessary.

4. Renal biopsy

Most of the renal pathology associated with acute GN is proliferative, either focal or diffuse.

Indications:

  • A. Unusual clinical features.
  • B. Uncertain diagnosis.
  • C. Rapid deterioration of renal function → crescentic GN.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

5. Specific Investigations

Needed for etiological diagnosis

  • ANA, ds DNA → SLE.
  • Throat swab & ASOT and transient decrease of C3 (<8 wks) → PSGN.

Treatment — General

General Treatment & Monitoring

1. General treatment: to control symptoms and prevent complications — fluid chart (fluid intake and output), measurement of body weight and blood pressure.

Home treatmentHospital admission
For uncomplicated casesFor complicated cases: severe hypertension, renal failure & oliguria, pulmonary edema, encephalopathy
Daily follow-up of blood pressureUntil hematuria, HTN & edema disappear, and proteinuria decreased
Urea and creatinine every few daysStrict bed rest in complicated cases, e.g. severe hypertension, pulmonary edema
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — General

Diet & Drug Measures

Restrictions

  • Salt restriction.
  • Fluid restriction in oliguric patients (1/2 L + volume of previous day urine).
  • Protein restriction in renal failure (0.6–0.8 gm/kg/day = about 40 gm/day for an adult patient).

e.g. Hypertension & edema

  • Salt restriction.
  • Diuretics: loop diuretic.
  • Antihypertensive drugs (BB should be used with caution, as it may precipitate pulmonary edema in patients with impending H.F.).
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — Complications

Management of Complications

A. Hypertensive encephalopathy

  • Maintain airway.
  • Control blood pressure by parenteral agent, such as hydralazine 5–20 mg infusion.
  • Control of fits: IV diazepam (10 mg).

B. Pulmonary edema / H.F.

  • Salt and water restriction.
  • Ultrafiltration (dialysis) to remove excess fluid in oliguric patient.
  • Acute R.F.: dialysis.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
The syndrome

Acute GN is a clinical syndrome of acute onset hematuria, proteinuria (<3.5 gm/day), hypertension, edema and oliguria (<300 ml/day in severe cases).

✓
Pathognomonic findings

Dysmorphic red cells and red cell casts are pathognomonic for acute GN; HTN and edema are volume dependent (salt & water retention).

✓
Renal biopsy

Indicated with unusual clinical features, uncertain diagnosis, or rapid deterioration of renal function → crescentic GN.

✓
Treatment

General (bed rest, salt / fluid / protein restriction, diuretics) + treatment of the cause; hypertensive encephalopathy needs parenteral hydralazine 5–20 mg infusion.

Part 2 — Clinical Features

Clinical Features

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

History of Streptococcal Infection

History of streptococcal infection (tonsillitis, pharyngitis, O.M., or cellulitis): 1–2 weeks before the onset of acute nephritis. In skin infection, the latent period may be prolonged to 4 weeks.

ℹ
Latent period

The time between exposure to infection and development of acute nephritis — reflects the time taken for I.C. formation & deposition & glomerular injury. A shorter latent period signifies exacerbation of underlying CKD (e.g. IgA nephropathy) rather than de novo acute G.N.

Clinical features

Epidemiological Notes

✓
Key points
  • About 10% of infected will develop GN.
  • Infection may be mild and pass unnoticed.
  • No relationship between the severity of infection & the probability of development of acute nephritis.

Part 3 — Investigations

Investigations

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Evidence of Streptococcal Infection

  • Antibody to streptococcal exo-enzymes (SO, DNAase, BNDase and hyalurenidase).
  • Decrease of CH50 & C3 during the acute phase, then returns to normal within 8 weeks of nephritis (i.e. transient hypo-complementemia).
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Evidence of Acute GN

Urine & blood

  • Urine exam: dysmorphic red cells, red cell casts & proteinuria (<3 gm/day).
  • Blood: transient increase of S. cholesterol (in NS), ↓ S. albumin, renal impairment.

Renal biopsy

  • LM: diffuse endocapillary proliferative GN ± few crescents.
  • EM: subepithelial electron-dense deposits (humps) and variable degrees of mesangial deposits.
  • IF: granular deposit of C3 & IgG.

Part 4 — Treatment

Treatment

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

General / Supportive / Symptomatic

See treatment of GN.

  1. Bed rest.
  2. Diet: salt, fluid and protein restriction.
  3. Drugs:
    • Diuretics.
    • Vasodilators for control of blood pressure.
    • Steroid, cytotoxic drugs and anticoagulants are of no value and may be harmful — except in RPGN.
  4. Dialysis for R.F.
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — Specific

Antibiotic (Penicillin) Therapy — for Patients

Aim

  • Decrease antigen load → decrease I.C. formation → may stop progression of the disease.
  • Halt the spread of potentially nephritogenic streptococci from the patient to family members in close contact.
✎
N.B.

Such treatment, however, does not seem to be very effective in aborting or ameliorating the course of the disease.

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — Prophylaxis

Close Contacts & N.B.

For close contact

Short-term penicillin prophylaxis (phenoxy penicillin 500 mg/day) to all individuals at high risk in a closed community or family.

✎
N.B.

No effect of long-term penicillin in prophylaxis after the development of GN. Removal of the infected tonsil or the septic foci should be delayed until convalescence is advanced, as the operation may be followed by exacerbation of the disease; however, if needed, give benzyl penicillin on the day of operation and three days after.

Summary

Key Takeaways

✓
Organism

Group A β-hemolytic streptococci, nephritogenic types 1, 4, 12, 49 — type 49 is the most commonly isolated.

✓
Latent period

1–2 weeks after throat infection (up to 4 weeks after skin infection); a shorter latent period suggests exacerbation of underlying CKD, not de novo GN.

✓
Transient hypocomplementemia

CH50 & C3 decrease during the acute phase and return to normal within 8 weeks; biopsy shows endocapillary proliferative GN, subepithelial humps (EM) and granular C3 & IgG (IF).

✓
Treatment

Supportive care; steroids / cytotoxics / anticoagulants are of no value except in RPGN; penicillin for patients and phenoxy penicillin 500 mg/day prophylaxis for close contacts.

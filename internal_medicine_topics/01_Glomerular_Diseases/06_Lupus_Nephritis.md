# Lupus Nephritis (LN)

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definition:

L.N is the renal involvement in patients with SLE. It exists with or without clinical manifestations of SLE. It is extremely diverse in its presentation and pathology.

---

#### Prevalence:

The kidney is the most common organ involvement in SLE. Clinically, about 50 % of lupus patients have L.N at diagnosis.

---

#### Pathology:

1. The glomeruli are essentially involved which may show;
   - **Haematoxylin bodies** seen as rounded bluish inclusions in Hx & E stained section. They represent the naked nuclei that have been altered by binding to ANA resulting in clumping of nuclear chromatin. They are seen in only 2 % of cases and are pathognomonic of active L.N.
   - **Wire loop deposits** are subendothelial immune deposits which encircle entire gl. tuft circumference. They are best seen in trichrome & silver stained sections.
   - **Hyaline thrombi** are massive subendothelial immune deposits that protrude into or occlude the glomerular tuft lumina (i.e. intraluminal immune deposits).

2. Tubulo-interstitial disease is seen in 50% of cases especially proliferative LN.

---

#### Pathological classes:

6 Major classes based upon LM, IF, and EM studies of renal biopsy according to the International Society of Nephrology / Renal pathology Society "ISN/RPS" classification.

| Class | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Class I | Minimal mesangial LN | normal glomeruli by LM and mesangial immune deposits by IF or EM. |
| Class II | Mesangial proliferative LN | Mesangial hypercellularity and/or matrix expansion of any degree in addition to mesangial immune deposits. |
| Class III | Focal proliferative LN | <50% of glomeruli are involved. |
| Class IV | Diffuse proliferative LN | > 50% of glomeruli are involved. |
| Class V | Membranous LN | Diffuse (global) glomerular capillary wall thickness plus continuous subepithelial immune deposits. |
| Class VI | Advanced-stage LN | Global glomeruloscelerosis of ≥90% of the total glomeruli, without evidence of active lesion. |

---

#### Clinical Features:

**N.B**
- Asymptomatic in many patients.
- Symptomatic: manifested as;
  - Hypertension; a clue of renal disease.
  - Symptoms and signs of; acute nephritis, NS, or rapidly progressive R.F., and ESRD.

---

#### Diagnosis:

**• Clinical:** Hypertension and/or renal edema.

**Laboratory findings:**
- Urine Exam (red cells, casts, proteinuria)
- Elevated serum creatinine level.

**• Renal biopsy:** useful in determining initial management, every patient suspected of microangiopathy may having L.N. require a biopsy to:
A. Determine the nature of the renal pathology and pathological class.
B. Exclude other lesions that may complicate the course of SLE such as; hypersensitivity interstitial nephritis when the patient develops ATN, and pure antiphospholipid $.
C. Assessment of the activity index "0-24 score" (represent acute and potentially treatable lesions) and chronicity index "0-12 score" (irreversible lesions) that determine therapy & prognosis.

---

#### Management:

Principle goal in LN is to normalize renal function & prevent loss of renal function.

**I- General treatment:** to treat extra-renal manifestations that may affect kidney:

| Measure | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Hydroxychloroquine | or an equivalent antimalarial to all patients with LN unless contraindicated. |
| control of hypertension | Target blood pressure is ≤ 120/80 mmHg |
| Control of proteinuria | low doses of ACEIs or ARBs & protein restriction |
| Control of hyperlipidemia | use statins and low fat diet. |
| Avoid nephrotoxic drugs | e.g. NSAIDs. Non-acetylated salicylates is safe |
| Avoid pregnancy in patients with active LN | it may worsen their renal disease. Moreover, certain medications may be teratogenic. |

**II- Specific treatment:** Corticosteroids and immunosuppressive treatment of LN is divided into induction and maintenance phases.

- Corticosteroids should be instituted if the patient has clinically significant renal disease.
- Immunosuppressive agents (cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine, or cyclosporine A)

---

#### Prognosis:

- Class I, II and V; have a generally good prognosis.
- Class III and IV; have poor prognosis as they tend to progress to ESRD, particularly class IV.
- Class VI; HD or transplantation.

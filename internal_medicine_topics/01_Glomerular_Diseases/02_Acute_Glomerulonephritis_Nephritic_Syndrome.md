# Acute Glomerulonephritis (GN) — Nephritic Syndrome

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definition:

Acute G.N. is a clinical syndrome characterized by acute onset of:

1- Hematuria (Macro or Microscopic)
2- Proteinuria (<3.5 gm/d/1.73 m2 s.a)
3- Hypertension
4- Edema
5- Oliguria (<300 ml/day) in severe cases.

---

#### Clinical Features:

**A. Manifestations of acute nephritis:**

**1. Hematuria:** Due to damage of glomerular capillary walls. It may be:

| Macroscopic | Microscopic |
|---|---|
| • Color of urine depends on pH: Red color urine (in alkaline urine). Smoky (or reddish brown) urine in acidic urine, due to denaturation of Hb with formation of acid haematine and prolonged transit time through the nephron. Formation of methemoglobin gives the smoky color of urine. | • Characterized by presence of dysmorphic red cells: Small & distorted due to mechanical injury of RBCs passing through the damaged glomerular capillary walls. Have low Hb content. Best seen by phase contrast microscopy. |

**2. Proteinuria:**
- glomerular origin (consists mainly of albumin).
- non-nephrotic range < 3.5 gm/day
- It is due to:
  - Increase glomerular capillary permeability.
  - Mechanical disruption of glomerular capillary walls.
  - Altered glomerular hemodynamics.

**3. Edema:**
- Early → eyelids (puffiness) as this area is of low tissue pressure.
- Then progress to involve the legs and other parts.
- It is due to salt and water retention because of;
  - Increase reabsorption of filtered Na+ at the distal tubules.
  - Decrease GFR → decrease salt delivery to tubules.

**4. Hypertension:** Due to salt & water retention i.e. volume dependent.

**5. Oliguria:** (urine output <300 ml/day) Due to decreased GFR

**B. Manifestations of the associated specific disease,** e.g.; Infection (PSGN), SLE, vasculitis, malignancy.

---

#### Investigations:

**1. Urine examination:**
- Color→ red or smoky (dark brown).
- Oliguria: < 300 ml/day in severe cases.
- Proteinuria < 3.5 gm/day.
- Microscopic examination:
  - Hematuria.
  - Dysmorphic red cells & Red cell casts (both are pathognomonic for acute GN)
  - Granular casts.

**2. Blood:** (Urea, Creatinine & GFR) Variable impairment of renal function.

**3. Renal imaging:** Usually unnecessary.

**4. Renal biopsy:**
Most of renal pathology associated with acute GN are proliferative either focal or diffuse.
Indications for renal biopsy:
A. Unusual clinical features.
B. Uncertain diagnosis.
C. Rapid deterioration of renal function → crescentic GN.

**5. Specific investigations:** Needed for etiological diagnosis. e.g.;
- ANA, ds DNA for SLE
- Throat swab & ASOT and transient decrease of C3 (< 8ws) for PSGN.

---

#### Treatment:

**1. General treatment:** To control symptoms and prevent complications.
- Fluid chart (i.e. Fluid intake and output).
- Measurement of body weight and blood pressure.

| Home treatment | Hospital admission |
|---|---|
| For uncomplicated cases | For complicated cases; severe hypertension, |
| Daily follow up of bl. pressure. | Renal failure & oliguria |
| Urea and creatinine every few days. | pulmonary edema |
| | encephalopathy |
| | Until hematuria, HTN & edema disappear, & proteinuria decreased. |
| | Strict bed rest in complicated cases e.g. severe hypertension, pulmonary edema. |

- Salt restriction.
- Fluid restriction in oliguric patients (1/2 L + volume of previous day urine).
- Protein restriction in renal failure (0.6 - 0.8 gm/kg/day = about 40 gm/day for adult patient).

**e.g. Hypertension & edema:**
- Salt restriction.
- Diuretics: loop diuretic.
- Antihypertensive drugs (BB should be used with caution, as it may precipitate pulmonary edema in patients with impending HF.

**A. Hypertensive encephalopathy;**
- Maintain air way.
- Control bl. pressure by parenteral agent such as hydralazine 5-20 mg infusion.
- Control of fits, IV diazepam (10 mg).

**B. Pulmonary Edema/ HF:**
- Salt and water restriction
- Ultrafiltration (dialysis) to remove excess fluid in oliguric patient.
- Acute RF: Dialysis

**6. Specific treatment:** Depend upon the cause.

| primary GN | secondary GN |
|---|---|
| corticosteroid ± immunesuppressives depending on histopathological type (see the appropriate section). | treat the cause e.g.; PSGN, diabetic nephropathy, Lupus nephritis (see appropriate section). |

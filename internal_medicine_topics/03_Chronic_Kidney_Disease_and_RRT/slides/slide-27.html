Treatment — Mineral Bone Disease (1)

Mineral Bone Disease (MBD) — Control of hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, vitamin D deficiency and secondary hyperparathyroidism

Diet Control

  • 1. Increased elementary dietary calcium to 1500–2000 mg/day (hold if there is hypercalcemia).
  • 2. Low phosphate diet (800–1000 mg/day) adjusted by requirement of protein in grades 3–5 CKD patients.

Phosphate Binders — indicated when dietary phosphate restriction is insufficient to control phosphate and PTH levels

  • Calcium-based phosphate binders; e.g. Ca carbonate, given orally during meals.
  • A. Decreases serum phosphate level effectively and can be used for initial phosphate binding treatment.
  • B. Long-term administration can result in hypercalcemia.
  • C. Should not be used in dialysis patients with hypercalcemia and in patients with plasma PTH level < 130 pg/dL. In these conditions use calcium-free phosphate binders.
  • Non-calcium-based phosphate binders; Sevelamer (Renagel) hydrochloride or bicarbonate — safe and effective but expensive.
  • Lanthanum carbonate.

Definitions & Markers

Definitions and Markers of Kidney Damage

Definitions

CKD: abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for > 3 months with implications for health; classified based on cause, GFR category and albuminuria category.

Chronic renal failure (CRF): GFR < 60 ml/min/m² for ≥ 3 months (Grade 3 CKD).

End-stage renal disease (ESRD): a permanent and irreversible renal impairment which necessitates renal replacement therapy = Grade 5 CKD.

Functional markers

  • Albuminuria — albumin excretion rate (AER) ≥ 30 mg/24 h, or albumin creatinine ratio (ACR) ≥ 30 mg/g
  • Urine sediment abnormalities
  • Electrolytes and other abnormalities due to tubular disorders

Structural markers

  • Structural abnormalities detected by histology
  • Structural abnormalities detected by imaging
  • History of kidney transplantation

Causes

Causes of CKD

Causes (1/2)

  1. Diabetes mellitus
  2. Hypertension
  3. Glomerulopathies either primary or secondary
  4. Tubulo-interstitial diseases
  5. Renal vascular diseases (renal artery stenosis)

Causes (2/2)

  1. Hereditary renal diseases e.g. PCKD, Alport's syndrome, oxalosis
  2. Obstructive uropathy e.g. urolithiasis, prostatic enlargement, tumors, retroperitoneal fibrosis
  3. Recurrent UTI (pyelonephritis, vesicoureteral reflux)
  4. Drugs and toxins — NSAID, chronic lead poisoning
  5. Uncertain (in up to 15% of cases)

Section 03 — Clinical Features & Complications

Clinical Features & Complications

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

Clinical Features — GI & Respiratory

CKD leads to disturbances in the function of every system in the body. The presentation and severity of signs and symptoms vary greatly from patient to patient depending on the grade/severity of disease.

ℹ
When symptomatic?

Grades 1-3 CKD are usually asymptomatic; grades 4 & 5 are symptomatic.

Gastrointestinal

  • Anorexia, nausea, vomiting & hiccough (early & controlled by protein restriction)
  • Uremic fetor — uriniferous odor of breath (breakdown of urea to ammonia)
  • Mucosal ulcerations (in late stages) → GIT bleeding
  • Peptic ulcer — increased gastric acidity, gastrin secretion, secondary hyperparathyroidism
  • Increased incidence of diverticulosis (in patients with PCKD)
  • NB: GIT symptoms are improved by dialysis except peptic ulcers and diverticulosis.

Respiratory

  • Acidotic breathing
  • Pleurisy
  • Chest infection

Clinical features

Clinical Features — Cardiovascular

  • CHF and pulmonary edema — dyspnea, orthopnea, increased jugular venous pressure, lower limb edema due to volume overload
  • Arrhythmias — due to electrolyte abnormalities
  • Hypertension
  • Pericarditis and pericardial effusion
  • Accelerated atherosclerosis
✎
NB

Absence of hypertension in a patient with CKD may be due to: salt-wasting nephropathy (e.g. PCKD), use of anti-hypertensive drugs, or volume depletion (e.g. GIT loss or diuretic overdose).

Clinical features

Clinical Features — Hematological

Anemia → easy fatigability, palpitation & tachycardia

  • Erythropoietin deficiency
  • Decreased life span of RBCs and toxic bone marrow suppression
  • Decreased nutrient intake and absorption — iron, vitamin B12, folate
  • Bleeding tendency
  • Secondary hyperparathyroidism
  • Chronic inflammation
  • Blood loss, during dialysis and from GIT

Bleeding diathesis

Due to:

  • Prolongation of bleeding time
  • Decreased factor III activity
  • Abnormal platelet aggregation and adhesion
  • Impaired prothrombin consumption

Enhanced susceptibility to infection

  • Impaired leukocyte formation and function — due to acidosis, hyperglycemia, hyperosmolarity of serum and tissues by azotemia, and malnutrition
  • Defective mucosal barriers
  • Use of steroids and immunosuppressive drugs
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

Endocrine & Metabolic Disturbances

  • Insulin resistance and impaired glucose tolerance
  • Decreased insulin metabolism and degradation by the diseased kidney → decreased insulin requirement for diabetic patients
  • Thyroid dysfunction — increased TRH → release of prolactin with impaired prolactin clearance → galactorrhea, amenorrhea, and impotence
  • Hypocalcemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism
  • Decreased serum testosterone level → impotence, decreased spermatogenesis and infertility
  • Menstrual abnormalities (oligomenorrhea, amenorrhea) and infertility
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features

Neurological Features

CNS

  • Early: inability to concentrate, loss of memory, drowsiness, insomnia, hiccups, cramps and muscle twitching
  • Late: asterixis, myoclonus, chorea, stupor, seizures and coma

Peripheral nerves

  • Sensory (early) and motor neuropathy
  • Restless legs syndrome — discomfort in the feet and legs with frequent leg movement; treatment is early dialysis or transplantation

In dialysis patients

  • Dialysis dementia (encephalopathy)
  • Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome — nausea, vomiting, drowsiness, headache, seizures; occurs in the first few sessions of dialysis; due to rapid removal of urea with rapid reduction of osmolality of the extracellular compartment within the cranium → cerebral edema and increased intracranial pressure

Clinical features

Dermatological Features

Earthy (muddy) face

Combination of anemia and retention of urochrome pigment.

Pallor

Due to anemia.

Ecchymosis & hematomas

Due to defective homeostasis.

Pruritus

Due to calcium deposition and secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Poor skin turgor

Dry mucous membranes due to dehydration.

Malnutrition

Evidence of malnutrition.

Uremic frost

In advanced cases — a fine white powder due to high urea concentration in sweat which precipitates on the skin after evaporation.

Slate-gray-bronze skin

Hemochromatosis due to multiple blood transfusions in dialysis patients.

Half and half nails

Characteristic nail change.

Calciphylaxis

Calcific uremic arteriolopathy — vascular calcification of skin, muscles and subcutaneous tissues; violaceous rashes, nonhealing ulcers and gangrene → high mortality.

✎
NB

Most skin abnormalities improve with dialysis except pruritus, which persists.

Clinical features

Urinary & Electrolyte Disturbances

Urinary system

  • UTI
  • Urine volume:
    • Early — polyuria and nocturia
    • Late — oliguria and anuria

Electrolytes & acid-base disturbances

  • Hyperkalemia → muscle weakness, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest
  • Hypocalcemia → tetany is rare as acidosis increases ionized calcium
  • Hyperphosphatemia → itching, metastatic calcification
  • Metabolic acidosis → rapid deep breathing, convulsions
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Clinical features — bone

CKD-MBD (Mineral Bone Disease)

Definition

A systemic disorder of mineral and bone metabolism due to CKD, manifested by either one or a combination of the following:

  • Abnormalities of calcium, phosphorus, PTH, or vitamin D metabolism
  • Abnormalities in bone turnover, mineralization, volume, linear growth or strength (renal osteodystrophy)
  • Vascular or other soft tissue calcification e.g. cardiac valve, arterial calcification and calciphylaxis
  1. Reduction of GFR < 60 ml/min → decreased phosphorus filtration → hyperphosphatemia → stimulation of PTH secretion.
  2. Decreased renal production of one alpha hydroxylase enzyme → decreased production of active vitamin D3 "1.25 (OH)2 vit. D" → hypocalcemia → in addition to hyperphosphatemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT).

Figure 10 flow

↓ Renal mass → Pi retention → abnormal PT growth and function → ↓ calcitriol → skeletal resistance → hypocalcemia → hyperparathyroidism.

Clinical features — bone

Renal Osteodystrophy

Definition

The skeletal component of the systemic disorder of mineral bone disease (MBD) that occurs in patients with CKD. Patients may suffer from bone pain, pathological (spontaneous) fractures, and bone changes in bone imaging and bone biopsy.

Osteitis fibrosa

High-turnover bone disease — a manifestation of hyperparathyroidism: increased osteoclast and osteoblast activity (turnover) and peritrabecular fibrosis.

Osteomalacia

Abnormally low bone turnover.

Adynamic bone disease

Decreased rates of bone formation with normal bone mineralization; due to aggressive suppression of PTH leading to hypoparathyroidism.

Osteopenia / osteoporosis

Increased skeletal fragility due to reduced bone quantity and quality.

Mixed lesions

High PTH levels + impaired bone formation and mineralization; usually in patients with previously established hyperparathyroid bone disease who develop aluminum-related bone disease.

Section 04 — Investigations

Investigations

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Investigations (1/2) — Degree of Renal Impairment

A. To establish the degree of renal impairment and complications

  1. Urine analysis with evaluation of albuminuria, broad casts, and low fixed specific gravity
  2. Kidney function tests — urea, creatinine and eGFR
  3. Serum electrolytes — sodium, potassium, calcium and phosphorus
  4. Arterial blood gases
  5. Serum uric acid
  6. Complete blood count
  7. Iron profile
  8. iPTH level
  9. Skeletal survey
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Investigations

Investigations (2/2) — Underlying Disease & Acute-on-CKD

B. To determine the underlying disease (if the cause is not clear)

  • Serum and urine protein electrophoresis
  • ANA, anti-ds DNA ab, C3 & C4, ANCA, AGBM ab
  • Hepatitis C and B and HIV serology
  • Renal imaging — kidney ureter bladder X-ray (KUB), ultrasound, CT, MRI, radionuclide scan (renogram)
  • Renal biopsy — not indicated if there is a small atrophic kidney

C. To diagnose a patient with acute RF on top of CKD

  • Bone disease
  • Uremic neuropathy
  • Profound anemia, hyperphosphatemia, and high serum creatinine with mild symptoms
  • Small-sized kidney except in DM, amyloidosis, multiple myeloma and PCKD

Section 05 — Treatment

Treatment

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — conservative

Conservative Treatment — Correctable Causes

  1. Correction of any reversible cause and underlying disease
  2. Control of symptoms
  3. Educational programs to the patients and their relatives

Correction of any reversible cause (to delay CKD progression) — (1/2)

  1. Good blood glucose control
  2. Good blood pressure control
  3. Good hydration
  4. Correction of renal obstruction and UTI
  5. Treatment of active primary renal disease

(2/2)

  1. Treatment of sepsis
  2. Reduction of proteinuria (ACEI/ARBs — avoid combination)
  3. Control of dyslipidemia
  4. Lifestyle modification — diet, weight reduction, smoking & alcohol cessation, exercise
  5. Avoid nephrotoxins and large doses of contrast agents
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — symptom control

Diet, GIT & CVS

Diet

(Low protein, potassium, phosphate, and salts) + fluid balance.

  • Low protein diet (0.6-0.8 gm/kg/day); protein intake should be increased when the patient starts dialysis
  • Low potassium diet (if the patient is hypokalemic, increase potassium diet)
  • Low phosphate diet
  • Salt restriction (free salt intake in salt-losing nephropathy)
  • Fluid balance — depends on urine output

GIT

  • Hiccough and vomiting → prokinetics; if persist → dialysis
  • Gastritis — proton pump inhibitors (avoid long-term use as may cause interstitial nephropathy)

CVS

  • Control of hypertension — ACEI/ARBs, CCB, BB
  • ACEI or ARBs preferred if there is proteinuria, with careful monitoring of serum creatinine and potassium levels
  • Volume overload → loop diuretics
  • Pericarditis → dialysis
  • Correction of electrolyte and acid-base disorders (see chapter V)
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment — mineral bone disease

CKD-MBD Treatment (1/2) — Diet & Phosphate Binders

Control of hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, vitamin D deficiency and secondary hyperparathyroidism. Phosphate binders are indicated when dietary phosphate restriction is insufficient to control phosphate and PTH levels.

Diet control

  • Increased elementary dietary calcium to 1500-2000 mg/day (hold if there is hypercalcemia)
  • Low phosphate diet (800-1000 mg/day) adjusted by requirement in protein in grades 3-5 CKD patients

Calcium-based phosphate binders

e.g. Ca carbonate, given orally during meals.

  • A. Decreases the serum phosphate level effectively — can be used for initial phosphate binding treatment
  • B. Long-term administration can result in hypercalcemia
  • C. Should not be used in dialysis patients with hypercalcemia and in patients with plasma PTH level < 130 pg/dL — in these conditions use calcium-free phosphate binders

Non-calcium-based phosphate binders

  • Sevelamer (Renalgel) hydrochloride or bicarbonate — safe and effective but expensive
  • Lanthanum carbonate

Treatment — mineral bone disease

CKD-MBD Treatment (2/2) — Hormonal & Surgical

Calcimimetic agents

  • Cinacalcet — inhibits iPTH secretion

Vitamin D analog

Alfacalcidol/calcitriol; 0.25-2 µg daily according to serum Ca, PO4 and iPTH levels. Hold if there is hypercalcemia and/or hyperphosphatemia.

Recommended for renal patients with:

  • Total serum calcium level < 9.5 mg/dl
  • Serum phosphate level < 4.6 mg/dl
  • Elevated serum PTH level

Parathyroidectomy

  • In severe hyperparathyroidism (PTH > 800 pg/dl) not responding to medications

Treatment — symptom control

Management of Anemia & Pruritus

Management of anemia

  • Iron therapy — oral in pre-dialysis patients and better I.V. for HD patients
  • Erythropoietin — subcutaneous or intravenous:
    • Erythropoietin alpha or beta
    • Long-acting darbepoetin alfa
    • Continuous erythropoiesis receptor activator (CERA)

Pruritus

  • Moistening ointment
  • Gabapentin, Naltrexone
  • Short-wave ultraviolet
  • Increase dose of dialysis
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Treatment

Educational Program & Renal Replacement Therapy

Educational program

  • Explain the possibility of eventual renal failure and forms of therapy
  • In patients planning for HD → preparation of A-V fistula
  • In patients planning for PD or transplantation → early education of family members for selection and preparation of a home dialysis helper or a related donor
ℹ
II) Renal replacement therapy

Dialysis and renal transplantation.

Closing

Key Takeaways

✓
Definition & classification

CKD = abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for > 3 months; classified by cause, GFR category (G1-G5) and albuminuria category (A1-A3). CRF = GFR < 60 ml/min/m² for ≥ 3 months (Grade 3); ESRD = Grade 5, requiring renal replacement therapy.

✓
Clinical presentation

Grades 1-3 are usually asymptomatic; grades 4 & 5 are symptomatic. Uremia disturbs every system — GI, cardiovascular, hematological, endocrine, bone, neurological and skin.

✓
CKD-MBD

↓ Phosphorus filtration → hyperphosphatemia and ↓ calcitriol → hypocalcemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism. Renal osteodystrophy: osteitis fibrosa, osteomalacia, adynamic bone disease, osteopenia/osteoporosis and mixed lesions.

✓
Treatment

Correct reversible causes (glycemia, BP, proteinuria with ACEI/ARB), control symptoms (diet, GIT, CVS), manage MBD and anemia, and offer RRT (dialysis and transplantation) for ESRD.

**Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT)**

## A. Dialysis

#### Definition:

Dialysis is a process of diffusion of substances and water across a semipermeable membrane in both directions (blood in one side and fluid "dialysate" on the other side of the membrane).

#### Indications:

**I) Temporary (acute / urgent) dialysis:**

1- In AKI: Many nephrologists advices early dialysis in AKI in all patients at least for some periods of times to simplifies management which enhances recovery and minimizes development of complications.

2- Control of some uremic symptoms and complications (in both AKI and CKD) as;
1. Refractory hyperkalemia
2. Intractable metabolic acidosis
3. Progressive fluid overload unresponsive to diuretics
4. Pericarditis and pleuritis.
5. CNS complications.
6. GIT complications.
7. Bleeding due to uremia.

3- Removal dialyzable toxins & drugs as; Alcohols, Aspirin, Lithium, contrast dyes

**II) Permanent (chronic, regular or maintenance) dialysis:**
Grade 5 CKD (ESRD) as a replacement therapy when GFR ≤ 10 ml/min which may correspond to a serum creatinine of 8-10 mg/dL.

#### Contraindications:
- Refractory hypotension unresponsive to vasopressors.
- Terminal illness.
- Organic brain damage.

#### Types: Peritoneal dialysis (PD) and Hemodialysis (HD).

---

### 1. Peritoneal Dialysis (PD)

#### Principles:
Peritoneal dialysis utilizes the peritoneal membrane (with its capillaries) as a semipermeable membrane. When fluid is introduced into the peritoneal cavity it will equilibrate with the extracellular fluid and removal of the introduced fluid (effluent) will result in removal of waste products (solutes) and of excess fluid from the body

#### Indications:
all patients with symptomatic uremia. However, the following are specific indications:
1. A slowly rising urea.
2. Patients with cardiovascular or hemodynamic instability
3. Absence of H.D. facilities.
4. Patients with immature A-V fistula
5. Poor vascular access
6. High risk of anticoagulation in a dialysis dependent patient

#### Contraindications:

**Absolute contraindications:**
1. Peritoneal fibrosis (> 50%) inadequate peritoneal surface area for dialysis.
2. Pleuro-peritoneal leak → hydrothorax
3. Multiple large abdominal wounds with drains

**Relative contraindications:**
1. Abdominal problems; colostomy, nephrostomy, recent abdominal or thoracic surgery, extensive abdominal adhesions, hernia, abdominal malignancy.
2. Severe hyper catabolic states e.g. burn
3. Fresh or infected aortic prosthesis
4. Huge polycystic kidneys (insufficient intraperitoneal space)
5. Active diverticulosis
6. Morbid obesity (inadequate clearance)
7. Hyperlipidemia
8. Severe gastroparesis (worsening vomiting)
9. For self-care (home dialysis) patient;

#### Advantages of P.D.:
1. Safe and easy to perform
2. Preserve residual renal function
3. No routine anticoagulation
4. Avoidance of vascular surgery
5. Slow clearance rate → fewer dialysis-related symptoms e.g. disequilibrium or hypotension.
6. Better control of PTH
7. More liberal diet
8. Fewer medications
9. Less expensive than HD
10. Less risk of transmission of blood born viruses

#### Disadvantages:
1. Long time
2. Low efficiency
3. Inadequate clearance in some patients e.g. malignant hypertension, peritoneal disease, vasculitis
4. Contraindicated in recent abdominal surgery or pulmonary compromise.
5. Associated complications

#### Complications:
1. Peritonitis
2. Catheter exits infection
3. Constipation
4. Leakage of dialysate through a diaphragmatic defect into the thoracic cavity → Massive pleural effusion and respiratory embarrassment or to scrotum.
5. Failure of PM function → ultrafiltration failure
6. Sclerosing peritonitis (rare but potentially fatal)
7. Moderate protein loss → malnutrition
8. Hyperlipidemia → accelerated atherosclerosis.
9. Obesity due to mobilization of glucose from the dialysate to the body
10. Inguinal and abdominal hernias
11. Traumatic perforation of an abdominal organ (bowel, bladder, bl. Vessel)
12. Clotting of the cannula.

### 2. Hemodialysis (HD)

#### Definition:
HD is a process of diffusion across a semipermeable membrane, to remove unwanted substance from blood, while adding desirable components.

#### Advantages of HD:
1. Relatively short time causing minimal interruption of life style between treatment.
2. More efficient than PD.
3. Can be performed in the home.

#### Complications of HD:

**I- Surgical; related to vascular access.**
**II- Medical (directly related to HD); acute and chronic.**

**I) Surgical complications:**

**Of Catheters:**
Bleeding, infection, thrombosis or stenosis of the vessels, pneumothorax and air embolism.

**Of A-V fistula and of graft:**
1. Stenosis → inadequate blood flow.
2. Thrombosis
3. Infection
4. Failure to develop adequate venous outflow
5. Vascular steal → limb ischemia
6. Venous hypertension syndrome (distal venous stasis → skin necrosis)
7. High outflow heart failure
8. Pseudo-aneurysms.

**II) Medical complications: (acute and chronic);**

**Acute Complications:**
1- Intradialytic hypotension (IDH).
2- Hypertension.
3- Cramps.
4- Nausea and vomiting.
5- Headache.
6- Fever
7- Hemolysis: Rare, but potentially serious.
8- Bleeding: Anticoagulant overdose.
9- Clotting of extracorporeal circuit (blood lines and dialyzer)
10- Chest pain.
11- Arrhythmias
12- Cardiac arrest in the dialysis unit.
13- Air embolism.
14- Disequilibrium Syndrome.

**Chronic (Long-term) HD complications:**

1- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Dialysis patients are more likely to die from CV disease than the general population. The cause is multifactorial; cardiac or vascular.
   - **Cardiac:**
     1. Ischemic heart disease
     2. Valvular heart disease (aortic and mitral valves)
     3. Uremic pericarditis and pericardial effusion
     4. Uremic endocarditis
     5. Cardiomyopathy (dilated)
   - **Vascular:**
     1. Peripheral vascular disease due to;
        - Decreased peripheral blood flow, related to vascular access.
        - Hypertension and dyslipidemia.
     2. Metastatic calcification;
        - Abnormal calcification of soft tissues due to persistent elevation of plasma phosphate and calcium.

2- **Neuropsychiatric complications:** Anxiety, depression, dementia
3- **Malnutrition.**
4- **Dialysis osteomalicia**
5- **Dialysis related amyloidosis (DRA).**
6- **Rheumatologic complications:**
   1. Crystal induced arthritis
   2. Infection of joints and bone
   3. Ischemic necrosis of bone
7- **Hypersplenism**
8- **Acquired renal cystic disease;**
   - In patients with prolonged dialysis > 3 years
   - May show malignant changes

## Principles of Kidney Transplantation

#### Definition:
Kidney transplantation is surgical implantation of a kidney that is obtained from either healthy kidney donor or brain stem dead cadaver (deceased).

#### Types of donors:
- Living (blood related, unrelated) donor.
- Deceased (cadaveric) donor.

#### Contraindications:
1. Patient's refusal
2. Psychosis
3. Elderly (relative contraindication)
4. Active sepsis
5. Unstable cardiovascular disease
6. Severe respiratory distress
7. Cerebro-vascular hemorrhage
8. Advanced liver disease; unless combined liver and kidney transplantation.
9. Malignancies
10. Un-repairable urological abnormalities.

#### Common complications:

**Surgical complications;**
1. Technical failures.
2. Renal vein and renal artery thrombosis.
3. Urine leakage at uretero-vesical anastomosis.
4. Leg edema (lymphedema) at the same side of the implanted kidney.

**Medical complications:**
1. Acute tubular necrosis (ATN)
2. Rejection (hyperacute, acute, chronic)
3. Complications of immunosuppression
4. Recurrence of the primary renal disease
5. Infection (opportunistic)
6. Malignancy (Kaposi sarcoma of the skin, lymphoproliferative)
7. Hypertension, DM, atherosclerosis, bone disease
8. GIT bleeding, cataract, bone marrow suppression
9. Nephrotoxicity and hepatotoxicity.

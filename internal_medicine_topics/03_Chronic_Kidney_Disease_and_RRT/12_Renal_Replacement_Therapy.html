Section 01 — Dialysis: Definition & Indications

Dialysis — Definition & Indications

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Dialysis

Definition & Temporary Indications (1/2)

Definition

Dialysis is a process of diffusion of substances and water across a semipermeable membrane in both directions (blood on one side and fluid "dialysate" on the other side of the membrane).

I) Temporary (acute / urgent) dialysis

In AKI: many nephrologists advise early dialysis in AKI in all patients, at least for some periods of time, to simplify management, which enhances recovery and minimizes the development of complications.

Control of some uremic symptoms and complications (in both AKI and CKD):

  1. Refractory hyperkalemia
  2. Intractable metabolic acidosis
  3. Progressive fluid overload unresponsive to diuretics
  4. Pericarditis and pleuritis
  5. CNS complications
  6. GIT complications
  7. Bleeding due to uremia
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Dialysis

Indications (2/2) & Contraindications

Removal of dialyzable toxins & drugs

Alcohols Aspirin Lithium Contrast dyes
✓
II) Permanent (chronic, regular or maintenance) dialysis

Grade 5 CKD (ESRD) as a replacement therapy when GFR ≤ 10 ml/min, which may correspond to a serum creatinine of 8-10 mg/dL.

Contraindications

  • Refractory hypotension unresponsive to vasopressors
  • Terminal illness
  • Organic brain damage
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Dialysis

Types of Dialysis

Two types: Peritoneal dialysis (PD) and Hemodialysis (HD).

Peritoneal dialysis (PD)

Utilizes the peritoneal membrane (with its capillaries) as a semipermeable membrane.

Hemodialysis (HD)

A process of diffusion across a semipermeable membrane, to remove unwanted substances from blood while adding desirable components.

Section 02 — Peritoneal Dialysis

Peritoneal Dialysis (PD)

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Peritoneal dialysis

PD — Principles & Indications

Principles

Peritoneal dialysis utilizes the peritoneal membrane (with its capillaries) as a semipermeable membrane. When fluid is introduced into the peritoneal cavity it will equilibrate with the extracellular fluid, and removal of the introduced fluid (effluent) will result in removal of waste products (solutes) and of excess fluid from the body.

Indications

All patients with symptomatic uremia. However, the following are specific indications:

  1. A slowly rising urea
  2. Patients with cardiovascular or hemodynamic instability
  3. Absence of HD facilities
  4. Patients with immature A-V fistula
  5. Poor vascular access
  6. High risk of anticoagulation in a dialysis-dependent patient
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Peritoneal dialysis

PD — Contraindications

Absolute contraindications

  • Peritoneal fibrosis (> 50%) — inadequate peritoneal surface area for dialysis
  • Pleuro-peritoneal leak → hydrothorax
  • Multiple large abdominal wounds with drains

Relative contraindications

  • Abdominal problems — colostomy, nephrostomy, recent abdominal or thoracic surgery, extensive abdominal adhesions, hernia, abdominal malignancy
  • Severe hypercatabolic states e.g. burn
  • Fresh or infected aortic prosthesis
  • Huge polycystic kidneys (insufficient intraperitoneal space)
  • Active diverticulosis
  • Morbid obesity (inadequate clearance)
  • Hyperlipidemia
  • Severe gastroparesis (worsening vomiting)
  • For self-care (home dialysis) patient
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Peritoneal dialysis

PD — Advantages

Advantages (1/2)

  1. Safe and easy to perform
  2. Preserve residual renal function
  3. No routine anticoagulation
  4. Avoidance of vascular surgery
  5. Slow clearance rate → fewer dialysis-related symptoms e.g. disequilibrium or hypotension

Advantages (2/2)

  1. Better control of PTH
  2. More liberal diet
  3. Fewer medications
  4. Less expensive than HD
  5. Less risk of transmission of blood-borne viruses
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Peritoneal dialysis

PD — Disadvantages

  • Long time
  • Low efficiency
  • Inadequate clearance in some patients — e.g. malignant hypertension, peritoneal disease, vasculitis
  • Contraindicated in recent abdominal surgery or pulmonary compromise
  • Associated complications
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Peritoneal dialysis

PD — Complications (1/2)

  1. Peritonitis
  2. Catheter exit-site infection
  3. Constipation
  4. Leakage of dialysate through a diaphragmatic defect into the thoracic cavity → massive pleural effusion and respiratory embarrassment, or to the scrotum
  5. Failure of PM (peritoneal membrane) function → ultrafiltration failure
  6. Sclerosing peritonitis (rare but potentially fatal)
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Peritoneal dialysis

PD — Complications (2/2)

  1. Moderate protein loss → malnutrition
  2. Hyperlipidemia → accelerated atherosclerosis
  3. Obesity due to mobilization of glucose from the dialysate to the body
  4. Inguinal and abdominal hernias
  5. Traumatic perforation of an abdominal organ (bowel, bladder, blood vessel)
  6. Clotting of the cannula

Section 03 — Hemodialysis

Hemodialysis (HD)

Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Hemodialysis

HD — Definition & Advantages

Definition

HD is a process of diffusion across a semipermeable membrane, to remove unwanted substances from blood, while adding desirable components.

Advantages of HD

  • Relatively short time, causing minimal interruption of lifestyle between treatments
  • More efficient than PD
  • Can be performed in the home
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Hemodialysis

HD — Surgical Complications (Vascular Access)

Of catheters

  • Bleeding
  • Infection
  • Thrombosis or stenosis of the vessels
  • Pneumothorax
  • Air embolism

Of A-V fistula and of graft

  • Stenosis → inadequate blood flow
  • Thrombosis
  • Infection
  • Failure to develop adequate venous outflow
  • Vascular steal → limb ischemia
  • Venous hypertension syndrome (distal venous stasis → skin necrosis)
  • High-output heart failure
  • Pseudo-aneurysms
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Hemodialysis

HD — Acute Medical Complications (1/2)

  1. Intradialytic hypotension (IDH)
  2. Hypertension
  3. Cramps
  4. Nausea and vomiting
  5. Headache
  6. Fever
  7. Hemolysis — rare, but potentially serious
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Hemodialysis

HD — Acute Medical Complications (2/2)

  1. Bleeding — anticoagulant overdose
  2. Clotting of extracorporeal circuit (blood lines and dialyzer)
  3. Chest pain
  4. Arrhythmias
  5. Cardiac arrest in the dialysis unit
  6. Air embolism
  7. Disequilibrium syndrome
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Hemodialysis

HD — Chronic Complications (1/2): Cardiovascular

Dialysis patients are more likely to die from cardiovascular disease than the general population. The cause is multifactorial — cardiac or vascular.

Cardiac

  • Ischemic heart disease
  • Valvular heart disease (aortic and mitral valves)
  • Uremic pericarditis and pericardial effusion
  • Uremic endocarditis
  • Cardiomyopathy (dilated)

Vascular

  • Peripheral vascular disease — due to decreased peripheral blood flow related to vascular access, and hypertension and dyslipidemia
  • Metastatic calcification — abnormal calcification of soft tissues due to persistent elevation of plasma phosphate and calcium
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Hemodialysis

HD — Chronic Complications (2/2)

Neuropsychiatric & metabolic

  • Neuropsychiatric complications — anxiety, depression, dementia
  • Malnutrition
  • Dialysis osteomalacia
  • Dialysis-related amyloidosis (DRA)

Rheumatologic & other

  • Crystal-induced arthritis
  • Infection of joints and bone
  • Ischemic necrosis of bone
  • Hypersplenism
  • Acquired renal cystic disease — in patients with prolonged dialysis > 3 years; may show malignant changes

Principles of Kidney Transplantation

Kidney transplantation

Definition & Types of Donors

Definition

Kidney transplantation is the surgical implantation of a kidney that is obtained from either a healthy kidney donor or a brain-stem-dead cadaver (deceased).

Types of donors

Living (blood related, unrelated) donor Deceased (cadaveric) donor
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Kidney transplantation

Contraindications

Contraindications (1/2)

  1. Patient's refusal
  2. Psychosis
  3. Elderly (relative contraindication)
  4. Active sepsis
  5. Unstable cardiovascular disease

Contraindications (2/2)

  1. Severe respiratory distress
  2. Cerebro-vascular hemorrhage
  3. Advanced liver disease — unless combined liver and kidney transplantation
  4. Malignancies
  5. Un-repairable urological abnormalities
Internal Medicine — NephrologyPrinciples of Nephrology — Dr. H. Abd-Elhady

Kidney transplantation

Common Complications

Surgical complications

  • Technical failures
  • Renal vein and renal artery thrombosis
  • Urine leakage at uretero-vesical anastomosis
  • Leg edema (lymphedema) at the same side of the implanted kidney

Medical complications

  • Acute tubular necrosis (ATN)
  • Rejection (hyperacute, acute, chronic)
  • Complications of immunosuppression
  • Recurrence of the primary renal disease
  • Infection (opportunistic)
  • Malignancy (Kaposi sarcoma of the skin, lymphoproliferative)
  • Hypertension, DM, atherosclerosis, bone disease
  • GIT bleeding, cataract, bone marrow suppression
  • Nephrotoxicity and hepatotoxicity

Closing

Key Takeaways

✓
What RRT is

Renal replacement therapy = dialysis (peritoneal and hemodialysis) + renal transplantation. Permanent dialysis: Grade 5 CKD (ESRD) when GFR ≤ 10 ml/min (may correspond to a serum creatinine of 8-10 mg/dL); temporary for AKI, refractory hyperkalemia, intractable acidosis, fluid overload, pericarditis, CNS/GIT complications, uremic bleeding, and dialyzable toxins/drugs.

✓
Peritoneal dialysis

Uses the peritoneal membrane — preserves residual renal function, no routine anticoagulation, slow clearance (fewer disequilibrium or hypotension symptoms), less expensive. Main complications: peritonitis, catheter exit-site infection, ultrafiltration failure, sclerosing peritonitis.

✓
Hemodialysis

More efficient, short treatment time. Complications: vascular access (bleeding, thrombosis, stenosis, infection, steal → limb ischemia), acute (IDH, cramps, bleeding, arrhythmias, disequilibrium syndrome), chronic (cardiovascular disease, malnutrition, DRA, acquired renal cystic disease).

✓
Kidney transplantation

Living (related/unrelated) or deceased (cadaveric) donor. Contraindicated in active sepsis, unstable cardiovascular disease, malignancy, psychosis. Complications: ATN, rejection (hyperacute, acute, chronic), immunosuppression complications, recurrence of primary disease, opportunistic infection, malignancy (Kaposi sarcoma).

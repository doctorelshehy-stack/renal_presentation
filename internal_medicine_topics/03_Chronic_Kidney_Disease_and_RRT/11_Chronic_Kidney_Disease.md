# Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

**Source:** Internal Medicine Nephrology (Principles of Nephrology) – Dr. Hassan Abd-Elhady, Menoufia University

---

#### Definitions:

- **CKD** is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for > 3 months with implications for health and is classified based on cause, GFR category and albuminuria category.
- **Chronic renal failure (CRF)** : GFR is < 60 ml/min/ m2 for ≥ 3 month (Grade 3 CKD).
- **End stage Renal Disease (ESRD)** is a permanent and irreversible renal impairment which necessitate renal replacement therapy = Grade 5 CKD.

---

#### Markers of kidney damage:

**Functional markers:**
- Albuminuria; Albumin excretion rate (AER) ≥ 30 mg/ 24 hs. Or albumin creatinine ratio (ACR) ≥ 30 mg/g.
- Urine sediment abnormalities.
- Electrolytes and other abnormalities due to tubular disorders.

**Structural markers:**
- Structural abnormalities detected by histology.
- Structural abnormalities detected by imaging.
- History of kidney transplantation.

---

#### Grades (stages) of CKD:

Classified according to GFR category (five grads) and albuminuria category (3 grades).

**GFR categories in CKD:**

| Grade | GFR (ml/min/1.73 m2) | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | ≥ 90 | Normal or high |
| G2 | 60-89 | mildly decreased* |
| G3a | 45-89 | mildly to moderately decreased |
| G3b | 30-44 | moderately to severely decreased |
| G4 | 15-29 | severely decreased |
| G5 | < 15 | ESRD |

* Relative to young adult level.
NB: In the absence of evidence of kidney damage neither GFR category G1 nor G2 fulfill the criteria for CKD.

**Albuminuria categories in CKD:**

| Category | AER (mg/24hs) | ACR (mg/g) | Terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | < 30 | < 30 | normal to mildly increased |
| A2 | 30 - 300 | 30 - 300 | moderately increased* |
| A3 | > 300 | > 300 | severely increased** |

* Relative to young adult level.
** Including nephrotic syndrome (albumin excretion usually > 2200 mg/24 hs & ACR > 2200 mg/g).

---

#### Causes:

1. Diabetes mellitus.
2. Hypertension.
3. Glomerulopathies either; primary or secondary.
4. Tubulo-interstitial diseases.
5. Renal vascular diseases (renal artery stenosis).
6. Hereditary renal diseases e.g. PCKD, Alport's syndrome, oxalosis.
7. Obstructive uropathy e.g. urolithiasis, prostatic enlargement, tumors, retroperitoneal fibrosis.
8. Recurrent UTI (pyelonephritis, vesicoureteral reflux).
9. Drugs and toxins; NSAID, chronic lead poisoning.
10. Uncertain (in up to 15% of cases).

---

#### Diagnosis:

**1. Clinical features and complications:** CKD leads to disturbances in the function in every system in the body. The presentation and severity of signs and symptoms vary greatly from patient to patient depending on the grade/ severity of disease.

- Grades 1-3 CKD are usually asymptomatic.
- Grades 4 & 5 are symptomatic.

**o Gastrointestinal:**
1. Anorexia, nausea, vomiting & hiccough (early & controlled by protein restriction)
2. Uremic fetor; uriniferous odor of breath (breakdown of urea to ammonia).
3. Mucosal ulcerations (in late stages) → GIT bleeding.
4. Peptic ulcer; increased gastric acidity, gastrin secretion, 2ry hyperparathyroidism.
5. Increased incidence of diverticulosis (in patients with PCKD).
NB: GIT symptoms are improved by dialysis except peptic ulcers and diverticulosis.

**o Cardiovascular:**
1. CHF and pulmonary edema; dyspnea, orthopnea, increased jugular venous pressure, lower limb edema due to volume overload.
2. Arrhythmias; duo to electrolyte abnormalities.
3. Hypertension.
   NB: absence of hypertension in patient with CKD may be due to;
   - Salt wasting nephropathy e.g. PCKD,
   - Use of anti-hypertensive drugs, or
   - Volume depletion e.g. GIT loss or diuretic overdose.
4. Pericarditis and pericardial effusion.
5. Accelerated atherosclerosis.

**o Respiratory:** Acidotic breathing, pleurisy, and chest infection.

**o Hematological:**
1. Anemia → easy fatigability, palpitation & tachycardia, due to:
   - Erythropoietin deficiency
   - Decreased life span of RBCs and toxic bone marrow suppression
   - Decreased nutrient intake and absorption; iron, vitamin B12, folate
   - Bleeding tendency
   - Secondary hyperparathyroidism
   - Chronic inflammation
   - Blood loss, during dialysis and from GIT
2. Bleeding diathesis due to; prolongation of bleeding time, decreased factor III activity, abnormal platelet aggregation and adhesion, and impaired prothrombin consumption.
3. Enhanced susceptibility to infection;
   - Impaired leukocyte formation and function due to; acidosis, hyperglycemia, hyperosmolarity of serum and tissues by azotemia, and malnutrition.
   - Defective mucosal barriers.
   - Use of steroids and immunosuppressive drugs.

**o Endocrine and metabolism:**
1. Insulin resistance and impaired glucose tolerance
2. Decreased insulin metabolism and degradation by the diseased kidney → decreased insulin requirement for diabetic patients.
3. Thyroid dysfunction; increased TRH → release of prolactin with impaired prolactin clearance → galactorrhea, amenorrhea, and impotence
4. Hypocalcemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism
5. Decreased serum testosterone level → impotence, decreased spermatogenesis and infertility.
6. Menstrual abnormalities (oligomenorrhea, amenorrhea) and infertility.

**o Bone: (CKD-MBD "mineral bone disease" and renal osteodystrophy):**

**Definition of CKD-MBD:**
A systemic disorder of mineral and bone metabolism due to CKD manifested by either one or a combination of the following;
1. Abnormalities of; calcium, phosphorus, PTH, or vitamin D metabolism.
2. Abnormalities in bone turnover, mineralization, volume, linear growth or strength (renal osteodystrophy).
3. Vascular or other soft tissue calcification e.g. cardiac valve, arterial calcification and calciphylaxis.

**Figure (10): Pathophysiology of CKD-MBD (Martin et al., 2007)**

Pathophysiology: (Figure 10)
1. Reduction of GFR < 60 ml/min → decreased phosphorus filtration → hyperphosphatemia → PTH secretion stimulation.
2. Decreased renal production of one alpha hydroxylase enzyme → decreased production of active vitamin D3 "1.25 (OH)2 vit. D" → hypocalcemia, → in addition to hyperphosphatemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT).

**Definition of Renal osteodystrophy:**
The skeletal component of the systemic disorder of MBD that occurs in patients with CKD. Patients may suffer from; bone pain, pathological (spontaneous) fractures and bone changes in bone imaging and bone biopsy.

It includes: (figure:11).

1. **Osteitis fibrosa (high turnover bone disease):** a manifestation of hyperparathyroidism characterized by increased osteoclast and osteoblast activity (turnover) and peritrabecular fibrosis.
2. **Osteomalacia;** a condition characterized by abnormally low bone turnover.
3. **Adynamic bone disease:** characterized by decreased rates of bone formation with normal bone mineralization. It is due to aggressive suppression of PTH leading to hypo-parathyroidism.
4. **Osteopenia or osteoporosis:** Increased skeletal fragility as a result of reduced bone quantity and quality.
5. **Mixed lesions:** (combination of these abnormalities); Patients have high PTH levels + impaired bone formation and mineralization. It is usually seen in patients with previously established hyper-parathyroid bone disease who develop aluminum related bone disease.

**o Neurological:**

1. **CNS:**
   - Early; inability to concentrate, loss of memory, drowsiness, insomnia, hiccups, cramps and muscle twitching.
   - Late; asterixis, myoclonus, chorea, stupor, seizers and coma.
2. **Peripheral nerves:**
   - Sensory (early) and motor neuropathy.
   - Restless legs syndrome; Discomfort in the feet and legs with frequent leg movement. Treatment is early dialysis or transplantation.
3. **In dialysis patients:**
   - Dialysis dementia (encephalopathy).
   - Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome; (Nausea, vomiting, drowsiness, headache, seizures). It occurs in the first few sessions of dialysis. It is due to rapid removal of urea with rapid reduction of osmolality of extracellular compartment within the cranium → to cerebral edema and increase intracranial pressure.

**o Dermatological:**
1. Earthy (muddy) face due to combination of anemia and retention of urochrome pigment.
2. Pallor due to anemia.
3. Ecchymosis and hematomas due to defective homeostasis.
4. Pruritus due to calcium deposition and secondary hyperparathyroidism.
5. Poor skin turgor and dry mucous membranes due to dehydration.
6. Evidence of malnutrition.
7. Uremic frost (in advanced cases); a fine white powder due to high urea concentration in sweat which precipitate in skin after evaporation.
8. Slate-gray-bronze discoloration of skin (hemochromatosis) due to multiple blood transfusions in dialysis patient.
9. Half and half nails.
10. Calciphylaxis (calcific uremic arteriolopathy): vascular calcification of skin, muscles and subcutaneous tissues. The skin shows violaceous rashes, nonhealing ulcers, and gangrene → high mortality.
NB: Most skin abnormalities improve with dialysis except pruritus which persist.

**o Urinary system:**
1. UTI.
2. Urine volume:
   - Early; polyuria and noctouria,
   - Late; oliguria and anuria.

**o Electrolytes and acid-base disturbances:**
1. Hyperkalemia → muscle weakness, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest.
2. Hypocalcemia → tetany is rare as acidosis increase ionized calcium.
3. Hyperphosphatemia → itching, metastatic calcification.
4. Metabolic acidosis → rapid deep breathing, convulsions.

#### 2. Investigations:

**A. To establish the degree of renal impairment and complications:**
1. Urine analysis with evaluation of albuminuria, broad casts, and low fixed specific gravity.
2. Kidney function tests; urea, creatinine and eGFR.
3. Serum electrolytes: Sodium, Potassium, Calcium and Phosphorus
4. Arterial blood gases.
5. Serum uric acid.
6. Complete blood count.
7. Iron profile.
8. iPTH level.
9. Skeletal survey.

**B. To determine the underlying disease (if the cause is not clear):**
1. Serum and urine protein electrophoresis.
2. ANA, anti-ds DNA ab, C3&C4, ANCA, AGBM ab.
3. Hepatitis C and B and HIV serology.
4. Renal imaging: Kidney ureter bladder X-ray (KUB), ultrasound, CT, MRI, radionuclide scan (renogram)
5. Renal biopsy: not indicated if there is small atrophic kidney.

**C. To diagnosis patient with acute RF on top of CKD:**
1. Bone disease
2. Uremic neuropathy.
3. Profound anemia, hyperphosphatemia, and high serum creatinine with mild symptoms.
4. Small sized kidney except in; DM, amyloidosis, multiple myeloma and PCKD.

#### Treatment:

**I) Conservative treatment includes;**

1. Correction of any reversible cause and underlying disease
2. Control of symptoms
3. Educational programs to the patients and their relatives

**Correction of any reversible cause (to delay CKD progression):**
1. Good blood glucose control
2. Good blood pressure control
3. Good hydration
4. Correction of renal obstruction and UTI
5. Treatment of active primary renal disease
6. Treatment of sepsis
7. Reduction of proteinuria (ACEI/ ARBs, avoid combination)
8. Control of dyslipidemia
9. Lifestyle modification (diet, weight reduction, smoking & alcohol cessation, exercise).
10. Avoid nephrotoxins and large doses of contrast agents.

**Control of symptoms:**

**Diet:** (low protein, potassium, phosphate, and salts) + fluid balance.
1. Low protein diet (0.6-0.8 gm/ kg/ day). Protein diet should be increased when patient start dialysis.
2. Low potassium diet (if the patient is hypokalemic increase potassium diet).
3. Low phosphate diet
4. Salt restriction (free salt intake in salt loosing nephropathy)
5. Fluid balance; depends on urine output.

**GIT:**
1. Hiccough and vomiting → prokinetics. If persist → dialysis.
2. Gastritis; proton pump inhibitors (avoid long term use as may cause interstitial nephropathy).

**CVS:**
1. Control of hypertension; ACEI/ARBs, CCB, BB.
2. ACEI or ARBs are preferred if there is proteinuria but with careful monitoring of serum creatinine and potassium levels.
3. Volume overload → loop diuretics.
4. Pericarditis → dialysis.

**Mineral bone disease (MBD):** Control of hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, vitamin D deficiency and secondary hyperparathyroidism.

- **Diet control:**
  - Increased elementary dietary calcium to 1500-2000 mg/day (hold if there is hypercalcemia).
  - Low phosphate diet (800-1000 mg/day) adjusted by requirement in protein in grades 3-5 CKD patients.
- **Phosphate binders:** Indicated when dietary phosphate restriction is insufficient to control phosphate and PTH levels.
  - **Calcium-based phosphate binders;** e.g. Ca carbonate, giving orally during meals.
    A. It decreases the serum phosphate level effectively and can be used for initial phosphate binding treatment.
    B. Its long-term administration can result in hypercalcemia.
    C. It should not be used in dialysis patients with hypercalcemia and in patients with plasma PTH level < 130 pg/dL. In these conditions use calcium-free phosphate binders.
  - **Non-calcium-based phosphate binders;** Sevelamer (Renalgel) hydrochloride or bicarbonate, safe and effective but expensive.
  - **Lanthanium carbonate**
- **Calcimimitic agents** (cinacalcet) inhibits iPTH secretion.
- **Vit. D analog** (alphcalcidol/ calcitriol; 0.25- 2 µg daily according to serum Ca, Po4 and iPTH levels). Hold if there is hypercalcemia and/or hyperphosphatemia.
  Treatment with vitamin D is recommended for renal patients with:
  1- Total serum calcium level <9.5 mg/dl.
  2- Serum phosphate level < 4.6 mg/dl.
  3- Elevated serum PTH level.
- **Parathyroidectomy** in severe hyperparathyroidism (PTH > 800 pg/dl) not responding to medications.

**Management of anemia:**
1. Iron therapy: oral in pre-dialysis patients and better I.V. for HD patients.
2. Erythropoietin; subcutaneous or intravenous.
   - Erythropoietin alpha or beta.
   - Long acting darbapoetin alfa.
   - Continuous erythropoiesis receptor activator (CERA).

**Pruritus:**
1. Moistening ointment.
2. Gabapentin, Naltrexone.
3. Short wav ultraviolet.
4. Increase dose of dialysis.

**Correction of electrolyte and acid-base disorders (see chapter V).**

**Educational program:**
- Explain the possibility of eventual renal failure and forms of therapy.
- In patients planning for HD → preparation of AF fistula.
- In patient planning for PD or transplantation → early education of family members for selection and preparation for a home dialysis helper or a related donor.

**II) Renal replacement therapy:** Dialysis and renal transplantation.

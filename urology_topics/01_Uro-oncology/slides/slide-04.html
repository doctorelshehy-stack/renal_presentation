Prostate Cancer · 1/8

Incidence & Risk Factors

Factors determining the risk of clinical PCa are not well known; a few have been identified

Incidence

  • In Europe, PCa is the most common solid neoplasm — incidence 214 cases per 1000
  • Currently the second most common cause of cancer death in men

Three Well-Established Risk Factors

  • Increasing age — the single most important demographic factor
  • Ethnic origin — e.g., Japanese men have a low incidence of PCa
  • Heredity — family history is a strong determinant

Heredity — Quantified Risk

  • One first-line relative with PCa → risk is at least doubled
  • Two or more first-line relatives affected → risk increases 5–11-fold

Clinical Note

Most early-stage cancers are asymptomatic; risk factors determine who needs screening with PSA and digital rectal examination. Heredity remains the strongest patient-level signal after age and ethnicity.

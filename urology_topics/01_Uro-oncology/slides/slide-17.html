Bladder Cancer · 5/9

Natural History & Clinical Presentation

What the patient experiences and what the tumour does

Natural History

  • Roughly 55–60% of all newly diagnosed bladder cancers are well- or moderately differentiated, superficial (confined to urothelium or lamina propria) papillary transitional cell carcinomas
  • 40–45% of newly diagnosed bladder cancers are high-grade lesions — more than half are muscle invading or more extensive at the time of diagnosis

Other Signs & Symptoms

  • Flank pain from ureteral obstruction
  • Lower extremity edema
  • Pelvic mass
  • Very rarely: symptoms of advanced disease — weight loss, abdominal or bone pain

Most Common Presentation

  • Painless hematuria — occurs in about 85% of patients
  • Bladder irritability + urinary frequency, urgency, dysuria — second most common presentation; usually associated with diffuse carcinoma in situ or invasive bladder cancer
  • These symptoms almost never occur without (at least) microscopic hematuria
Hematuria is the hallmark: painless hematuria in ~85% of patients is the most common presenting symptom of bladder cancer.

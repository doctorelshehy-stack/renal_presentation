Prostate Cancer · 2/8

Diagnosis — Clinical Findings & PSA

Symptoms, signs and prostate-specific antigen (PSA)

A. Symptoms

  • Most patients with early-stage PCa are asymptomatic
  • Presence of symptoms often suggests locally advanced or metastatic disease
  • Obstructive or irritative voiding complaints result from local growth of the tumour into the urethra or bladder neck, or from its direct extension

B. Signs

A physical examination, including a digital rectal examination (DRE), is needed. Induration, if detected, must alert the physician to the possibility of cancer and the need for further evaluation.

Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA)

  • Glycoprotein acting as a serine protease
  • Produced by benign and malignant prostate tissues
  • Normal PSA values: ≤ 2.5 – 4 ng/mL (according to age)

PSA Derivatives

  • Free/total PSA ratio — ratio < 25% is more suggestive of PCa
  • PSA density — PSA ÷ prostate volume = 0.15; a value above 0.15 means high susceptibility for cancer

Bladder Cancer · 1/9

Epidemiology & Carcinogenesis

Chemical carcinogens and alterations in DNA

Molecular Basis

  • Chemical carcinogens
  • Alterations in DNA often lead to induction of oncogenes (p21 ras oncogene) or reversal of tumour suppressor genes (p53), resulting in a malignantly transformed cell

Key Epidemiology

  • More than 2.5× more common in men than in women
  • In men: fourth most common cancer after prostate, lung and colorectal cancers
  • Mortality rate: 22% of patients with bladder cancer per year

Inducing Agents

  • Viruses
  • Chemical carcinogens — aniline dyes
  • Cigarette smoking — nitrosamines, 2-naphthylamine, 4-aminobiphenyl, increased urinary tryptophan metabolites
  • Coffee drinking
  • Ingestion of analgesics (containing phenacetin) or artificial sweeteners
  • Bacterial, parasitic, fungal and viral infections
  • Harboring of bladder calculi
  • Receiving genotoxic chemotherapeutic agents

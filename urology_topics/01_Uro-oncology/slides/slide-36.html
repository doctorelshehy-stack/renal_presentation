URO-ONCOLOGY

Uro-Oncology — Key Takeaways

Prostate cancer: most common solid neoplasm in Europe; PSA, DRE and TRUS-guided biopsy diagnose; Gleason score grades 2–10; hormonal therapy acts via the LHRH–LH–testosterone–DHT axis; treatment is stage-dependent (T1: surveillance/surgery/RT — T2: radical prostatectomy is standard).
Bladder cancer: painless hematuria in ~85%; urothelial carcinoma is most common; WHO 2004 grading; spread via en bloc (~60%), lymphatic (obturator 74%) and vascular routes (liver 38%); NMIBC → TURBT + intravesical therapy; MIBC → radical cystectomy + pelvic lymphadenectomy.
Upper urinary tract tumours: bladder > renal pelvis > ureter (125:2.5:1); smoking is the top risk factor; TCC > 90%; radical nephroureterectomy with bladder cuff is the gold standard; endoscopic therapy for solitary kidney, bilateral disease or low-grade lesions; MVAC or gemcitabine + taxanes for advanced disease.
Renal tumors: benign lesions (cysts — Bosniak I–IV, oncocytoma, angiomyolipoma) are increasingly incidental; RCC = 85% of malignant renal tumours; 2017 TNM (T1 ≤ 7 cm, T4 beyond Gerota); localized → radical nephrectomy; metastatic → multimodal therapy.
Testicular tumors: most common GU malignancy at 15–35 years; cryptorchidism is the commonest risk factor; seminoma (40%) vs NSGCT (yolk sac, embryonal, teratoma, choriocarcinoma); treatment starts with ipsilateral radical orchiectomy ± chemo/RT/RPLND — cure rate 90%.

Genitourinary oncology demands accurate staging before therapy — stage drives every treatment decision.

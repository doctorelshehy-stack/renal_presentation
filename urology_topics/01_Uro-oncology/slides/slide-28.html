Benign Renal Tumors

Cysts, oncocytomas, angiomyolipomas and adenomas

Overview & Diagnosis

  • Diagnosed more frequently recently due to increasing use of abdominal imaging (US and CT)
  • Commonest benign renal lesions: renal cysts, oncocytomas, angiomyolipomas and renal adenomas
  • Most often diagnosed accidentally during abdominal imaging (US, CT, MRI)
  • Symptoms: pain, haematuria and palpable mass

Renal Cysts — Types & Significance

  • Types — sporadic; genetic (autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease); acquired renal cystic disease (chronic dialysis)
  • Significance — exclude malignant cystic lesions
  • Simple cysts on US — smooth thin walls and clear fluid content
  • If malignancy suspected — contrast-enhanced CT is required

Oncocytoma & Angiomyolipoma

  • Oncocytoma — most common enhancing benign soft tissue lesion; on CT: hypervascular mass with central scar (not definitively diagnostic); most are surgically removed and diagnosed at histopathology
  • Angiomyolipoma — formed of dysmorphic blood vessels, muscle and fat cells; diagnosed by CT detecting the fat component; when large → retroperitoneal haemorrhage; more common in females, more prone to bleeding during pregnancy

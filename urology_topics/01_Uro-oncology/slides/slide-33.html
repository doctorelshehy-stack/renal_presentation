Testicular Tumors · 1/3

Introduction, Aetiology & Presentation

Introduction

  • Most common genitourinary tract malignancy in males aged 15–35
  • Relatively rare disease — affects less than 1% of all human neoplasms
  • Only 7% incidence at infancy and teen age, and 7% above 55 years old
  • More common on the right side
  • More common in Caucasian than Asian and African populations
  • Highly curable and treatable — cure rate 90% in all ages

Aetiology

  • Unknown, but may be cryptorchidism — the commonest risk factor; unfortunately orchiopexy does not prevent cancer
  • Orchitis and trauma
  • Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)
  • Positive family history
  • Testicular carcinoma in situ
  • Testicular atrophy

Presentation

  • Painless scrotal lump
  • Dull aching pain or heaviness
  • Acute pain in 10% — may be due to hemorrhage
  • Metastasis
  • Functional — gynecomastia — in 5% of germ cell tumours

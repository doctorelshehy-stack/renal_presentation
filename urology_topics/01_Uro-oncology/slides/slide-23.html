Upper Urinary Tract · 2/5

Pathology

Benign lesions, abnormal urothelium and spread

Benign Lesions

  • 1 — Inverted papilloma: may be benign or have malignant potential
  • 2 — Fibroepithelial polyps and neurofibromas

Abnormal Urothelium

  • 1 — Metaplasia and dysplasia
  • 2 — Carcinoma in situ: whitish plaque or velvety red patch
  • 3 — TCC in more than 90% of upper tract tumours; may be sessile or papillary
  • 4 — SCC in 7% of cases; associated with chronic inflammation or analgesic abuse
  • 5 — Adenocarcinoma in 1% of cases
  • 6 — Sarcomas very rare

Spread — Four Routes

  • 1 — Direct invasion — to the renal parenchyma or surrounding structures
  • 2 — Lymphatic invasion — para-aortic, para-caval, iliac and pelvic lymph nodes
  • 3 — Vascular invasion — liver, lung, bone
  • 4 — Epithelial spread — by seeding or direct extension — e.g., recurrence of a ureteral stump left in situ after nephrectomy
TCC accounts for > 90% of upper tract tumours; SCC relates to chronic inflammation or analgesic abuse.

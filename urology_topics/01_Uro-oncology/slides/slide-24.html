Upper Urinary Tract · 3/5

Diagnosis

Clinical presentation, laboratory and imaging investigations

Clinical Presentation

  • Hematuria (gross or microscopic) — the most common presenting symptom
  • Flank pain — typically dull — gradual obstruction and hydronephrotic distention; can be acute, mimicking renal colic due to passage of clots
  • Asymptomatic — discovered incidentally on radiologic evaluation
  • Advanced disease — flank or abdominal pain, weight loss, anorexia, or bone pain

Laboratory

  • Urine analysis and culture
  • Urine cytology: directly related to tumour grade

Imaging

  • U/S — detects hydronephrosis or pelvic mass
  • IVU — suggestive findings: a) radiolucent filling defect; b) obstruction of part of the tract; c) non-visualization of the collecting system
  • CT scanning — higher accuracy: parenchymal lesion, extent of invasion, associated lesions, enlarged lymph nodes and metastasis
  • Renal scan — determines function of the diseased and the contralateral kidney

Endoscopy

  • Cystoscopy — to exclude coexistent bladder lesions
  • Ureteroscopy evaluation and biopsy — biopsy of suspected areas

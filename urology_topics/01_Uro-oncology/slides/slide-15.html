Bladder Cancer · 3/9

Pathology — Squamous Cell Carcinoma & Adenocarcinoma

Geographic variability and chronic irritation

Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)

  • Prevalence varies worldwide: 1% of bladder cancers in England, 3–7% in the United States, 40–75% in Egypt
  • About 80% of SCCs in Egypt are associated with chronic infection with S. haematobium
  • Patients are on average 10–20 years younger than patients with TCC
  • Bilharzial cancers: exophytic, nodular, fungating lesions, usually well differentiated, with a relatively low incidence of lymph node and distant metastases
  • Whether the low metastasis rate is due to capillary and lymphatic fibrosis from chronic schistosomal infection or to the low histologic grade is not clear

Non-Bilharzial SCC & Adenocarcinoma

  • Non-bilharzial SCC: chronic irritation from urinary calculi, long-term indwelling catheters, chronic urinary infections, or bladder diverticula
  • As many as 80% of paraplegics with chronic infections and/or indwelling catheters have squamous changes in the bladder; about 5% develop squamous cell carcinoma
  • Adenocarcinomas account for less than 2% of primary bladder cancers

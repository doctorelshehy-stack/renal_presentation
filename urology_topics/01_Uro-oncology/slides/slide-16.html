Bladder Cancer · 4/9

Patterns of Spread

Direct extension, lymphatic, vascular and implantation spread

Direct Extension (Local Invasion)

  • En bloc spread (~60%) — cancer cells invade in a broad front directly beneath the primary mucosal lesion
  • Tentacle-like invasion (~25%) — finger-like infiltration into the wall
  • Lateral spread (~10%) — tumour cells grow under normal-appearing mucosa

Implantation

Bladder cancer also spreads by implantation in abdominal wounds, denuded urothelium, resected prostatic fossa, or traumatized urethra.

1 — Lymphatic Spread (pelvic nodes)

  • Lymphatic metastases occur earlier and independently of haematogenous metastases in some patients
  • Patients with limited nodal metastases may be cured with radical cystectomy and pelvic lymphadenectomy
  • Paravesical nodes — 16%
  • Obturator nodes — 74%
  • External iliac nodes — 65%
  • Presacral nodes — ~25%
  • Juxtaregional common iliac nodes — ~20%

2 — Vascular Spread

  • Liver — 38%
  • Lung — 36%
  • Bone — 27%
  • Adrenal glands — 21%
  • Intestine — 13%
  • Bone metastases are considerably more common with bilharzial bladder cancer

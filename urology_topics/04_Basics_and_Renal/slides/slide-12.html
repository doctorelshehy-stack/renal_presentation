The Prostate Gland & Urethra

Prostate — Overview

  • Fibromuscular and glandular organ lying just inferior to the bladder
  • Normal prostate weighs about 20 g; contains the posterior urethra (~2.5 cm in length)
  • Supported anteriorly by the puboprostatic ligaments and inferiorly by the urogenital diaphragm
  • Perforated posteriorly by the ejaculatory ducts, which open into the verumontanum on the floor of the prostatic urethra
  • Lowsley classification: 5 lobes — anterior, posterior, median, right lateral, left lateral
  • McNeal (1972): peripheral, central and transitional zones; an anterior segment; and a preprostatic sphincteric zone

Prostate — Vessels, Nerves & Relations

  • Arterial — inferior vesical, internal pudendal and middle rectal (hemorrhoidal) arteries
  • Venous — periprostatic plexus, connected with the deep dorsal vein of the penis and internal iliac (hypogastric) veins
  • Nerves — rich supply from sympathetic and parasympathetic plexuses
  • Lymphatics — internal iliac (hypogastric), sacral, vesical and external iliac nodes
  • Relations — lies behind the pubic symphysis; vasa deferentia and seminal vesicles close to its posterosuperior surface; posteriorly separated from the rectum by the two layers of Denonvilliers' fascia

Urethra

  • Extends from the base of the bladder to the outside world
  • Female: 4 cm long
  • Male: 15 cm long

CKD — Complications, Anemia & Renal Replacement Therapy

Complications

  • Of renal dysfunction — volume overload, hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, hyperphosphatemia, renal osteodystrophy, hypertension, anemia, dyslipidemia, sexual dysfunction
  • Of ESRD — malnutrition, uremic bleeding, pericarditis, uremic neuropathy, thyroid dysfunction, increased risk of infection (managed via vaccination)

Anemia in CKD

  • Causes — relative erythropoietin (EPO) deficiency; diminished RBC survival; bleeding diathesis and iron deficiency; secondary hyperparathyroidism (bone marrow fibrosis); chronic inflammation; hemoglobinopathies; vitamin deficiencies (folate, B12); comorbidities (thyroid dysfunction, pregnancy, HIV, autoimmune disease)
  • Treatment — maintain hemoglobin 10.5–12 g/dL; options — RBC transfusions, androgens, erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESA); initiate ESA when Hb < 10 g/dL; standard dose 10,000 units EPO subcutaneously weekly; supplemental iron to maintain transferrin saturation 20–50% and serum ferritin 100–500 ng/mL

Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT)

  • Early identification of CKD and timely referral to specialists
  • Clear indications to initiate RRT — uremic pericarditis and encephalopathy; intractable muscle cramping; persistent anorexia and nausea (not due to other causes such as PUD); evidence of malnutrition; refractory fluid/electrolyte issues (hyperkalemia or ECF volume overload)
CKD complications span every system — anemia management targets Hb 10.5–12 g/dL with ESA + iron.

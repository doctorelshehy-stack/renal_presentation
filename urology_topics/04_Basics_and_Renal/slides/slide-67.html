Transplant — Pulmonary Complications & Benefits

Pulmonary Complications

  • Pneumonia is the most common pulmonary complication
  • Non-opportunistic post-op pneumonia occurs in the 1st month; opportunistic infections predominate thereafter; after the 1st year community-acquired infections are common
  • Drug interaction — if macrolides (erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin) are used, doses of cyclosporine, tacrolimus and sirolimus must be reduced

Fever Recap — Non-Infectious & Infectious

  • Infectious (1st month) — pneumonia, wound infection, line sepsis, catheter-associated UTI; pathogens: E. coli (UTI), S. aureus and S. viridans (line/wound), S. pneumoniae (pneumonia)
  • Non-infectious — pulmonary atelectasis (early post-op), severe acute rejection, reaction to antilymphocyte antibodies, post-transplant lymphoma (PTLD)
  • Work-up — FBC with differential, serum creatinine, urine dipstick; blood and urine cultures; CXR and transplant U/S

Benefits of Transplantation

  • Improved life expectancy
  • Cardiovascular benefits
  • Enhanced quality of life — higher life satisfaction, physical and emotional well-being, ability to return to work; uremic complications more fully reversed; fertility often restored
  • Socioeconomic benefits — increased rates of return to work; hemodialysis costs $60,000–$80,000 annually; transplantation > $100,000 in the first year but drops to ~ $10,000/year thereafter; mean cumulative costs are equal for the first 3–4 years; transplantation becomes significantly more cost-effective long term
Transplantation is cost-effective long term despite higher first-year costs.

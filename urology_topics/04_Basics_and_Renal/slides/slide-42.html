AKI · 5/13

Renal AKI — Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)

The type of AKI in the majority of hospitalized patients

Epidemiology & Classification

  • The type of AKI in hospitalized patients is ATN in the majority of cases
  • AKI may occur in 2–7% of hospitalized patients in tertiary care centers
  • The incidence in intensive care units may exceed 25%
  • Classified by primary site of injury — tubular (ATN); interstitium (AIN); glomerulus (AGN); vascular

Causes of ATN

  • 1 — Renal hypoperfusion and renal ischemia: the most common causes
  • 2 — Nephrotoxic insults: aminoglycosides (10–30% risk); amphotericin B; cisplatin; cyclosporin; temsirolimus

Prevention of Aminoglycoside- & Amphotericin-Related AKI

  • In patients with normal, steady-state kidney function, aminoglycosides should be administered as a single-dose daily regimen rather than multiple doses
  • Drug levels for aminoglycosides should be monitored if treatment lasts more than 48 hours
  • Topical or local applications (respiratory aerosols or antibiotic beads) are preferred over IV application when feasible, to reduce systemic toxicity
Single daily dosing + level monitoring + topical preference = the triad of aminoglycoside nephroprotection.

The Ureter

Course

  • Continue from the renal pelvis at the level of L1
  • Each ureter is about 25 cm long
  • Runs inferomedially along the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae
  • Crosses the pelvic brim at the SIJ, anterior to the origins of the external iliac artery
  • Runs anterior to the internal iliac artery in the pelvis
  • Passes towards the ischial spine, then turns towards the pubic tubercle and enters the bladder
  • In females — the uterine arteries cross the juxtavesical portion of the ureters
  • In males — the vasa deferentia sweep over the lateral pelvic walls anterior to the ureters, lying medial to them before joining the seminal vesicle

Ureteral Landmarks (Radiologic)

  • UPJ — ureteropelvic junction (where the renal pelvis joins the ureter)
  • UVJ — ureterovesical junction (where the ureter enters the bladder)
  • Iliac vessels — the landmark where the ureter crosses into the pelvis
  • UO — ureteral orifice
  • On retrograde pyelography the ureter is divided into three segments (I, II, III) corresponding to the abdominal and pelvic portions

Blood Supply & Lymphatics

  • Supplied along its course by: aorta, renal arteries, gonadal arteries, common iliac, internal iliac, vesical and uterine arteries
  • Lymphatics — upper ureter → lumbar nodes; middle → internal iliac (hypogastric) and common iliac nodes; lower → vesical and hypogastric nodes

Kidney — Anomalies of Ascent, Form & Fusion

Renal Ectopia (Anomalies in Ascent)

  • The mature kidney fails to reach its normal location in the renal fossa
  • Positions — pelvic, iliac, abdominal, thoracic, contralateral = crossed
  • The renal pelvis is located anteriorly (different from renal ptosis)

Anomalies of Form & Fusion

  • Crossed ectopia with and without fusion
  • Sigmoid or S-shaped kidney
  • Lump or cake kidney
  • Horse-shoe kidney

Renal Ptosis vs Ectopia

  • Ptosis: a normally positioned (fixed) kidney that descends with posture — the pelvis remains anterior
  • Ectopia: the kidney never reaches the fossa; the pelvis is anteriorly placed and the ureter is short
Horseshoe kidney — the commonest fusion anomaly — is often complicated by UPJ obstruction and stone disease.

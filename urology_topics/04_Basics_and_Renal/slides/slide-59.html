Renal Transplantation — Introduction, Definitions & ESRD Indications

Introduction & Definitions

  • Renal transplantation is the preferred treatment for ESRD
  • Hemodialysis is center-based (2.5–5 hours, 3×/week)
  • Transplantation offers better quality of life and greater longevity than long-term dialysis
  • Allograft — transplant between genetically different individuals of the same species (human to human)
  • Autograft — donor and recipient are the same person
  • Xenograft — donor and recipient of different species

ESRD — Indications & Risks

  • Diabetic nephropathy is responsible for 40% of diseases leading to transplantation; diabetic patients are more prone to post-transplant complications
  • Any patient with GFR < 20 mL/min should meet with a transplant team
  • Diseases with significant risk of recurrence in the allograft — primary focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS); hemolytic-uremic syndrome (HUS); membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (MPGN); primary oxalosis
  • Perioperative risk — patients over age 50 with comorbidities have approximately a 25% risk for complications
  • Pretransplant infection screening — HIV, hepatitis B & C, EBV, CMV, syphilis, tuberculosis

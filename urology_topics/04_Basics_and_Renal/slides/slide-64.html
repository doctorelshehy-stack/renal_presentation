Renal Transplantation — Immunosuppression & Surgical Complications 1–3

Immunosuppressive Therapy

  • Renal transplant patients require lifelong immunosuppression to prevent rejection
  • 'Triple' therapy regimens — cyclosporine-microemulsion or tacrolimus + mycophenolate mofetil or azathioprine + corticosteroids
  • Sirolimus (available 1994) is now part of standard protocols
  • Treatment of rejection — IV corticosteroids — methylprednisolone 250–1000 mg daily or dexamethasone 100 mg daily, for 3 days

Complications 1–3

  • Acute vascular occlusion — 0.5–8% in the first post-transplant week; oligoanuria, ARF; renal vein thrombosis — graft tenderness, dark hematuria, decreased urine; diagnosis: Doppler U/S or radioisotope scan; treatment: surgery
  • Peritransplant haematoma — 2–3%, early post-op or related to anticoagulation; severe pain over allograft, decreased Hb/hematocrit, increased creatinine, recurrent hyperkalemia (RBC lysis); diagnosis: CT; treatment: surgery (often allograft nephrectomy)
  • Urinary leak — 2–5% during the first month; urine extravasation, ARF, fever, pain, distended abdomen; diagnosis: U/S or radioisotope scan; treatment: Foley catheter and surgery

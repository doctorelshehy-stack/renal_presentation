Plain X-Ray of the Urinary Tract (KUB)

Introduction

  • KUB may be used as a scout film, to which contrast studies may be compared
  • Or as a primary survey to assess urologic or abdominal pathologic processes
  • Typical field of view: kidneys superiorly, bladder inferiorly; also lower ribs, liver, bowel, spleen, lumbar spine, sacrum, bony pelvis
  • In larger patients two films may be required
  • A good KUB should show the last 3 ribs and the lower border of the ischial tuberosity

Assessment — Film & Technical Factors

  • Film specifics — name of patient; age & date of birth; location of patient; date taken; film number (if applicable)
  • Technical factors — type of projection (supine is standard); markings of any special techniques used

Calcified Structures ('White Bits')

  • Type 1 — calcium that is an abnormal structure — e.g., gallstones and renal calculi
  • Type 2 — calcium within a normal structure, but representing pathology — e.g., nephrocalcinosis
  • Type 3 — calcium within a normal structure, but harmless — e.g., lymph node calcification

Soft Tissue & Foreign Bodies

  • Soft tissue — kidneys, spleen, liver and bladder (if filled) seen, plus psoas shadows and abdominal fat; poorly seen compared with US or CT — rarely is action taken on this imaging alone
  • Foreign bodies — ingested and rectal foreign bodies; items in the x-ray path (belt buckles, buttons, jewellery); deliberately placed objects (aortic stent, IVC filter, suprapubic catheter); sterilization clips and intra-uterine devices in women

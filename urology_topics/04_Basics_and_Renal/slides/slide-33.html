Intravenous Urography (IVU)

Introduction

  • Depends on visualization of the urinary tract by injecting contrast medium that is concentrated and excreted by the kidneys, delineating the tract on X-ray
  • Nowadays its use in urology has diminished due to the availability of cross-sectional imaging such as CT and MRI

Uses

  • 1 — Obstruction: congenital (UPJO); acquired (ureteric strictures)
  • 2 — Haematuria
  • 3 — Recurrent UTIs
  • 4 — Stone disease
  • 5 — Urinary tract fistulas

Phases

  • 1 — Plain film (KUB or scout film)
  • 2 — Nephrogram (dye concentrated into the kidney parenchyma)
  • 3 — Urogram (supine film after 5–10 minutes, visualizing the collecting system)
  • 4 — Prone film (pressure on the abdomen causes fullness of the collecting system with a better contrast image)
  • 5 — Full bladder film (can detect filling defects in the bladder)
  • 6 — Post-micturition film (to evaluate bladder emptying)
The six IVU phases progress from scout film through nephrogram, urogram, prone, full bladder and post-micturition films.

The Urinary Bladder — Vessels & Innervation

Arterial Supply & Venous Drainage

  • Arterial — superior, middle and inferior vesical arteries from the anterior trunk of the internal iliac (hypogastric) artery; smaller branches from obturator and inferior gluteal arteries; in females, uterine and vaginal arteries also send branches
  • Venous — rich plexus surrounding the bladder, emptying into the internal iliac (hypogastric) veins

Lymphatics

  • Drain into the vesical, external iliac, internal iliac (hypogastric), and common iliac lymph nodes

Innervation

  • Pelvic nerves: sensory nerves detect the degree of stretching in the bladder wall
  • Parasympathetic motor nerves then cause contraction of the bladder
  • Somatic nerves (pudendal) control the voluntary skeletal muscle of the external sphincter
  • Sympathetic nerves stimulate the bladder neck and external sphincter during filling (contraction); this is inhibited during micturition
  • Spinal cord levels: sympathetic outflow T11–L2; parasympathetic (S2–S4) and pudendal nerve (S2–S4)
Sensory → parasympathetic → somatic (pudendal) → sympathetic: the four players of bladder storage and emptying.

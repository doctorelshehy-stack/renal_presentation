Renal AKI — Acute Glomerulonephritis & Interstitial Nephritis

Acute Glomerulonephritis (AGN)

  • Proteinuria, hematuria and red blood cell (RBC) casts are highly suggestive of glomerulonephritis
  • Puffiness of the face; blood in urine
  • Types of glomerular disease — acute post-infectious GN; lupus nephritis; IgA glomerulonephritis; infective endocarditis; Goodpasture syndrome; Wegener disease (ANCA-associated)
AGN = RBC casts; AIN = WBC casts + eosinophiluria (Hansel stain); ATN = muddy brown casts.

Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN)

  • Diagnosis suggested by urinalysis: sterile pyuria, white blood cell casts, and eosinophiluria (using Hansel stain)
  • Most frequently induced by drug therapy
  • Common causative drugs — NSAIDs (especially fenoprofen); penicillins and cephalosporins; rifampin; sulfonamides (including furosemide and thiazides); cimetidine, omeprazole, allopurinol; ciprofloxacin and 5-aminosalicylates

Vascular (Renal) AKI

  • Large vessels — bilateral renal artery stenosis; bilateral renal vein thrombosis
  • Small vessels — vasculitis; malignant hypertension; atherosclerotic or thrombotic emboli; haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS); thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)

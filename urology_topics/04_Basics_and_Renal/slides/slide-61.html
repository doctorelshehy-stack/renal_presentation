Renal Transplantation — Allocation & Preservation

Allocation

  • ABO blood groups — ABO-incompatible transplantation triggers antibody binding to non-inherited antigens on endothelial cells → complement activation → coagulation → thrombosis → rapid graft loss; achievable if antibody titers are low and antibody production is limited with immunosuppression
  • MHC/HLA — the human major histocompatibility complex consists of over 200 genes on chromosome 6p21.3, coding cell surface proteins expressed on renal allografts; recipient leukocytes recognize these proteins and trigger an immune response

Kidney Preservation & Donation

  • The renal tubular sodium-potassium pump maintains high intracellular potassium; preservation solutions maintain organ viability during transport
  • UW solution contents — K⁺ lactobionate 100 mM, KH₂PO₄ 25 mM, MgSO₄ 5 mM, raffinose 30 mM, adenosine 5 mM, glutathione 3 mM, insulin 100 U/L, dexamethasone 8 mg/L, allopurinol 1 mM, hydroxyethyl starch 50 g/L, penicillin 200,000 U/L; pH 7.45, osmolarity 320 ± 5 mOsm/L
  • Absolute contraindications to donation — HIV, sepsis, non-CNS malignancy, severe cardiovascular disease; age > 75 is a relative contraindication
  • Living donor operation — laparoscopic donor nephrectomy (LDN) is the standard of care — 6% minor complications, < 2% major, mortality 0.03%; most LDNs on the left side (longer renal vein)

AKI · 8/13

Assessment of a Patient with AKI — Focused History

History of the Cause

  • Pre-renal clues — nausea, vomiting, history of dehydration, renal or liver disease
  • Post-renal clues — hesitancy, frequency, urgency, weak stream, dribbling, feeling of incomplete bladder emptying, flank pain; history of kidney stones, BPH or spinal cord injury
  • Any recent illnesses? Fever? Rashes?
  • Any recent surgery? Cardiovascular instability?
  • Toxin exposure: new medications (antibiotics, NSAIDs)? IV contrast?
  • Change in urination, any edema/SOB/weight gain?

History of the AKI Itself

  • Decreased urine output (urine output can sometimes remain normal)
  • Fluid retention — edema of legs/ankles/feet; shortness of breath, fatigue, confusion, nausea, weakness
  • Irregular heartbeat, chest pain/pressure; in severe cases seizures or coma
  • AKI can be asymptomatic and detected incidentally via lab tests

History of the Complications

  • Hyperkalaemia, hypo/hypernatraemia, hypercalcaemia
  • Metabolic acidosis
  • Pulmonary oedema
  • Hypertension
  • Uraemic encephalopathy
  • Uraemic pericarditis

Symptoms of the Cause — Examples

  • Dehydration — thirst, light-headedness, dry mouth, dark urine
  • Sepsis — fever, rigors, localized symptoms of the infectious focus
  • Drug history — ACE inhibitors (ACEIs), ARBs, NSAIDs, aminoglycosides (e.g., gentamicin)

Stone Disease · 10/16

Intervention — Indications & Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL)

Indications for Intervention

  • Failed conservative treatment
  • Larger stones > 1 cm
  • Increasing backpressure changes
  • Deterioration of renal functions
  • Unresolved or persistent UTI
  • Associated pathology needing intervention

ESWL — Principle

  • High-energy electricity-generated acoustic shock waves
  • Focused to the stone using X-ray (C-arm), ultrasonography, or both
  • The stone is fragmented into minute particles
  • Conservative treatments are maintained to facilitate passage of stone particles
  • Indication: stones 2 cm or less

Contraindications of ESWL

  • Urological — associated pathology requiring intervention; acute episode of UTI; distal obstruction; larger stones; renal insufficiency; single renal unit unless stented
  • Non-urological — pregnancy; bone deformities that render localization of the stone difficult; uncontrollable bleeding diathesis

Advantages & Disadvantages of ESWL

  • Advantages — non-invasive; relatively painless; suitable for risky patients; successful for most urinary calculi
  • Disadvantages — transient hematuria; colicky pain; fever; Stein-Strasse (multiple small stone fragments obstructing the ureter); failure to disintegrate hard stones (e.g., calcium oxalate monohydrate and cystine stones)

Recurrent UTI — Subclassification & Factors Suggesting Complicated UTI

Recurrent UTI — Three Groups

  • 1 — Unresolved infection — subtherapeutic level of antimicrobial; non-compliance with treatment; malabsorption; resistant pathogens
  • 2 — Bacterial persistence — may be due to a nidus for persistent infection in the urinary tract; surgical correction or medical treatment for urinary dysfunction may be needed
  • 3 — Reinfection — each episode is a new infection acquired from periurethral, perineal or rectal flora

Factors Suggesting a Potential Complicated UTI

  • Indwelling catheter, stent or splint (urethral, ureteral, renal) or intermittent bladder catheterization
  • Post-void residual urine of > 100 mL
  • Obstructive uropathy of any aetiology — e.g., bladder outlet obstruction (including neurogenic bladder), stones and tumour
  • Vesicoureteric reflux or other functional abnormalities
  • Urinary tract modifications — ileal loop or pouch
  • Renal insufficiency and transplantation, diabetes mellitus and immunodeficiency
Unresolved = never cleared; persistence = same site, same organism; reinfection = new episode from the perineal/rectal reservoir.

Urinary Tract Infections · 1/13

Introduction & Significant Bacteriuria

The gold standard for diagnosis is detection of the pathogen in the presence of clinical symptoms

Burden of Disease

  • Among the most prevalent infectious diseases, with a substantial financial burden on society
  • Represent about 40% of all hospital-acquired infections
  • In the USA: over 7 million physician visits annually, including more than 2 million visits for cystitis
  • Approximately 15% of all community-prescribed antibiotics are dispensed for UTI — estimated annual cost over US $1 billion (Naber et al., EAU 2010)

Diagnosis & Bacteriuria Thresholds

  • Gold standard: detection of the pathogen (urine culture, midstream urine) in the presence of clinical symptoms; this also estimates the level of bacteriuria
  • In 1960, Kass developed the concept of significant bacteriuria (> 10⁵ cfu/mL) in pyelonephritis of pregnancy (Kass, 1960)
  • Acute uncomplicated cystitis (women) — > 10³ cfu/mL of uropathogens in MSU
  • Acute uncomplicated pyelonephritis (women) — > 10⁴ cfu/mL in MSU
  • Complicated UTI — > 10⁵ cfu/mL (women) or > 10⁴ cfu/mL (men, or straight catheter urine in women)
  • Suprapubic bladder puncture — any count of bacteria is relevant
MSU = mid-stream sample of urine; cfu = colony-forming units.

STONES & INFECTIONS

Stone Disease — Key Takeaways

Stones are polycrystalline aggregates; recurrence reaches 50–60% within 10 years — the 3rd most common urinary tract disease.
Formation requires supersaturation; inhibitors (citrate, magnesium, pyrophosphate, mucoproteins) protect; pH and ionic strength modulate solubility.
Calcium stones dominate (70–80%); uric acid stones are radiolucent; struvite stones signal infection.
Diagnosis: KUB (radio-opaque), US (radiolucent), non-enhanced/spiral CT is the gold standard; renogram (MAG3/DTPA) assesses function and drainage.
Management ladder: conservative (< 10 mm) → ESWL (≤ 2 cm) → PCNL (> 2 cm, staghorn) → open surgery (about 5% of patients); ureteric stones are treated per site with ureteroscopy ± laser or ESWL.
Metabolic work-up (serum calcium/phosphorus/uric acid + 24-h urine calcium, uric acid, oxalate, citrate) guides recurrence prevention.

Prevention of recurrence is as important as stone removal — metabolic evaluation and advice follow every treatment.

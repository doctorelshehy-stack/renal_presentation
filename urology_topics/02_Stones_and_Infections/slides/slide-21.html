Urinary Tract Infections · 2/13

Evaluation

History — The First Step

  • Effective management begins with a careful history
  • Attention to: current symptomatology; prior episodes of documented UTI; risk factors that can initiate a UTI (e.g., urethral catheterization or sexual intercourse)
  • Risk factors that can complicate a UTI: diabetes mellitus or pregnancy

Specimen Collection

  • A midstream voided specimen is generally adequate
  • Urethral catheterization or suprapubic aspiration may be necessary in an individual who cannot produce a clean specimen

Urinalysis — Pyuria & Bacteriuria

  • UTI is a host inflammatory response to bacterial invasion
  • Urinalysis reveals pyuria — the presence of white blood cells in the urine — and bacteriuria
  • If there is bacteriuria with no pyuria in an asymptomatic patient: an infection is not present, and therapy is usually not necessary

Key Clinical Point

Bacteriuria without pyuria in an asymptomatic patient is not infection — asymptomatic bacteriuria generally does not require therapy.

Urinary Tract Infections — Key Takeaways

UTIs represent about 40% of hospital-acquired infections; E. coli causes 85% of ambulatory and 50% of nosocomial infections, ascending from fecal flora.
Significant bacteriuria: >10³ cfu/mL cystitis, >10⁴ pyelonephritis, >10⁵ (women) / >10⁴ (men) complicated; any count on suprapubic puncture.
Bacteriuria without pyuria in an asymptomatic patient is not infection — therapy is usually unnecessary.
Uncomplicated cystitis: cotrimoxazole 160/800 mg bid × 3 days or trimethoprim 200 mg × 5 days; pyelonephritis: 7 days oral fluoroquinolone, severe cases parenteral.
Pregnancy: treat asymptomatic bacteriuria (4–7% prevalence; 20–40% progress to pyelonephritis); quinolones, tetracyclines and TMP are contraindicated in the 1st trimester.
Children: investigate after 2 episodes (girls) / 1 episode (boys); even a normal US misses VUR in up to 23% — VCU is the key study.
Urosepsis: SIRS → multi-organ failure; mortality 20–40% — prompt antibiotics, supportive care and relief of urinary obstruction.

A careful history, correct specimen and the right threshold for bacteriuria are the foundation of UTI management.

Urinary Tract Infections · 3/13

Pathogenesis

Route of infection, pathogens and virulence factors

Causative Pathogens

  • Escherichia coli is the most common — 85% of infections in ambulatory patients and 50% of nosocomial infections
  • Proteus mirabilis, Klebsiella pneumoniae and Enterococcus faecalis are the next most frequent isolates
  • Most infections: retrograde ascent of bacteria from the fecal flora via the urethra to the bladder and kidney

Catheter-Related Infection

  • A single catheter insertion in ambulatory patients results in a UTI in 1–2% of cases
  • Indwelling catheters with open-drainage systems lead to bacteriuria in nearly 100% of patients within 3–4 days

Hematogenous Infection

  • Restricted to a few relatively uncommon microbes that cause primary infections elsewhere in the body:
  • Staphylococcus aureus
  • Candida sp.
  • Salmonella sp.
  • Mycobacterium tuberculosis

Virulence & Host Factors

  • Urinary pathogen virulence factors promote adherence to mucosal surfaces
  • Bacteria usually express fimbriae or pili which mediate adherence to epithelial cell receptors
  • Host factors such as epithelial cell receptivity are also important
  • E. coli binds more avidly to vaginal epithelial cells from women with recurrent UTIs than from healthy individuals (Fowler et al., 1977)

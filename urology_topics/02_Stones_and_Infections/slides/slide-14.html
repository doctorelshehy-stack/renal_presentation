Percutaneous Nephrolithotomy (PCNL)

The Procedure

  • Insertion of a ureteric catheter through cystoscopy
  • A puncture needle punctures the desired calyx through skin under radiographic or ultrasound control
  • A guide wire is inserted through the puncture needle
  • The tract is dilated with different types of dilators to accommodate the Amplatz sheath
  • The nephroscope is passed; forceps and lithotripter probes are used under vision
  • Small stones removed in toto with stone forceps; larger stones fragmented by lithotripsy (ultrasonic, laser or electrohydraulic)
  • A nephrostomy tube is left for 1–2 days at the end

Indications

  • Large stones > 2 cm
  • Associated pathology that can be manipulated by the percutaneous route, e.g., PUJO
  • ESWL failure
  • Staghorn stones — PCNL can be started with, then ESWL used for small residual stones

Contraindications

  • Uncontrollable bleeding diathesis
  • Congenital anomalies rendering the pelvicalyceal system not amenable to the percutaneous route
  • During pregnancy

Advantages & Disadvantages

  • Advantages — small endoscopic wound; lesser post-operative pain; short hospital stay
  • Disadvantages — iatrogenic trauma to other organs (colon, duodenum, spleen or pleura); intra- or post-operative bleeding; intra- or extra-peritoneal extravasation; residual stones

Human Papillomavirus — Genital Warts

Etiology

  • Double-stranded DNA virus of the Papillomaviridae family
  • More than 100 types exist; more than 40 types can infect the genital area and be sexually transmitted
  • Types 6 and 11: non-oncogenic — responsible for about 90% of anogenital warts
  • Types 16 and 18: cervical cancer and other anogenital cancers (vulvar, vaginal, anal, penile)

Pathology & Diagnosis

  • Pathology — infection starts at the basal cell layer of stratified squamous epithelium, stimulating cell proliferation; Bowenoid papulosis — reddish-brown verrucous papules on the penis, a low-grade carcinoma in situ; giant condyloma acuminatum — large verrucous exophytic lesions, associated with HPV-6 or HPV-11
  • Diagnosis — warts seen clinically; latent viruses detectable only through demonstration of HPV DNA in skin or mucosa; biopsy from lesions to rule out malignancy

Treatment — Removal, Not Cure

  • Goal: removal of the warts — treatment will not eradicate the infection
  • Patient-applied — podofilox 0.5% solution or gel; imiquimod cream 5%; sinecatechins 15% ointment
  • Provider-administered — podophyllin resin 10–25% in tincture of benzoin; cryotherapy (liquid nitrogen); trichloroacetic acid; surgical therapy — direct excision, shaving, curettage, or laser ablation
Types 6/11 cause warts; types 16/18 drive anogenital cancers.

Chlamydia, Mycoplasma genitalium & Trichomonas

Chlamydia trachomatis

  • Accounts for 15–40% of non-gonococcal urethritis; more common in younger men
  • Incubation period 3–14 days
  • NAATs using first-catch urine are the most sensitive test in men
  • Positive patients screened also for gonorrhoea, syphilis and HIV
  • Treatment — azithromycin 1 g orally single dose, or doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, or erythromycin 500 mg orally four times daily for 7 days, or levofloxacin 500 mg orally once daily for 7 days, or ofloxacin 300 mg orally twice daily for 7 days

Trichomonas vaginalis

  • Flagellated parasite infecting male and female urethra, and female vulva and vagina
  • Rates vary by geographical area: 2–13%
  • Diagnosis: NAAT; should be screened for in areas with high prevalence
  • Treatment — metronidazole 2 g orally once, or tinidazole 2 g orally once

Mycoplasma genitalium

  • 15–20% of non-gonococcal urethritis cases
  • 30% of cases of recurrent or persistent urethritis
  • NAAT is the only available diagnostic test
  • Should be suspected in recurrent or persistent urethritis
  • Treatment — azithromycin 1 g orally single dose; resistant cases: moxifloxacin 400 mg daily for 7, 10 or 14 days
Recurrent or persistent urethritis after treatment should prompt evaluation for Mycoplasma genitalium.

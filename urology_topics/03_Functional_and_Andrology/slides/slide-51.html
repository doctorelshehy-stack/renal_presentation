Sexually Transmitted Diseases — Key Takeaways

Urethritis: gonococcal (N. gonorrhoea 5–20%) vs non-gonococcal (chlamydia 15–40%, Mycoplasma 15–25%, trichomonas, ureaplasma, HSV, adenovirus); gram stain with intracellular diplococci is diagnostic; NAAT is preferred.
Gonorrhoea (CDC): ceftriaxone 250 mg IM + azithromycin 1 g orally single dose (or cefixime 400 mg + azithromycin); abstain 7 days; treat partners from the preceding 60 days; test of cure at 3–4 months.
Chlamydia: azithromycin 1 g single dose or doxycycline 100 mg bid × 7 days; Mycoplasma genitalium: azithromycin 1 g, moxifloxacin 400 mg daily for resistant cases; Trichomonas: metronidazole or tinidazole 2 g single dose.
Genital ulcers: HSV (acyclovir 400 mg tds or 200 mg 5×/day, valacyclovir 1 g bid, famciclovir 250 mg tds × 7–10 days), syphilis (chancre → rash → latent → tertiary; VDRL/RPR + FTA-ABS/TP-PA; IV penicillin G for all stages), chancroid (H. ducreyi; azithromycin 1 g, ceftriaxone 250 mg IM, ciprofloxacin 500 mg bid × 3 days, erythromycin 500 mg tds × 7 days), donovanosis.
HPV warts: types 6/11 cause ~90% of anogenital warts; 16/18 cause anogenital cancers; treatment removes warts but does not eradicate the virus (podofilox, imiquimod, sinecatechins; cryotherapy, TCA, excision, laser).
HIV: retrovirus infecting T cells and dendritic cells; stages — acute (2–4 weeks, flu-like, antibodies often negative), clinical latency, AIDS (CD4 < 200/mm³); diagnosis — ELISA then western blot confirmation; treatment — HAART with NRTIs, NNRTIs, PIs and INSTIs.

Screen, treat and trace partners — STI control depends on completing the chain of transmission.

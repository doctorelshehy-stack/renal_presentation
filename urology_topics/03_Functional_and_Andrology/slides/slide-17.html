Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia · 2/10

Incidence & Epidemiology

Incidence

  • BPH is the most common benign tumour in men
  • Pathological incidence about 30% in men above forty
  • Clinical incidence about 40% in men above fifty, increasing 10% each decade

Epidemiology

  • Ethnic background — prostate enlargement is less common in Asian men than in white and black men
  • Diabetes and heart disease — diabetes, heart disease and use of beta blockers might increase the risk of BPH
  • Lifestyle — obesity increases the risk; exercise lowers the risk

Heredity

  • Autosomal dominant inheritance in approximately 50% of men undergoing prostatectomy for BPH when younger than 60 years
  • Only about 9% of men undergoing prostatectomy when older than 60 years would be predicted to have familial risk
  • Familial BPH characterized by large prostate size: mean volume 82.7 mL (hereditary) vs 55.5 mL (sporadic)

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)

Virology & Transmission

  • HIV is a retrovirus that infects T cells and dendritic cells
  • Spread through blood, semen, vaginal fluid, or breast milk
  • Immunosuppression occurs with the progressive loss of CD4 T-lymphocytes

Diagnosis

  • Serologic tests detect antibodies against HIV-1 and HIV-2; virologic tests detect HIV antigens or RNA
  • ELISA testing of serum detects antibodies against HIV antigens
  • The second confirmatory test is western blot

Three Recognized Stages

  • Stage 1 — Acute — occurs 2–4 weeks after infection; flu-like symptoms; patients at increased risk of transmitting the virus; routine HIV antibody tests are often negative during this phase
  • Stage 2 — Clinical latency — slow viral replication lasting years to decades depending on treatment; patients often asymptomatic
  • Stage 3 — AIDS — diagnosed when the CD4 count is less than 200 cells/mm³

Treatment — ART / HAART

  • Antiretroviral therapy (ART) suppresses HIV replication
  • Standard: combination of drugs (highly active antiretroviral therapy — HAART) to increase potency and reduce the likelihood of resistance
  • ART reduces mortality and morbidity and improves quality of life
  • Drug classes: nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs), non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs), protease inhibitors (PIs), integrase inhibitors (INSTIs)

Male Infertility · 8/8

Surgical Treatment & Assisted Reproductive Technologies

Surgical Treatment

  • Varicocelectomy for varicoceles
  • Vasovasostomy or epididymovasostomy for vasal or epididymal obstruction
  • Ejaculatory duct obstruction: TURED (transurethral resection of the ejaculatory duct)
  • Electroejaculation in spinal cord injury; pelvic or retroperitoneal surgery injuring the pelvic sympathetic nerves
  • Sperm aspiration: vas deferens, epididymis, or testicle for assisted reproductive techniques
  • Orchidopexy: early, at 12–18 months of age, to prevent sperm cell damage
  • Testicular torsion: the unaffected contralateral testis can become infertile after torsion of its mate (sympathetic orchidopathia, immunologic in nature) — so a gangrenous testis should be removed
  • Pituitary ablation of tumours; prolactinoma treated medically and surgically

Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

  • Eliminate many natural selection barriers; genetic defects that caused the infertility are expected to be passed on to offspring unabated
  • IUI — good for cervical factors, low sperm quality, immunologic infertility, poor sperm delivery; at least 5–40 million motile sperm in the ejaculate
  • IVF — controlled ovarian stimulation and ultrasound-guided transvaginal egg retrieval before normal ovulation; 500,000 to 5 million sperms required
  • ICSI — one viable sperm can be used
ICSI requires only a single viable sperm — transforming the prognosis of severe male factor infertility.

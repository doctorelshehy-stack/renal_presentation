Erectile Dysfunction & Premature Ejaculation — Key Takeaways

ED: persistent inability to attain and maintain an erection sufficient for satisfactory performance; prevalence 10–20% rising to 50–100% at 70–90 years; risk factors — age, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, smoking, overweight; ED predicts future cardiovascular events.
ED etiology: psychogenic, vasculogenic (atherosclerosis, diabetes, pelvic radiotherapy), neurogenic (diabetes, stroke, spinal trauma, pelvic surgery), hormonal (hypogonadism, panhypopituitarism), drug-induced (thiazides, beta-blockers, antidepressants, antiandrogens, recreational drugs), anatomical (Peyronie's disease, penile fracture).
ED diagnosis: history + IIEF; physical — genital, prostate, hypogonadism, cardiovascular/neurological; labs — fasting glucose, lipids, morning testosterone, LH/prolactin if low; NPTR differentiates organic from psychogenic; penile duplex and arteriography in selected cases.
ED management: treat curable causes; lifestyle modification; PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, avanafil, vardenafil) — contraindicated with organic nitrates; then topical alprostadil, intracavernosal injection (effective 70%; priapism 1%), vacuum devices, and penile prosthesis.
PE (ISSM 2014): ejaculation within ~1 minute of penetration (lifelong) or ~3 minutes (acquired), inability to delay ejaculation, negative personal consequences; lifelong PE — 5-HT2c hyposensitivity/5-HT1a hypersensitivity; classification — lifelong, acquired, variable, subjective.
PE management: treat the cause (ED, prostatitis, hyperthyroidism); psychological/behavioural (start-stop technique); dapoxetine 30–60 mg on demand — the only FDA-approved drug; daily paroxetine or citalopram; local anaesthetics (lidocaine/prilocaine).

ED is a vascular warning sign; PE responds to behavioural therapy, SSRIs and local anaesthetics.

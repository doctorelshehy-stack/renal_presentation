Erectile Dysfunction — Diagnosis

History Taking

  • Detailed medical and sexual history
  • Onset and course of ED
  • Validated questionnaires — international index of erectile function (IIEF)
  • Other sexual problems: decreased libido, premature ejaculation
  • Co-morbidities: diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular problems

Laboratory Evaluation

  • Fasting blood sugar and lipid profile
  • Morning total testosterone level — if clinical suspicion of hypogonadism
  • If total testosterone is low: free testosterone, luteinizing hormone (LH) and prolactin should be measured

Focused Physical Examination

  • Genital examination: penile deformities, size of the testis
  • Prostate examination: size of prostate and prostate cancer
  • Signs of hypogonadism
  • Cardiovascular and neurological examination

Advanced Workup (not routine)

  • Nocturnal penile tumescence and rigidity test (NPTR) — measures number and rigidity of erection episodes at night; differentiates organic from psychogenic ED
  • Duplex ultrasound of the penis — measures blood flow velocity; normal values exclude vasculogenic ED
  • Penile arteriography — indicated after trauma to the penis or perineum, or suspected pudendal artery stenosis, in patients prepared for penile revascularization surgery

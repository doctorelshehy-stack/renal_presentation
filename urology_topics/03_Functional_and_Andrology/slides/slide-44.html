Genital Ulcers — Herpes Simplex Virus

Causes of Genital Ulcers

  • Herpes simplex virus
  • Syphilis
  • Chancroid
  • Donovanosis

Pathology

  • HSV type 1 and type 2 are double-stranded DNA viruses
  • Infects epithelial cells and nerve endings, then travels through nerve axons to reach sacral ganglia and reside there in a latent stage
  • Reactivation triggers: trauma, ultraviolet light, immunosuppression, fever

Clinical Picture & Complications

  • Painful papules progressing to vesicles, then ulcer with local lymphadenitis
  • Possible complications: aseptic meningitis and autonomic dysfunction resulting in urinary retention

Diagnosis

  • Cell cultures and PCR of swabs taken from the base of the genital ulcer are the recommended diagnostic tool

Treatment

  • Acyclovir 400 mg orally three times a day for 7–10 days, OR
  • Acyclovir 200 mg orally five times a day for 7–10 days, OR
  • Valacyclovir 1 g orally twice a day for 7–10 days, OR
  • Famciclovir 250 mg orally three times a day for 7–10 days

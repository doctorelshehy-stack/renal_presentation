Urethritis & Gonococcal Infection

Aetiology of Urethritis

  • Gonococcal — Neisseria gonorrhoea (5–20%)
  • Non-gonococcal — Chlamydia trachomatis (15–40%); Mycoplasma genitalium (15–25%); Trichomonas vaginalis; Ureaplasma urealyticum; herpes simplex virus; adenovirus

N. gonorrhoea — Clinical Presentation

  • Incubation period 3–14 days
  • Males: purulent urethral discharge, dysuria, urethral itching
  • May progress to epididymitis, orchitis or prostatitis
  • Proctitis with anal intercourse
  • Females: often asymptomatic; sequelae — pelvic inflammatory disease and tubal scarring
  • About 450,000 cases of gonorrhoea were diagnosed in 2016

Pathology & Diagnosis

  • Gram-negative diplococcus that lives intracellularly
  • Gram stain of male urethral discharge/swab: polymorphonuclear leukocytes with gram-negative intracellular diplococci — diagnostic
  • Culture of urethral specimen if swab negative
  • NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test) on urethral swab or first-catch urine — quicker and more sensitive; the preferred diagnostic tool nowadays
  • Positive patients should be screened also for other STIs: chlamydia, syphilis and HIV

Treatment — CDC Recommendations

  • Single dose of ceftriaxone 250 mg IM + azithromycin 1 g orally
  • Alternative: single dose of cefixime 400 mg orally + azithromycin 1 g orally
  • Cephalosporin allergy: single dose of azithromycin 1 g orally + gemifloxacin 320 mg orally or gentamicin 240 mg IM
  • Abstain from sexual activity for 7 days after treatment
  • All sexual partners within 60 days before the date of infection should be referred for evaluation
  • Follow-up test to confirm cure at 3–4 months after treatment

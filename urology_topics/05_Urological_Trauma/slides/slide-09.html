Renal Injuries — Differential Diagnosis & Complications

Differential Diagnosis

  • Trauma to the abdomen and flank areas is not always associated with renal injury
  • In such cases: no hematuria, and the results of imaging studies are normal

Early Complications

  • Haemorrhage is the most important immediate complication of renal injury
  • Heavy retroperitoneal bleeding may result in rapid collapse
  • Patients must be observed closely, with careful monitoring of blood pressure and hematocrit

Late Complications

  • Hypertension
  • Hydronephrosis
  • Arteriovenous fistula
  • Calculus formation
  • Pyelonephritis
  • Renal atrophy may occur from vascular compromise — detected by follow-up urography

Follow-Up

  • Careful monitoring of blood pressure for several months to watch for hypertension
  • At 3–6 months: follow-up excretory urogram or CT to be certain that perinephric scarring has not caused hydronephrosis or vascular compromise

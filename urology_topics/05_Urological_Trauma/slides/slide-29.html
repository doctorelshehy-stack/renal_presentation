Urological Trauma · 22/27

Injuries to the Anterior Urethra — Clinical Findings

Symptoms

  • There is usually a history of a fall, and in some cases a history of instrumentation
  • Bleeding from the urethra is usually present
  • There is local pain in the perineum and sometimes a massive perineal hematoma
  • If voiding has occurred and extravasation is noted, sudden swelling in the area will be present
  • If diagnosis has been delayed, sepsis and severe infection may be present

Signs

  • The perineum is very tender, and a mass may be found
  • Rectal examination reveals a normal prostate
  • The patient usually has a desire to void, but voiding should not be allowed until assessment of the urethra is complete
  • No attempt should be made to pass a urethral catheter; if the patient's bladder is overdistended, percutaneous suprapubic cystostomy can be done as a temporary procedure

Laboratory Findings

  • Blood loss is not usually excessive, particularly if secondary injury has occurred
  • The white count may be elevated with infection
Do not allow voiding; do not catheterize — a normal prostate on DRE distinguishes anterior from posterior injury.

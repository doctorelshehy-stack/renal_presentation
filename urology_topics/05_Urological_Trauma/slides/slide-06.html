Renal Injuries — Pathology & Classification

Early Pathologic Findings

  • Lacerations from blunt trauma usually occur in the transverse plane of the kidney — force is transmitted from the center of the impact to the renal parenchyma
  • In rapid deceleration injuries, the kidney moves upward or downward, causing sudden stretch on the renal pedicle and sometimes complete or partial avulsion
  • Acute thrombosis of the renal artery may be caused by an intimal tear from rapid deceleration injuries owing to the sudden stretch

Pathologic Classification (Grades 1–5)

  • Grade 1 (most common) — renal contusion or bruising of the parenchyma; microscopic hematuria is common, gross hematuria can occur rarely
  • Grade 2 — parenchymal laceration into the renal cortex; perirenal hematoma is usually small
  • Grade 3 — laceration extending through the cortex into the medulla; bleeding can be significant with a large retroperitoneal hematoma
  • Grade 4 — laceration extending into the collecting system; main renal artery thrombosis from blunt trauma, segmental renal vein, or both; or artery injury with contained bleeding
  • Grade 5 — multiple Grade 4 parenchymal lacerations, renal pedicle avulsion, or both; main renal vein or artery injury from penetrating trauma

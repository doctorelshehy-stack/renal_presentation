Urological Trauma · 9/28

Injuries to the Ureter — Clinical Findings

Clinical Findings

  • Unilateral complete or partial ligation — fever, flank pain and lower quadrant pain, paralytic ileus; ureterovaginal or cutaneous fistula within the first 10 postoperative days
  • Bilateral complete ligation — anuria
  • Sustained stab or gunshot wounds to the retroperitoneum — ureteral injuries should be suspected; the mid portion of the ureter is the most common site of penetrating injury; usually associated vascular and other abdominal visceral injuries

Signs

  • Signs of acute peritonitis
  • Urine leakage: watery discharge from the wound or vagina may be identified as urine by determining the creatinine concentration
  • Intravenous injection of 10 mL of indigo carmine will appear in the urine as dark blue

Laboratory Findings

  • Ureteral injury from external violence is manifested by microscopic hematuria in 90% of cases
  • Urine analysis and other laboratory studies are of little use in diagnosis when injury has occurred from other causes

Urological Trauma · 13/27

Injuries to the Urinary Bladder — Ascending Cystogram

Ascending Cystogram Technique

  • 1 — The bladder should be filled with 300 mL of contrast material
  • 2 — Contrast medium should be allowed to drain out completely, and a second film of the abdomen should be obtained
  • 3 — It demonstrates areas of extraperitoneal extravasation of blood and urine that may not appear on the filling film
  • 4 — With intraperitoneal extravasation, free contrast medium is visualized in the abdomen

Complications

  • A pelvic abscess may develop from extraperitoneal bladder rupture; if the urine becomes infected, the pelvic hematoma becomes infected too
  • Intraperitoneal bladder rupture with extravasation of urine into the abdominal cavity causes delayed peritonitis
  • Partial incontinence may result from bladder injury when the laceration extends into the bladder neck
The post-drainage film is essential — extraperitoneal extravasation may only appear after the bladder empties.

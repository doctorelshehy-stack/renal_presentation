Injuries to the Urinary Bladder — Introduction & Pathogenesis

Introduction

  • About 15% of all pelvic fractures are associated with concomitant bladder or urethral injuries
  • Pelvic fracture accompanies bladder rupture in 90% of cases
  • Iatrogenic injury may result from gynaecologic and other extensive pelvic procedures, hernia repairs and transurethral operations

Pathogenesis & Pathology

  • Fragments from the fracture site may perforate the bladder, resulting in extraperitoneal rupture
  • When the bladder is filled to near full capacity, a direct blow to the lower abdomen may result in intraperitoneal rupture
  • If the diagnosis is not established immediately and the urine is sterile, no symptoms may be noted for several days
  • If the urine is infected: immediate peritonitis and acute abdomen; deep pelvic abscess and severe pelvic inflammation develop

Two Rupture Patterns

  • Extraperitoneal rupture — caused by bone fragments perforating the bladder — the commoner pattern
  • Intraperitoneal rupture — from a direct blow on a full bladder — urine enters the peritoneal cavity
  • Sterile urine → delayed symptoms; infected urine → immediate peritonitis
Fracture fragments → extraperitoneal; full-bladder blow → intraperitoneal.

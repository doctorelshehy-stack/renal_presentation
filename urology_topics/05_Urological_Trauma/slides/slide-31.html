Injuries to the Anterior Urethra — Treatment & Prognosis

General Measures

  • Major blood loss usually does not occur from straddle injury
  • If heavy bleeding does occur: local pressure for control, followed by resuscitation

Urethral Lacerations

  • Suprapubic cystostomy may be used
  • If only minor extravasation is noted on the urethrogram: a voiding study can be performed within 7 days after suprapubic catheter drainage
  • In more extensive injuries: wait 2–3 weeks before doing a voiding study through the suprapubic catheter
  • Healing at the site may result in stricture formation — most are not severe and do not require surgical reconstruction
  • The suprapubic cystostomy catheter may be removed if no extravasation is documented

Extensive Extravasation & Immediate Repair

  • Laceration with extensive urinary extravasation — after major laceration, extravasation may involve the perineum, scrotum and lower abdomen — drainage of these areas is indicated; suprapubic cystostomy for urinary diversion is required; infection and abscess formation are common and require antibiotic therapy
  • Immediate repair — can be performed, but the procedure is difficult and the incidence of associated stricture is high

Prognosis

  • Urethral stricture is a major complication but in most cases does not require surgical reconstruction
  • If, when the stricture resolves, urinary flow rates are poor and urinary infection and urethral fistula are present, reconstruction is required

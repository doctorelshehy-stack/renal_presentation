Renal Injuries — Clinical Findings & Imaging Indications

Clinical Findings

  • Microscopic or gross hematuria following trauma to the abdomen indicates injury to the urinary tract
  • Stab or gunshot wounds to the flank area should alert the physician to possible renal injury whether or not hematuria is present
  • Some cases of renal vascular injury are not associated with hematuria — almost always due to rapid deceleration accidents, and an indication for imaging
  • The degree of renal injury does not correspond to the degree of hematuria — gross hematuria may occur in minor trauma and only mild hematuria in major trauma

Symptoms & Signs

  • Symptoms — usually visible evidence of abdominal trauma; pain localized to one flank or over the abdomen; associated injuries (ruptured viscera, pelvic fractures) may obscure the renal injury; catheterization usually reveals haematuria; retroperitoneal bleeding may cause abdominal distention, ileus, nausea and vomiting
  • Signs — shock or signs of large blood loss from heavy retroperitoneal bleeding; ecchymosis in the flank or upper quadrants; lower rib fractures; diffuse abdominal tenderness — an 'acute abdomen' usually indicates free blood in the peritoneal cavity; a palpable mass may represent a large retroperitoneal hematoma or urinary extravasation

Laboratory Findings

  • Microscopic or gross haematuria is usually present
  • The haematocrit may be normal initially, but a drop may be found on serial studies — representing persistent retroperitoneal bleeding and development of a large retroperitoneal hematoma
Hematuria ≠ severity: minor trauma can cause gross hematuria and major vascular injury may cause none.

Injuries to the Ureter — Introduction & Pathogenesis

Introduction — When Does It Occur?

  • Ureteral injury is rare
  • 1 — Difficult pelvic surgical procedure: the ureter may be inadvertently ligated and cut
  • 2 — Endoscopic basket manipulation of ureteral calculi
  • 3 — Gunshot wounds
  • 4 — Rapid deceleration accidents may avulse the ureter from the renal pelvis

Prognosis Depends on Timing

  • Excellent if the diagnosis is made early and prompt corrective surgery is done
  • Delay in diagnosis worsens the prognosis because of infection, hydronephrosis, abscess and fistula formation

Pathogenesis — If the Ureter Is Partially Divided

  • Early — 1 — urinary extravasation; 2 — ileus and peritonitis; 3 — large urinoma
  • Late — 1 — ureterovaginal or ureterocutaneous fistula formation; 2 — reactive fibrosis and stricture; 3 — mild to moderate hydronephrosis
Partial division → extravasation early, fistula/stricture/hydronephrosis late.

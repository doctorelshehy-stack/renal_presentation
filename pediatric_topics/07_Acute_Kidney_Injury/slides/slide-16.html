Section 06
Intrinsic & Post-renal — History, Examination, Laboratory
Intrinsic renal
  • History: a 6-yr-old with recent pharyngitis, peri-orbital edema, hypertension, and gross hematuria → acute post-infectious GN. A critically ill child with protracted hypotension and nephrotoxic medications → ATN.
  • Examination: peripheral edema, rales, cardiac gallop → volume overload (GN or ATN). Rash and arthritis → SLE or HSP nephritis.
  • Laboratory: specific gravity < 1010; Uosm < 350 mOsm/kg; UNa > 40 mEq/L; FENa > 2% (> 10% in neonates).
Post-renal
  • History: a neonate with hydronephrosis on prenatal ultrasound and a palpable bladder → congenital urinary tract obstruction, perhaps posterior urethral valves.
  • Examination: palpable flank masses → renal vein thrombosis, tumors, cystic disease, or urinary tract obstruction.
  • For the cause: bladder catheterization; abdominal ultrasound to exclude obstructive uropathy.
Urinary findings that localize intrinsic AKI
1. Hematuria, proteinuria, RBC or granular casts → glomerular disease. 2. WBCs and WBC casts with low-grade hematuria and proteinuria → tubulointerstitial disease. 3. Urinary eosinophils → drug-induced tubulointerstitial nephritis.

Wrap-Up
Key Takeaways
  • UTI occurs in 3–5% of girls and 1% of boys; M:F = 2.8–5.4:1 in infancy, 1:10 beyond 1–2 yr.
  • Main organism: E. coli, then Klebsiella/Proteus, S. saprophyticus, adenovirus.
  • Three forms: pyelonephritis (fever, flank pain — causes scarring), cystitis (dysuria/urgency — no renal injury), asymptomatic bacteriuria (benign, girls).
  • Pathogenesis: ascending infection from fecal flora via urethra; risk factors include female sex, VUR, uncircumcised male, voiding dysfunction.
  • Diagnosis: urine culture confirms; WBC > 20,000–25,000/mm³ with abscess; blood cultures in infants/obstructive uropathy.
  • Imaging: renal ultrasound, VCUG (<5 yr, febrile UTI, ≥2 UTIs in school-aged girls, any male), DMSA for scarring.
  • Treatment: cystitis — TMP-SMX 3–5 days or nitrofurantoin; pyelonephritis — 14 days ceftriaxone or ampicillin + gentamicin (monitor levels), cefixime orally; drainage for abscess/obstruction.
  • Prophylaxis: TMP-SMX or nitrofurantoin at 1/3 therapeutic dose daily; amoxicillin or cephalexin alternative.

Section 06
Nutrition & Growth
Nutrition
  • Dietary phosphorus, potassium, and sodium restricted according to the individual patient's laboratory studies and fluid balance — to provide the recommended dietary allowance of caloric intake for age.
  • Protein intake: 2.5 g/kg/24 hr, of high biologic value — eggs and milk, followed by meat, fish.
  • Water-soluble vitamins, zinc and iron supplements only if deficiencies are confirmed. Supplementation with fat-soluble vitamins A, E, and K is usually not required.
Growth — short stature
  • Short stature is one of the most significant long-term sequelae of childhood CKD.
  • Key etiologic factor: a growth hormone (GH)-resistant state — elevated GH levels but decreased IGF-I levels and major abnormalities of IGF-binding proteins.
  • Children who remain less than −2 SD for height despite optimal medical support (adequate caloric intake, effective treatment of renal osteodystrophy, anemia, and metabolic acidosis) may benefit from recombinant human growth hormone (rHuGH).
  • rHuGH 0.05 mg/kg/24 hr subcutaneously, with periodic dose adjustment to achieve normal height velocity for age.
rHuGH may be continued until the patient:
1. Reaches the 50th percentile for midparental height, 2. Achieves a final adult height, or 3. Undergoes renal transplantation.

Section 06
Renal Osteodystrophy & Anemia
Renal osteodystrophy
  • A spectrum of bone disorders in CKD. Most common in children: high-turnover bone disease from secondary hyperparathyroidism — skeletal pathology: osteitis fibrosa cystica.
  • Clinical: muscle weakness, bone pain, fractures with minor trauma; in growing children — rachitic changes, varus and valgus deformities of long bones, slipped capital femoral epiphyses.
  • Laboratory: ↓ serum calcium, ↑ serum phosphorus, ↑ alkaline phosphatase, normal PTH; radiographs of hands, wrists, knees → subperiosteal resorption with widening of the metaphysis.
  • Treatment: prevent bony deformity and normalize growth velocity — low phosphorus diet and phosphate binders.
Anemia & vitamin D therapy
  • Anemia: primarily inadequate erythropoietin production; usually manifest at GFR < 35 mL/min/1.73 m². Recombinant human erythropoietin (rHuEPO) has dramatically decreased the need for transfusion.
  • Vitamin D therapy is indicated with: 1. Renal osteodystrophy with PTH levels > 3× the upper limit of normal, or 2. Persistent hypocalcemia despite reduction of serum phosphorus below 6 mg/dL.

Wrap-Up
Key Takeaways
  • Hematuria = blood in urine; microscopic = > 5 RBCs/HPF in sediment from 10 ml of centrifuged freshly voided urine.
  • Causes: glomerular (isolated renal disease or multi-system disease) vs extra-glomerular (upper and lower urinary tract).
  • Common gross hematuria: extra-glomerular (UTI, trauma, urolithiasis, coagulopathy, tumor) vs glomerular (IgA nephropathy, Alport, PSGN, HSP, SLE).
  • Differentiate by: color (cola/smoky vs pink/red), RBCs (dysmorphic vs normal), casts, clots, proteinuria, and clinical picture.
  • Workup: localize first — C3, ASO/DNase, ANA/dsDNA for glomerular; culture, 24-hr calcium/urate/oxalate, cystogram, Doppler for extra-glomerular.
  • Renal biopsy: unexplained persistent/recurrent gross hematuria, lupus nephritis, GN with nephritic nephrosis or absent low C3, unexplained ARF.

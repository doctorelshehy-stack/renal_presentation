Section 02
Transient & Orthostatic Proteinuria
Transient Proteinuria — causes
  • Fever
  • Exercise
  • Dehydration
  • Cold exposure
  • Seizure
Orthostatic (Postural) Proteinuria
  • The most common cause of persistent proteinuria in school-aged children and adolescents — up to 60% of children with persistent proteinuria.
  • Usually asymptomatic; discovered on routine urinalysis with unknown cause.
  • Normal or slightly increased protein in the supine position; in the upright position excretion increases up to 10-fold, up to 1,000 mg/24 hr (1 g/24 hr).
  • Hematuria, hypertension, and renal dysfunction are absent.
  • Long-term follow-up is prudent to monitor for hematuria, hypertension, diminished renal function, or proteinuria exceeding 1,000 mg/24 hr.

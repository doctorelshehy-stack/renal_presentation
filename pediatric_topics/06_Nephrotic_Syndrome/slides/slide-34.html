Wrap-Up
Key Takeaways
  • NS = heavy proteinuria (>40 mg/m²/hr), hypoalbuminemia (<2.5 g/dL), generalized edema, hyperlipidemia; 90% idiopathic (MCNS, DMP, FSGS).
  • Pathogenesis: ↑GBM permeability → proteinuria → hypoproteinemia → ↓ oncotic pressure → edema; hyperlipidemia from hepatic overproduction and ↓ lipase.
  • Edema amplified by aldosterone (JGA) and ADH activation from ↓ intravascular volume.
  • MCNS (85%): LM normal, IF negative, EM foot-process effacement; >95% steroid-responsive.
  • Diagnosis: frothy urine, 3+/4+ protein, Upr/UCr > 2, albumin < 2.5, total protein < 4.5, normal C3/C4; biopsy only for specific indications.
  • Treatment: prednisone 60 mg/m²/day × 4 wk, then alternate-day 40 mg/m² taper; relapse protocol; alternatives (cyclophosphamide, methylprednisolone, cyclosporine, ACEi/ARB).
  • Major complications: infection (SBP — S. pneumoniae), thromboembolism (2–5%), hypovolemic shock, renal failure.
  • Prognosis good in steroid-responsive disease; Finnish-type congenital NS (nephrin defect) ends in CRF.
